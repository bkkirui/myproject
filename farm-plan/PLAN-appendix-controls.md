# Appendix B: Authority, controls and fraud response (design defaults)

**Not for approval now.** Calibrated at G1/G2 with the lawyer and accountant (PLAN.md sections 3–4). AOB = annual operating budget, taken as trailing-12-month spend until G1 produces a budget. All numbers are defaults.

## B0. Day-7 financial pack and interim regime (G0 exit; accountant and lawyer own it)
**Two lawyer clearances; steps are tagged.** **Owner-only (proceeds regardless, changes no employee's terms):** statement redirects, new-payee freeze and the B0.2 onboarding route, owner release above the float, credential inventory, a desk match of vendor files (B0.3, vendor side only), and the opening count, which if the go is late is done by an auditor with a witness. **(a) Short Day-7 go, due before day 7:** the same-day count with access changes, the float (B0.4), the goods-received and disposal rules, and scheduled-class payroll approval. **(b) Full incumbent handling at G0 exit (~5 Nov):** retention agreement, notice, role change, candidate notice, and the open contract-manager search (quiet lawyer-led sourcing runs from week 1 under the day-7 brief). Staff-data matching in B0.3 (payroll muster, employee master) and the POA of B0.6 are under (a). The lawyer's day-7 brief also covers the acting lead of B0.7 (de facto director, OHS officer, licensed holder for the reporting duty), the lawful basis, staff notice and processor terms for the B0.3 data match, the role-change wording (pay preserved), and a limited POA or bank-level delegate mandate (B0.6).
**Sequence:** the opening count and access changes happen the same day, count first or together; notices follow. A refusal to sign the count is recorded by the counter and a witness.
1. **Opening count** of livestock (against tags and registers, with a roll-forward to the register on the count date), feed, grain, fuel, drugs. Counter: an auditor or an individual under separate terms from the bookkeeper, plus a vet or independent stockman; not the incumbent. Signed by both; disputed items are listed, not blocked. Late-pregnant stock is counted in situ (A6).
2. **Bank:** owner releases above the float; **freeze on new payees** (an added employee counts as a new payee and needs the owner's approval with ID). **Onboarding route:** vets, agronomist, contractors, window-memo suppliers and the auditor are onboarded by the owner using the B3 checks run by the accountant, target 5 working days. An interim representative holds **view and call-back rights only** until B2 vetting, fidelity cover, a signed conflict declaration and a confidentiality undertaking are in place; the owner, or the accountant co-releasing with call-back, is sole releaser. A **day-7 emergency list** (vet-of-record, second practice, water carter, fuel, electrician) is onboarded by this route in week 1; an unlisted emergency payee gets the accountant's registry check inside the 72-h review and the owner's approval with call-back. The accountant's PI/fidelity confirmation is a precondition of co-release; the co-releaser and the spot-counter are different people. Co-release excludes payments to payees the accountant onboarded and schedules the accountant prepared, unless the owner signed the schedule or payee in writing; when the accountant co-releases, the emergency approver is the agronomist or coordinator, never the accountant. The owner records these role sign-offs at G0 exit.
3. **Books-review baseline tests:** vendor bank account, address and phone against the employee master file; payroll muster against headcount; existing-payee review (registry lookups by the accountant); the existing bookkeeper and accountant are included, with the credential inventory. A phone or address match is a lead; a role change needs **independent confirmation**. A baseline finding above red or a confirmed match is a **B8 trigger led by the lawyer**. "Explained" means documented by movement, sale or bank records and judged by the accountant and vet, not by the person under test.
4. **Interim authority (defaults):** float for the incumbent or interim lead <=0.1% AOB per item and 0.5% per 30 days, set after the accountant's quick look at the last 12 months of purchase lines (a few named recurring items, such as fuel and casual wages, may carry higher floats); **scheduled payroll and supply call-offs are paid under the B1 scheduled class from day 7** (accountant-prepared schedule with headcount and prior-period difference). The A0.3 emergency letter carries the B5 caps and a **non-payee approver outside the incumbent's line** (coordinator or accountant, with logged call). **Goods-received sign-off by someone other than the orderer**, with the B3 small-staff controls (driver-signed delivery note, delivery-note photos to the coordinator, bag count or weighbridge photo, a goods-received sample in each count). **"Disposal" means** sale, transfer, slaughter-for-sale or scrap of stock, produce in bulk, feed, machinery or fuel: **none without the owner's written OK**, except: an owner-signed window-memo row or G1 drought-plan row (within its ceilings, including the stalled-feed rule) **is** the written OK; a standing owner-signed OK for named routine buyers within a price list, volume band and payment into the farm account; an item-by-item OK route with a 24-h owner response target for one-off buyers; and logged exceptions for staff rations and scrap of worn machinery, with a monthly money cap the owner sets at G0 (default: the monthly float). The B4 death-evidence rule (tagged photo, retained tag, witness, logged disposal) applies from day 7 for all species; after G2, routine sales run under an owner-signed standing OK renewed at G2 (T3 "disposals" excludes them). Dispatch to buyers not on the standing OK needs the item-by-item route. Fallen stock, welfare euthanasia, notifiable-disease measures, and daily egg or milk dispatch to those buyers are excluded (logged, reviewed in 72 h). Buyers are told in writing to pay only into the farm account; the accountant matches OK'd sales to bank receipts monthly. Unavoidable cash uses serialised receipts, two-person count, banking within 48 h.
5. **Interim counts.** Scope of each count: livestock against tags, feed, grain, fuel, drugs, payroll muster, sales against bank, goods-received sample. Cadence: ~6–8 weeks after the opening count; at G1; then **unannounced at least every 3 months (maximum 4) until the B6 regime starts; one count may serve both the 6–8-week and G1 slots if they fall within 2 weeks**; a signed handover count at manager start. **The B6 minimum (4 a year, with a maximum interval of 4 months) takes effect at G2 exit, defined as the sign-off one week before manager start**; a signed count is also required at any change of acting lead, manager or float holder; fortnightly counts apply from the pilot. A **monthly roll-forward** is run by the coordinator on **independent inputs**: for livestock the movement register, transport or traceability records and abattoir, saleyard and buyer confirmations against bank receipts; for feed, supplier statements, delivery-note photos and bag counts against issue; for fuel, supplier invoices against the meter; for drugs, vet invoices against the drug register; for eggs and milk, dispatch sheets against lay rate or herd yield. Births, deaths and sales photos from the line are a secondary input only. Each unannounced count re-performs the previous month's roll-forward as a test of the coordinator. The monthly signal is not relied on beyond the quarterly counts. T1 limits are calibrated on audited purchase lines after the books review. Pre-G1 counts sit in the pre-G1 cap; counts after G1 are booked to control-and-oversight cost (B10) and enter the G1 hurdle's control line.
6. **Incapacity route until the entity exists:** a limited power of attorney or bank-level delegate mandate with a named holder and a T2 cap (bank acceptance confirmed by the lawyer). The 14-day delegate is named on day 7 with T2-level powers only, never T3. A POA holder or delegate may sign the scheduled-class schedule within the prior approved totals, so payroll does not stop; T3 items before the entity exists go to the lawyer plus the accountant, with a third counter-signer (the executor or the vetted POA holder) named for that interval; any POA holder passes the B2 vetting, fidelity cover and conflict declaration. A stalled-feed rule links to the window memo's staged drought rows if the owner is unreachable.
7. **Bridge if the incumbent is excluded** (3–7 weeks before a contract manager arrives): the consultant stockman acts as lead with the unit lead (and is then excluded as counter and as B5 fall-through approver; the agronomist or coordinator is used, the accountant only if not co-releasing); the float moves to them; the owner or representative is on site for the first 1–2 weeks; the B8 72-hour playbook applies. Costed in the "no incumbent" 1–3%; the 1–2 weeks' owner site stint (about 40–80 h) is a contingency drawn from the crisis reserve.

| Tier | Who | Limit |

| T1 | Manager | Set at G0 so >=90% of the last 24 months' purchase lines fall within it (starting proposal 0.3% AOB); <=1.5% AOB per 30 days per supplier/category; within budget line. Call-offs under a pre-approved annual supply agreement go through the scheduled class |
| Scheduled recurring | Pre-approved by the owner per period (amounts and payees) | Payroll, tax, utilities, contracted supply call-offs (each call-off sits in exactly one route: this one); released as pure execution within whitelist and per-payee caps. The accountant prepares the schedule with a headcount and prior-period difference for the owner to sign; payroll schedules use last year's payroll by month as the baseline, restated for B0.3 findings and award changes, with a headcount cap per period from the roster and a pre-approved casual pool (named gang or contractor) onboarded once per season, its payments requiring countersigned day sheets and a muster sample in each count; other schedules use the supply-agreement price band or a seasonal baseline (B3); a schedule outside its baseline returns to per-item approval. Discretionary spend never passes this route |

| T3 | Owner only | Capital, disposals, borrowing, contracts >=12 months, hire/fire/pay of manager, deputy and representative, related-party deals, insurance settlements, **all vendor creation and whitelist entry**, bank-detail changes (call-back to a known number) |
| Emergency | Manager acts first | See B5 |

Anti-splitting is tested at audit. The charter includes the **"manager cannot" list** (release payments, hold bank signing rights, be sole recorder of stock, sole source of dashboard data, sole price contact with buyers), **compulsory 10-working-day consecutive leave** for manager and bookkeeper with the books reviewed meanwhile, and annual rotation of at least one unit lead.

## B2. Owner's representative
The 1.5% AOB per 30 days cap applies to **discretionary** (non-scheduled) items the representative approves after 48 h of owner silence, and to nothing else; scheduled recurring payments are executed against the owner's pre-approved schedule. Anything above, any T3 item and any bank-detail change needs the owner. The owner names a **delegate for up to 14 consecutive days of unavailability** (T2-level powers only, never T3); beyond that the lawyer-drafted entity-level alternate mandate applies. An emergency event is reviewed by someone other than its approver. Once the entity exists, the incapacity route uses the entity mandate or alternate-director resolution rather than a personal power of attorney (lawyer); before that, B0.6 applies. The bank sends every release over a threshold and every mandate change to both mandate holders and the external accountant. The representative is vetted like the manager, signs the conflict declaration and code of conduct, is covered by fidelity cover, may not be related to the manager, deputy or any supplier, and has their own payments and approvals reported in the quarterly cross-check. A paid steward is contracted by the entity with notice and independence terms (lawyer); cost sits inside the cap.

## B3. Payments, vendors and whitelist
Requester (unit lead), approver (manager), receiver (a different person, goods-received note with count), payer (owner or representative releases; the bookkeeper prepares only). Three-way match. **Vendor creation is T3:** independent registry check, bank account in the vendor's own name, relationship declaration, 3-quote record. Whitelist entries carry a fixed purpose and a **per-payee monthly cap**. New payees, changed amounts (+10% on prior period), bank-detail changes, over-cap and match failures drop out of the weekly batch to per-item approval. The batch screen is an **exception report**, not a list; payroll ties to the master file and muster. **Exception rules run in the banking platform** (new payee, changed bank details, over-cap, changed amount against the supply-agreement price band or a seasonal baseline plus an absolute floor, not a flat +10%), and the whitelist and payee-change log is held by the bank and the accountant, not the preparer. The bookkeeper reconciles monthly; **a non-preparer, from a different firm (or at least a different individual under separate engagement terms) than the bookkeeper, reconciles at least quarterly and matches vendor bank account, address and phone against the employee master file**; this quarterly step is in the core and the small-band cost. Vendor registry and beneficial-owner lookups are done by the accountant, not supplied by the manager or vendor. Bank daily limit equals the largest plausible batch. Owner credentials: app or hardware MFA; no approvals from messages or links; a "payment instruction from the owner" received by the bookkeeper is void unless in the banking system or confirmed by call-back.

**Small-staff compensating controls** (farms of 3–6 people where a separate receiver does not exist): supplier delivery note signed by the driver, calibrated bag count or weighbridge photo, representative spot-checks of deliveries, auditor samples goods-received notes.

## B4. Cash, stock, payroll, procurement, incentives
- **Cash:** buyers pay direct to farm account; unavoidable cash uses serialised receipts, two-person count, banking within 48 h; statements go to owner and external accountant; imprest petty cash with hard cap and weekly count by a non-custodian.
- **Stock:** monthly signed reconciliation; each death evidenced by timestamped photo (suspect deaths in situ, A1), witness above a value threshold, logged disposal; gate pass and two-person load-out; two-key drug store; feed by calibrated count at receipt and issue. Variance 3–5% plus feed-conversion and hen-day benchmarks; 4-week rolling trends catch slow drips. **Gate log and guards report to the representative or security contractor**, weekly copy to the owner's store.
- **Payroll:** master file with ID and photo held by the external bookkeeper; owner approves changes; pay to the employee's own account/wallet (unbanked workers: lawyer's alternative); attendance by geotagged check-in or auditor muster; casual day sheets countersigned; annual headcount-to-payroll match.
- **Procurement/sales:** three quotes above threshold, annual re-tender, yearly conflict declarations; minimum price list, owner-signed larger contracts, credit sales approved by owner. Audit scope includes **whitelist price benchmarking**.
- **Incentives:** bonus only on audited figures, 50% deferred with clawback (lawful deductions per lawyer), multi-year measures, welfare/safety gate, integrity multiplier; pool sized to be credible against the exposure estimate in B10. A charter variant exists for an equity/profit-share manager-operator.

## B5. Emergency envelope
Default <=1% AOB per event, **aggregate cap 3% AOB per year and 1.5% per quarter**, after which every event needs the owner or representative. **Vet attendance, humane destruction, carcass disposal and authority-directed measures are carved out of the cap** (still logged and reviewed in 72 h), and the deputy fall-through survives cap exhaustion for them. Notice to owner and representative goes through the entity system or carrier log **at declaration**, not self-logged. **Commitment now, payment later:** payment is released at the 72-h review; a pre-approved emergency vendor list extends the B0.2 day-7 list at G2, with an owner-held capped float or card. If both are silent for 2 h, the fall-through approver is outside the manager's line (the consultant stockman or agronomist), after the logged contact attempts, and **never the payee**; a vet cannot authorise spend on their own invoice. Payment by whitelisted vendor or direct bank transfer, not cash, with photo and incident note. Owner or representative reviews each event within 72 h; a repeating category triggers an audit check.

## B6. Audit
Minimum 4 spot-counts a year with a **maximum interval of 4 months**, from G2 exit (B0.5). The owner appoints, pays and instructs the auditor; reports go to the owner first; auditors are not peers, rotate at most every 2 years and declare conflicts. The charter grants access to premises, records and staff interviews without manager consent. Auditors (not the owner) perform spot-counts. Tiers: monthly bank/supplier reconciliation by the external bookkeeper from direct feeds, plus the quarterly non-preparer reconciliation (B3); randomised unannounced spot-counts (minimum 4 a year at the small band, 6–12 otherwise; fortnightly in the pilot) counting live animals against tags (a variance above amber gets a **second-team recount (same visit or within 24 h) and known-movement reconciliation** before it is called failed), payroll muster, procurement against market price, sales against bank; annual external review by a licensed accountant (a different firm from the bookkeeper; a statutory audit only where the law requires, lawyer and accountant to say) with bank/supplier/buyer confirmations. Vet who prescribes does not certify deaths for insurance claims.

## B7. Data integrity, privacy and labour law
The entity (not the owner personally) owns data stores, accounts and domains; owner is admin with the lawyer or representative as **second admin**, manager is editor with no delete; append-only log with user, timestamp, photo or GPS on deaths, deliveries, sales and feed issues; duplicate-image and metadata checks at audit. **Entity-owned devices** only (no personal phones), offline capture with a queue, sync on signal recovery and a device-clock flag; a **named raw-store reviewer independent of the farm line** (the coordinator) samples photos weekly. The no-signal fallback (SMS count plus paper) is counted as unverified until audited. Independent feeds the manager cannot touch: bank, supplier statements, vet invoices, fuel and power meters, gate records. The lawyer's privacy row covers cameras, biometrics, geotagged check-in, vehicle tracking, ID copies held by the bookkeeper (processor terms, retention, access log), the hotline, background and lifestyle checks, and staff notices in local language. Labour row: working-time and rest rules for night watch and 7-day cover, lawful deductions, right-to-work and age checks for casual labour and contractors. Retention: financial 7 years or local law; animal and drug records per regulator; off-site backup under entity control.

## B8. Fraud-response protocol (one page; lawyer and insurer sign)
Triggers: failed spot-count, variance above red, whistle-blower report, bank anomaly. **The recount rule applies to count variances only:** recount on the same visit or within 24 h with the gate controlled, and the protocol starts if the variance persists beyond red. A hotline report or bank anomaly starts the 24-h secure step at once. It is not a kill trigger. Led by the owner or representative, **or by the lawyer when either is conflicted**; the whistle-blower hotline goes to the owner and the lawyer or external auditor, never to the manager, deputy or representative. Device imaging covers entity-owned devices only (lawyer to confirm). First 24 h: secure bank access, credentials, records, stores and cameras; image devices; manager informed only after records are secured; suspension on pay as local law allows; interview protocol. Notify the insurer within the policy's deadline; police-referral criteria set in advance; no-retaliation for the reporter; dismissal only after a documented hearing. Sudden manager exit uses the 72-hour playbook (bank control, count, stores, staff briefing, credentials revoked within 1 h, keys re-set).

## B9. Continuity and legal
Lawyer scope items: transfer map and cost of licences, holding registration, tenure and lender consent into the entity (tax, stamp duty, non-transferability); entity-level mandate or alternate-director route; de facto director and OHS-officer analysis for the manager and representative. Separate farm entity (owner director; manager an employee, never director or signatory); written contracts (90-day notice, return of records, confidentiality, clawback per lawyer); manager vetting (non-nominated references, lawful checks, probation); named deputy unrelated to the manager; code of conduct and anonymous channel; limited power of attorney (per B0.6), standing letter of instruction, "if I am gone" file with the representative, will/trust; insurance (fidelity/crime for manager, bookkeeper and representative, and professional-indemnity confirmation for the accountant; employer's and public liability; property; motor; livestock/crop where available), with claims run only by owner or representative.

## B10. Control-and-oversight cost: definition, bands, expected-loss test
**Included (numerator):** external bookkeeper; spot-counts/auditor; quarterly non-preparer reconciliation and annual accountant review; consultant agronomist and stockman backstop for the first cycle; Farm Model upkeep after G1 (hours and fee; held by the coordinator with agronomist co-sign); representative or steward; hotline; fidelity premium; entity/legal (amortised over 3 years); GSM alarms and sensors (amortised); data store, geotag/camera tooling; coordinator. **Excluded, reported separately as "management cost":** manager and deputy pay and the **interim lead** while acting as manager. Vet-of-record and agronomist routine services are production inputs. Denominator: base-case gross revenue.

| Band (base-case gross revenue, USD-equivalent default) | Minimum set | Cap on control-and-oversight cost |
|---|---|---|
| Small (<250k) | Core only; spot-counts at 4+/yr; part-time representative | ~7% |
| Medium (250k–1m) | Core plus quarterly accountant review, steward at part-time | ~8% |
| Larger (>1m) | Full set | ~8–10% |

The cap must also leave control cost at most one-third of **OMBM** (operating margin before management: revenue less all operating costs except manager, deputy and controls, including hired production labour, repairs, depreciation, rates and insurance; **unpaid family and owner labour priced at the local wage**; before interest, tax and drawings; no land rent charged; in real terms). The margin screen (PLAN decision sheet) is a necessary test and the B12 hurdle decides; the bands only set the cap.

**Core (never dropped):** owner/representative-held mandates; bank and supplier feeds direct to owner; external bookkeeper; owner-only vendor creation; individual ID and death evidence; randomised spot-counts; quarterly non-preparer reconciliation; annual accountant review; Day-7 financial pack; Day-7 safety pack items; compulsory leave; fraud-response protocol. If the core does not fit the cap, the answer is scale down or lease.

**Expected-loss test (optional controls):** for each channel (feed, drugs, livestock, fuel, cash sales, payroll, procurement) estimate annual exposure x probability x detection effectiveness against the control's annual cost. Controls below break-even are cut; the owner signs the residual risk. The bonus pool is sized against the same exposure estimate. Wage expectations of 3–5 manager candidates are tested against the control set before it is finalised.

## B11. Technology
Paper-first for **recording**; all **transmission** goes through the evidence layer in B7. Life-critical alarms (poultry house temperature and power, vaccine fridge, water reserve) run on stand-alone GSM units, not data. Fallbacks: two recipients per alert; dead-man's switch (24 h silence to the deputy, 48 h to the representative); SIM-expiry and 3-day power-out drills; paper power and water procedures; sensor cost-benefit and calibration budget. Dashboard capped at 8–12 KPIs, each with method and data owner; labour model includes control and compliance hours; manager and bookkeeper leave falls in a trough.

## B12. Hurdle procedure, two worked examples, role map (illustrative assumptions, not forecasts)
**One convention, all in real terms** (OMBM, rents, returns and land value are inflation-adjusted). Each option's value is its **annual after-risk income to the owner on the same asset base** (land, stock, machinery, plus the owner's time priced at p). A hold option keeps the land, so its value includes the return on the land (OMBM charges no land rent; lease rent is that return) **plus g × land value**, where g is the expected real annual change in land value. Sale converts the asset base to cash and earns the declared real return. No land charge is subtracted from any option.
**Terms.** OMBM = operating margin before management (B10; "gross margin" is narrower and not used). Risk charge r = 4% (default 3–5%). K = operating capital at risk (stock, machinery, working capital). p = hourly shadow price (50). **g: default +1.0% real a year, declared by the owner or an independent valuer; it is uncertain, and land-value risk is stated, not charged: the printed flip point shows how much it matters.** Declared real return on sale proceeds: default 2%. Set-up (self-run, post-G1; 60k over 4 years): entity and legal 15k, SOPs and tools 15k, interim lead after G1 and consultant backstop 20k, double-running 10k. Transition (every restructure option; default 30k over 4 years): lawyer, tenant or operator search, stock-sale commission and tax, plus staff redundancy to the extent the tenant or operator does not take staff (labour law).
**Value, per year:**
- Self-run: OMBM − manager and deputy − controls − (hours × p) − (set-up ÷ years) − r × K + g × L
- Management company: 0.9 × OMBM (10% performance haircut, default) − fee (5–10% of revenue) − light controls − (hours × p) − transition − r × K + g × L
- Lease: rent + return on net sale proceeds of stock and machinery − **landlord-retained costs (rates, insurance, fixed repairs; default 9k, range 6–12k, unless the rent is stated net of them)** − (hours × p) − transition + g × L
- Contract grazing: grazing fee + return on proceeds of stock sold − retained costs (fencing, water, the landlord items above) − (hours × p) − transition − r × K retained + g × L
- Manager-operator: **qualitative only** until Gate 0 supplies terms; it cannot be ranked and appears only as a negotiation option when the outcome is "restructure"
- Sale (only if you would sell): net proceeds of land, stock and machinery after selling cost and tax (default 5%; tax can be far higher, so the accountant quantifies it, with debt repaid from proceeds) × declared real return
- Pre-G1 spend is sunk: excluded from every value (it still counts toward the cumulative-loss kill trigger).
**Printed output:** each option's value and its rate on the asset base, and the **flip point g\***: the g at which sale and the best hold option swap places. **Like-for-like check:** every option is stated after the same items (owner time, risk charge, set-up or transition, g) and in real terms; an option that omits one is not comparable.

**Stress limit (default):** in the worst single scenario (price −20%; yield −20%; drought −30%; disease −10% stock; inputs +25%; notifiable event), computed on the option's own cash flows after fees, **net cash flow after management, controls, debt service, tax and drawings is no lower than −10% of K** and cash stays above the floor with the funded reserve. You sign the amount at G1. The examples assume no debt, tax or drawings (declared at G1). Lease and sale pass trivially (tenant default is a qualitative flag); contract grazing is computed from its quote or flagged conditional.
**Procedure, deterministic:**
1. Value each option you declared acceptable.
2. **Drop any option with value at or below zero or failing the stress limit.**
3. Rank the rest. A tie is any option within 5% of the **top** value; ties go to the lower owner hours, then the lower worst-scenario loss.
4. Top-ranked is self-run: **proceed**. Lease, grazing or management company: **restructure** to it. Sale: **conditional exit**: obtain an independent land valuation and tax advice before any step, and re-run with them. If the top option cannot be executed at quoted terms, the next-ranked applies and the ranking is re-run.
5. A self-run or management-company winner whose value is under 10% of its OMBM (or whose OMBM is zero or negative) is a **conditional pass** (probation-length, re-confirmation triggers). Lease and sale are not subject.
6. If no option remains: **no viable option, do not proceed**; the owner chooses lease or sale at the best price, or revisits the declared alternatives.
"Proceed" can never be returned at a value at or below zero or a failed stress limit.

**Common inputs:** revenue 500k; K 500k (stock 300k, machinery 150k, working capital 50k); asset base 2.45m (land L = 2m, stock and machinery 450k); manager and deputy 60k; controls 40k (8%); owner hours 280 (14k) self-run, 115 (5.75k) management company, 30 (1.5k) lease; r×K = 20k; g × L = +20k; lease rent 60k (3%) plus 9k (2% on 450k); management company fee 35k (7%), light controls 30k; sale 2.45m less 5% = 2.3275m at 2% = 46.6k. Stress: drought −30% takes 150k off OMBM; the assumed notifiable-event hit is 40k off OMBM.

| Option (value, k; rate on 2.45m) | Example 1: typical farm, OMBM 110k (22%) | Example 2: strong farm, OMBM 250k (50%) |
|---|---|---|
| Self-run | −39 + 20 = **−19** (−0.8%): dropped; also fails the one-third control limit (40k vs 36.7k) | 101 + 20 = **121** (4.9%); net cash: drought 0, notifiable +110, both above −50 |
| Management company | 0.75 + 20 = **20.8**; net cash in drought −101, below −50: dropped | 126.75 + 20 = **146.8** (6.0%) |
| Lease | 60 + 9 − 9 − 1.5 − 7.5 + 20 = **71.0** (2.9%) | **71.0** (2.9%) |
| Sale (if you would sell) | **46.6** (1.9%) | **46.6** (1.9%) |
| Result | Lease first: **restructure to lease**, whether or not you would sell. Flip point: sale overtakes lease only if g < −0.2% a year. At g = −2%, lease is 11.0 and the result becomes **conditional exit** | Management company first: **restructure**. If none is available, self-run (121) beats lease (71): **proceed**. Flip points: sale overtakes self-run only if g < −2.7%, the management company only if g < −4.0% |

Break-even of self-run (g cancels between hold options): against lease, OMBM about 200k (40% of revenue); against sale, about 176k (35%). A management company beats self-run until OMBM is about 508k, so on these placeholders it wins at every credible margin. Most farms with 5–15% net margins fail the hurdle, and on many farms the honest answer is lease, restructure or exit. Example 2 is not typical; it shows what a farm must earn for self-run to win. The pre-G1 cap of 25k is 23% of OMBM in Example 1 and 10% in Example 2. All defaults (g, the 2% real return, rents, fees, sale costs) are placeholders; at the days 1–3 screen replace rent and fees with 1–2 indicative quotes each and ask a valuer for g.

**Role map:** one named person per role (representative, coordinator, Farm Model owner, raw-store reviewer, reconciler, accountant, emergency approver, co-releaser, goods-received signer, spot-counter, interim lead, deputy, delegate, hotline recipient); no person holds more than three roles without the owner's sign-off, recorded at G0 exit; the co-releaser and spot-counter differ; coordinator, raw-store reviewer and reconciler merge into one independent role where possible, never the representative.
