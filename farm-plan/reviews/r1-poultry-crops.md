# Review R1: Poultry and Crops/Agronomy (adversarial)

Scope: PLAN.md Draft v1. The plan is a management-structure and work-package plan. It contains no technical content, so most findings are omissions that will bite when the work packages are executed or when the farm goes live.

1. [MAJOR] No decision on the poultry enterprise, and no gating on location, which drives everything. WP2 says "layers or broilers". Layers are a 72-80 week, daily-collection, cold-chain, egg-grading business. Broilers are a 5-7 week all-in/all-out business with a hard processing/slaughter bottleneck. They have different labor, capital, market, licensing and disease profiles. Location/country is only an open question (section 6), yet HPAI vaccination policy, Newcastle (ND) vaccination, drug legality and withdrawal times, slaughter licensing, and cattle/small-ruminant disease lists all depend on it. Why it matters: SOPs written before this is settled are generic and will be wrong. Fix: make country, poultry type and scale hard gates on Phase 1. Do not start enterprise SOPs until they are answered. Add a go/no-go economic screen per enterprise (gross margin per labor-hour at manager-run scale), because "run without owner" favors fewer, simpler enterprises.

2. [MAJOR] Four-plus enterprises (cattle, sheep, goats, poultry, crops) with a remote owner is a complexity trap, and the plan never questions enterprise mix. Each enterprise needs specialist skills the Farm Manager must supervise. Why it matters: absentee operations fail on enterprise sprawl and supervision depth, not on SOP quality. Fix: add an enterprise-rationalization step in Phase 1. Default to the minimum number of enterprises that share infrastructure (e.g. one ruminant species plus one poultry module plus crops that feed them), and drop the rest or lease them out.

3. [MAJOR] Biosecurity is listed but not designed as a farm-level system. Cattle, small ruminants, poultry (possibly wild-bird exposed) and crops share people, vehicles, boots, water, feed trucks and manure. HPAI, ND, Salmonella and Brucella/zoonoses need: single-species zoning, a clean/dirty line, a perimeter and a visitor/vehicle log, and dedicated boots and clothing per unit with no cross-entry by workers or herders. Why it matters: one HPAI or velogenic ND introduction wipes out the flock and triggers movement restrictions on the whole holding, including live animal and egg sales. Fix: WP4 must produce a site layout with a poultry exclusion zone, a rule that poultry attendants never enter ruminant areas, control of free-range access, wild-bird/waterfowl contact prevention (netting, no open surface water, covered feed), rodent control, a feed-delivery protocol, and a written outbreak-response plan keyed to the national veterinary authority's reporting duty.

4. [MAJOR] Notifiable disease reporting and legal exposure are absent. HPAI, ND, FMD, Brucellosis, anthrax and others (country-dependent) are notifiable. Under "management by exception" the owner gets alerts, but nothing says the manager must notify the state vet within hours, or who stops sales and movements. Why it matters: failure to report carries fines, loss of compensation and prosecution. Fix: add a notifiable-disease SOP with a phone tree (vet, state authority, owner), a standstill rule (no movement of birds, eggs, manure or people off-farm until cleared), and a sample-submission protocol for mortality clusters.

5. [MAJOR] Mortality alert thresholds and "exception" triggers are not defined, and the poultry timing makes daily-check failure fatal. A house of layers or broilers can go from normal to 5-10% mortality in 24-48 hours (heat stress, ventilation or power failure, water failure, ND, HPAI). Weekly dashboards do not catch this. Fix: define numeric triggers per bird class (e.g. broilers: more than 0.5% in a day or more than 1% in 2 days at any age; layers: more than 0.1% per day, or a 10% egg-production drop in 2 days or a feed/water intake drop above 10%). Provide automated house sensors (temperature, ammonia optional, water meter, power) with phone alerts. Require a mandatory dead-bird count by phone form every evening, and an alert if the form is not submitted by a set time.

6. [MAJOR] Power and water are single points of failure for poultry and are only mentioned as "maintenance". Tunnel-ventilated or closed houses fail within 15-30 minutes of a power cut in hot weather (mass mortality). Water failure of a few hours cuts intake and kills birds in heat. Fix: generator with automatic changeover and fuel stock, alarm to the manager and owner, backup water storage sized for at least 24-48 h of the full flock plus livestock, and a documented manual-ventilation fallback (curtain drop). This is also the cold-chain issue below.

7. [MAJOR] Cold chain and egg/meat handling are not addressed anywhere. Eggs need grading, cooling and, in many markets, refrigerated transport at 15-18 C, with a distribution plan that does not rely on the owner. Vaccines (Marek's, ND, IBD, IB, fowl pox, plus cattle/small-ruminant vaccines such as FMD, CCPP, PPR, brucellosis, clostridials) need 2-8 C storage with a temperature log, and power failure there silently renders a whole season's vaccination program ineffective. Broiler processing needs slaughter capacity and a chilling chain. Fix: add a cold-chain SOP with temperature loggers (alarmed), a dedicated vaccine fridge on backup power, a cool-box and ice-pack protocol for field vaccination, an egg-room design, and a named processor or cold-room/transport contract.

8. [MAJOR] Drug and withdrawal-period control lacks an enforcement mechanism. WP4 mentions withdrawal tracking, but nothing stops an untrained worker treating a layer or dairy animal and sending product to market. Why it matters: residue violations carry legal and market-access consequences, and antibiotic misuse breeds resistance. Fix: a locked drug store, treatment only on vet or manager order, a treatment register tied to a "no-sale" flag in the records system, and a vet prescription requirement (also a legal requirement in many countries).

9. [MAJOR] Feed is the largest cost and integration point (about 60-70% of poultry production cost, and it dominates ruminant cost in the dry season). The plan checks "feed demand vs crop output" only at the end (integration reviewer, Phase 3). Layers and broilers need balanced rations (maize/soy/mineral premix), and farm crops rarely meet poultry protein needs without soybean meal, so the mass balance will usually show a large purchase. Mycotoxin risk (aflatoxin in stored maize, groundnut cake) is a serious poultry and dairy hazard. Fix: do a feed budget in Phase 1: tonnes of energy, protein and forage per enterprise per month against farm production, with explicit buy-in volumes, price scenarios, a storage plan with moisture limits (maize at or below 13%) and a mycotoxin binder/testing rule. Decide early what crops serve as feed and what are cash crops.

10. [MAJOR] Crop calendar vs livestock labor peaks is not planned, yet it is the usual reason absentee farms collapse. Planting, weeding, harvest and drying coincide with calving/lambing/kidding, brooder restocking, vaccination rounds and wet-season disease peaks. Staff "run from daily task sheets" cannot absorb seasonal peaks. Fix: build a month-by-month labor histogram (hours by enterprise) in Phase 2 and check it against the rostered headcount. Staff peaks with contract/casual labor with pre-agreed rates, mechanize, or drop crops with high labor peaks. Time calving and harvest apart where possible.

11. [MAJOR] The timeline mismatches biological and crop seasons, and today is 15 October 2026. Phases 0-3 run about 12 weeks and Phase 4 runs to month 6, but crops are decided by planting windows. Depending on hemisphere and rainfall regime, the next planting window may be imminent or already passed, and the plan has no seed, land preparation, soil test or input procurement lead times (seed orders, fertilizer, vaccines for incoming chicks, which are typically ordered 4-8 weeks ahead). Hands-off by month 6 means the first full season is unsupervised by the designers. Fix: add a "this season" fast track now: confirm planting dates with local rainfall records, procure inputs on the existing manager's judgment, and run the Phase 4 shadow period across at least one complete crop cycle and one complete poultry cycle (a layer cycle is about 18 months, so use at least one broiler batch or point-of-lay purchase plus a full ruminant calendar event). Do not declare hands-off before one full season.

12. [MAJOR] Climate risk and drought/flood contingency are treated as a generic "weather playbook" with no specifics. Rainfed crops are exposed to dry spells, so the plan needs irrigation sizing, water source reliability (borehole yield tests, seasonal river flow, legal abstraction permits), soil moisture monitoring and a drought destocking trigger (stock reduction at defined pasture or rainfall thresholds). Fix: quantify water demand (cattle about 30-50 L/d, small ruminants 5-10 L/d, poultry about 0.25-0.4 L/bird/d plus cooling, irrigation mm/ha) against reliable supply in the driest month of a 1-in-10 year. Define destocking and crop-insurance triggers in the risk register.

13. [MAJOR] Single point of failure on a Farm Manager with no verification layer beyond a monthly audit. Fraud, theft, feed pilferage, egg shrinkage and silent drug misuse are the dominant losses on absentee farms. Monthly audits catch problems after losses. Fix: add weekly flock and herd reconciliation (feed in vs eggs/weight out, with feed conversion ratio and hen-day production as automatic fraud indicators), cameras at feed store and egg room, a signed dispatch book, unannounced spot checks and manager incentives tied to audited, not self-reported, numbers. Note that the owner's "1 hr/week" is an assumption with no stress test: during an outbreak he is on call for days.

14. [MINOR] Poultry vaccination and flock-management details are missing: Marek's at the hatchery, ND (Lasota/I-2 or inactivated), IBD (Gumboro), IB, fowl pox, Salmonella (layers), coccidiosis programs. They must be matched to local disease challenge and supplier. Fix: have the vet retainer produce a dated vaccination calendar per flock keyed off placement date, and require chick sourcing from a certified, disease-free hatchery with a placement checklist.

15. [MINOR] Cleanout/restocking SOP must include a downtime interval and litter handling. All-in/all-out with at least 14 days downtime, wet cleaning, disinfection, a rodent and darkling beetle program, and a litter removal plan. Litter and manure are an asset and a hazard: composting must reach temperature to kill pathogens before spreading, and fresh poultry litter must not be put on ready-to-eat crops. Fix: define a compost windrow SOP (about 55 C for at least 3 days, turned), spreading intervals before harvest (e.g. 90-120 days for edible crops touching soil), and nitrogen rates (poultry litter is very high in N and P).

16. [MINOR] Manure integration is unquantified. Nutrient flows from cattle, sheep, goats and poultry could cover a substantial part of crop fertility, but nobody is tasked with the nutrient budget, and excess P and N can cause runoff or groundwater problems, and legal exposure in some jurisdictions. Fix: add a manure and nutrient balance to the crop WP, with storage capacity (a manure store sized for the closed spreading season) and soil-test-based rates.

17. [MINOR] Soil testing is mentioned without cadence or depth. Fix: baseline test per management unit (0-20 cm and 20-40 cm, pH, organic C, P, K, micronutrients, salinity/sodicity if irrigated), repeat every 2-3 years, tie liming and fertilizer to results, and map fields by soil type before planning rotations.

18. [MINOR] IPM is named but relies on "agronomist retainer visits". Pest outbreaks (fall armyworm, locusts, blight, rust, stem borers, etc., depending on region) move in days. Fix: a scouting schedule with phone-photo reporting by field hands, action thresholds per pest, and an approved pesticide list with PHI/REI intervals, PPE, spray records and safe storage. Add a rule against spraying near poultry houses or water sources.

19. [MINOR] Post-harvest losses are only "drying/storage SOP". Grain losses to moisture, insects and rodents can be 10-30% without hermetic storage. Fix: drying to safe moisture, hermetic bags or silos, inspection logs, and a sell-or-store rule that accounts for price seasonality (harvest-glut prices) rather than ad hoc marketing.

20. [MINOR] Pasture and forage conservation is thin. Stocking rates must be set from measured forage production, not generic tables, and dry-season feed (hay, silage, crop residues) must be made during the labor-peak wet season. Fix: a forage budget by month, a hay or silage target tonnage, and a destocking trigger when carry-over feed falls below a set number of days.

21. [MINOR] Mortality disposal. Dead birds and carcasses need composting pits, incineration or rendering to a standard, particularly during a disease event when 100% mortality or culling means thousands of carcasses. Fix: pre-identify a burial or composting site (away from water, with permission), equipment access and a stockpile of disinfectant and PPE.

22. [MINOR] Animal welfare and food safety certification is generic. Egg and meat buyers (supermarkets, hotels, export) typically require traceability, HACCP-style records and sometimes certification. Fix: ask in Phase 0 which channel is targeted and design records to meet it, including flock-to-egg-batch traceability.

23. [MINOR] Staff skill assumptions. Poultry attendants and herders "running from task sheets" need hands-on training in necropsy basics, thermal comfort observation, and recognition of heat stress and respiratory signs; the plan has training only as a job-description item. Fix: a certified training and competency sign-off per role before anyone works unsupervised, with refreshers each season.

24. [MINOR] Data and monitoring rely on phone forms and a spreadsheet, and the plan assumes connectivity that is an open question. Fix: offline-capable forms, SMS fallback for critical alerts, and a defined escalation if the manager's phone is unreachable. Sensors without maintenance create false alarms and alarm fatigue, so budget for calibration and replacement.

25. [MINOR] The Stata/R analysis templates and the 90-day transition plan are included but no one is tasked with checking that the baseline KPIs are physically measurable by this team (e.g. hen-day %, FCR, calving interval, kg grain/ha). Fix: restrict the dashboard to about 10-12 KPIs, each with a defined measurement method and a data owner.

## Summary
Biggest risks: no enterprise/location/scale decisions, no farm-wide biosecurity and notifiable-disease design, no power/water/cold-chain resilience, no early feed and labor-peak balance, a timeline that ignores seasons, and weak fraud/verification. Fix those before writing enterprise SOPs.
