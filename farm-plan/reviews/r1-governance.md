# R1 Governance / Internal-Control Review of PLAN.md (Draft v1)

Reviewer stance: adversarial. The plan is an operations-design plan with a control veneer. It says "controls" in nine places and specifies none. An absentee owner with a weekly one-page dashboard fed by the people being watched is the textbook setup for slow, quiet looting. Items are ranked roughly by severity within each tag.

## MAJOR

1. [MAJOR] The Farm Manager is a single point of failure AND a single point of fraud.
   - Problem: The manager "runs daily ops", holds delegated spending authority, supervises all unit leads, and (implicitly) controls hiring of workers, supplier choice, stock records and the data that feeds the dashboard. "Deputy + documented handover" addresses departure, not abuse.
   - Why it matters: One person who orders, receives, records, pays (or approves payment), and reports is able to steal and conceal. Manager capture is the dominant loss mode in absentee farms; losses typically run 5-20% of turnover for years before detection.
   - Fix: Split the role by function, not just by title. Manager may initiate and approve operations; may NOT (a) release payments, (b) be the sole recorder of stock movements, (c) be the sole contact with buyers/suppliers on pricing, (d) be the sole source of dashboard data, (e) hold bank signing rights. Write these prohibitions into the charter as "manager cannot" list, not only "manager can" list. Add a mandatory annual rotation of at least one unit lead and a 10-working-day compulsory consecutive leave per year for the manager and bookkeeper, during which the deputy runs the farm and someone reviews the books (leave is the cheapest fraud detector available).

2. [MAJOR] No segregation of duties anywhere in the cash cycle.
   - Problem: Section 3.6 lists "petty-cash & purchase-approval matrix" and "monthly reconciliation" with no owner for each step. Nothing separates request, approval, receipt, recording, payment, reconciliation.
   - Why it matters: Classic schemes (fake suppliers, inflated invoices, kickbacks, split purchases below the threshold, paying for undelivered inputs) all rely on one person spanning two or more of these steps.
   - Fix: Mandate a four-way separation: Requester (unit lead) / Approver (manager, within limit) / Receiver (a different person, signs goods-received note with quantity count) / Payer (owner or owner's nominee releases funds; bookkeeper prepares, never releases). Three-way match (PO, GRN, invoice) before any payment. Bank dual authorisation: manager proposes, owner (or independent finance agent) releases every payment above a small floor via banking app with owner as second approver. Bookkeeper must be external or off-site and must not have bank access.

3. [MAJOR] Bank, cash and payment control is undefined; owner is told only "cash below floor".
   - Problem: Alert is on a cash floor only. No rule on who holds bank mandates, card limits, mobile-money wallets, cash sales receipts, or how cash sales (eggs, live animals, manure, grain at farm gate) get banked.
   - Why it matters: Farm-gate cash sales are the highest-risk revenue stream: unrecorded sales are invisible to any dashboard. Cash floor alerts do not detect skimming, only exhaustion, i.e. after the damage.
   - Fix: (a) Owner or independent party is bank mandate holder; manager has a capped card/wallet with monthly top-up against reconciled spend. (b) No cash sales unless unavoidable; buyers pay direct to farm account/mobile-money paybill; where cash taken, pre-numbered receipt books (carbon, serialised), two-person count, banked within 24-48 hours, deposit slip reconciled to receipts by the bookkeeper. (c) Bank statements delivered directly to owner and external accountant, never via manager. (d) Petty cash on imprest with a hard cap and weekly count by someone other than the custodian.

4. [MAJOR] The "independent monthly audit" is neither independent nor defined.
   - Problem: "external/rotating peer" - a peer farmer is a neighbour, possibly a relative of the workers, a competitor, a potential buyer or a collusion partner. Who selects, schedules, pays, and receives the report? Not stated. Monthly notice patterns are predictable. Scope is "stock counts, feed & drug use vs records, cash", which excludes payroll, procurement, sales and related-party checks.
   - Why it matters: If the manager picks or schedules the auditor, the audit is a management tool, not a control. Predictable visits are gamed (borrow animals, pre-stage feed, fix the books the day before).
   - Fix: Owner (or an independent party) appoints, pays and instructs the auditor; the report goes direct to owner, with a copy to the manager only after the owner has read it. Unannounced, randomised timing (not "first Friday"). Two tiers: monthly unannounced spot-count by a rotating non-peer (e.g. vet-tech student or agri-accounting firm junior), plus annual full external review by a licensed accountant with livestock census and a bank/supplier/buyer confirmation sample. Auditors must be rotated at most every 2 years and must declare conflicts in writing. Scope must include payroll (physical muster against payroll), procurement vs market price, sales vs bank, and related-party checks.

5. [MAJOR] Livestock and inventory controls are asserted ("animal ID & records") but absent as controls; shrinkage is the default outcome.
   - Problem: No reference to reconciling opening herd + births + purchases - deaths - sales - slaughter - theft = closing herd; no rule on who certifies a death, who disposes of a carcass, who signs off a cull; no tag/ID audit; no feed or drug usage-to-output benchmark (feed per kg gain, per dozen eggs, per litre of milk). Hens and small ruminants are easiest to bleed.
   - Why it matters: Deaths are the standard cover for theft ("it died, we buried it"). Mortality alerts at a "spike" threshold are exactly calibrated to what a thief avoids: a steady low drip of 1-2% a month.
   - Fix: (a) Monthly herd/flock reconciliation as a formal ledger, signed by manager and counter. (b) Every death: photo of tagged carcass (or ear tag retained) with timestamp via phone form, vet or unit-lead witness for animals above a value threshold, disposal witnessed (burial/incineration log). Tags/ears retained and counted at audit. (c) Slaughter, culls, and sales need movement permits/gate passes, a two-person load-out count, and a security log. (d) Feed conversion, egg per hen-day, weaning % and kidding/lambing % benchmarks with variance triggers that fire at modest deviation (e.g. 3-5% unexplained), not only at crisis levels. (e) Drug and vaccine register with batch numbers, vet reconciliation to purchases, and locked store with two keys (store-keeper and unit lead). (f) Feed and grain: weighbridge or calibrated bag counts at receipt and at issue; silo/bin dip readings.

6. [MAJOR] Delegated authority is unquantified and the escalation tree is left as a deliverable, so the architecture is not testable.
   - Problem: "up to a spending limit" and "call the owner triggers" are not defined. No cumulative cap (month/quarter/enterprise), no anti-splitting rule, no treatment of emergency spend, no dual-signature tier, no rule for related-party contracts, no limit on hiring/firing/pay changes, loans, asset disposals, or lease/sales contracts.
   - Why it matters: A per-transaction limit is defeated by splitting. Without cumulative caps and a "no related-party" rule, the manager can steer money to relatives. Without disposal controls, machinery and animals walk out as "sold cheap".
   - Fix: A written matrix with at least four tiers (unit lead / manager / manager + owner / owner only), per-transaction AND rolling 30-day cumulative per supplier and per category, and a budget-variance lock (spend over budget line needs owner approval). Explicit owner-only list: capital items, asset sales/write-offs, new suppliers above a threshold, contracts over 12 months, hiring/firing/pay of managers and deputies, any related-party deal, any borrowing, any insurance claim settlement. Suppliers: vendor master file changes (new vendor, bank-detail change) need owner or independent confirmation, with call-back to a known number (bank-detail changes are the standard invoice fraud).

7. [MAJOR] Incentive design is not fraud-resistant and may reward concealment.
   - Problem: "KPI-linked incentives" with no specification. If bonuses key on mortality, cost per kg, or profit, then the manager controls both the numbers and the payout. Low reported mortality earns a bonus and invites unreported deaths and sold-not-recorded animals.
   - Why it matters: Misaligned bonuses create the exact cover-up they were meant to prevent. Short-horizon KPIs also induce deferred maintenance, understocked inputs, and mining soil fertility to flatter this year's margin.
   - Fix: Pay bonus on audited figures only (after the annual external review), deferred or clawback-able (e.g. 50% paid after next audit), with multi-year metrics (herd value change, soil/pasture condition, asset register condition) and a safety/health/welfare gate. Make the data-integrity score (audit variance, unexplained shrinkage) a bonus multiplier, so honesty is the most profitable strategy. Consider a modest equity-like long-term element (profit share vesting over 3 years) to reduce quit-and-loot risk. Never pay bonuses on self-reported production.

8. [MAJOR] The monitoring data is self-reported by the monitored; the dashboard is a manager-controlled narrative.
   - Problem: Section 3.9 has "phone-form record keeping into central spreadsheet." Spreadsheets are editable, have no audit trail, and are typically administered by the manager. The weekly one-page dashboard is built from this.
   - Why it matters: The owner sees only what the manager (or his staff) chooses to enter. Records can be retro-edited; the owner has no way to tell. Dashboard exceptions are only triggered if the data is truthful.
   - Fix: Append-only logging with timestamps, user IDs and GPS/photo evidence on key events (deaths, deliveries, sales, feed issues); the manager gets no delete/edit-past-records right; edits are logged and visible to the owner. Data store owned by the owner's account (owner = admin; manager = editor). Independent data feeds that the manager cannot touch: bank feeds direct to owner, supplier statements direct from suppliers, buyer remittances, utility/fuel/electricity meter reads, vet invoices direct from vet, gate/weighbridge records, cameras recorded to cloud under owner's account. Cross-check dashboard to independent feeds quarterly (cash in vs reported sales; feed purchased vs fed).

9. [MAJOR] Key-person, quit, and collusion scenarios are not designed; "deputy + handover" is a sentence.
   - Problem: No scenario for: manager quits suddenly (often with a rival or taking a customer list); manager is arrested/ill/dies; manager and unit lead collude; manager colludes with the bookkeeper, vet, buyer or supplier; manager takes the herd records or passwords; deputy is the manager's relative.
   - Why it matters: In an absentee model the owner cannot respond within days; knowledge sits in one head and one phone. Collusion defeats any control that depends on two internal people.
   - Fix: (a) Contract: notice 90 days, garden leave, non-solicit/confidentiality, return of records and credentials, assignment of work product, clawback of deferred bonus on misconduct. (b) Credentials: all system, bank, supplier, cloud-camera, and domain accounts owned by the owner or an entity he controls; password manager with owner recovery; shared admin removed on exit within an hour; keys/safes/gate codes re-keyed on exit. (c) Deputy and a second named successor both trained; deputy must not be related to the manager (relationship declaration). (d) Collusion-resistant controls: at least one control owner who is outside the farm hierarchy (external bookkeeper, external vet, auditor, owner's trusted local nominee), plus an anonymous whistle-blower line to the owner or a third-party hotline with a written no-retaliation rule and a reward for verified reports. (e) A "first 72 hours" playbook for sudden manager exit: who takes bank control, counts stock, secures stores, informs staff.

10. [MAJOR] No owner-side oversight capacity: "~1 hr/week" is a one-point-of-failure on the owner, and the owner is also the only escalation target.
   - Problem: The principal is a busy academic with no stated backup. Plan has no owner's representative, no independent board, no cover when the owner is unreachable (field season, conference, illness, incapacity, death). Escalation "call the owner" has no timeout rule.
   - Why it matters: The manager can learn the owner's attention pattern and act when it is lowest. If an emergency escalation goes unanswered (disease outbreak at night, theft), staff either do nothing or improvise outside authority with no cover. Owner incapacity freezes all payments if he is the sole bank signatory.
   - Fix: Name an owner's representative (trusted family member, farm-adviser, or paid independent "farm steward" / non-executive adviser) with delegated review rights and a second bank mandate; escalation ladder with response-time deadlines and automatic fall-through (owner -> representative -> vet/adviser authorised to approve emergency spend up to a cap). Pre-authorised emergency spending envelope (e.g. disease, water failure) so that safety is not blocked on the owner. Power of attorney and a standing letter of instruction for owner incapacity. Block-out the owner's real unannounced field visits (two to four a year, random timing, including night/weekend checks) as a deliberate control; the plan's "owner does no daily work" must not become "owner never looks."

11. [MAJOR] Insurance and legal structure are one line ("insurance" under Finance) and the entity is not addressed.
   - Problem: No decision on legal form (sole trader vs company/LLC/trust vs cooperative); who owns land, animals, equipment; who is the employer; who holds licences; who bears liability for worker injury, zoonosis, food-safety recall, environmental pollution or neighbour/trespass claims. No insurance specification (livestock mortality, crop/weather, property, fidelity/crime, employer's liability, public liability, motor, business interruption), and no mention that insurers require tag/ID and documented husbandry to pay claims.
   - Why it matters: Personal exposure of the owner's other assets; employment disputes with a dismissed manager (unfair-dismissal claims, wage claims); uninsured theft or collusion; claims denied for record gaps. Livestock mortality cover is also a fraud vector (staged deaths, tag swapping) if the claim process is run by the manager.
   - Fix: Take legal advice on putting the farm in a separate entity with owner as director/shareholder and manager as employee (never director or signatory); written employment contracts and labour-law compliance (registration, social insurance, minimum wage, working-time, housing); fidelity/crime (employee dishonesty) cover with the manager and bookkeeper bonded; liability, property, equipment, livestock/crop cover as available in the jurisdiction; claims reported and settled only by the owner or representative with vet/authority corroboration. Keep an asset register with title documents and keys held by owner. Review annually.

12. [MAJOR] Payroll and labour: ghost workers, inflated hours, padded wages, and cash-wage fraud are unaddressed.
   - Problem: "Rosters" and "workers run from daily task sheets" only. No headcount control, no ID verification, no attendance verification independent of the supervisor, no payment channel rule (cash vs mobile money), no wage-rate approval, no control over casual/seasonal labour (the favourite ghost channel at harvest).
   - Why it matters: Payroll is typically the largest or second-largest cost; a supervisor-padded muster costs a farm silently every month; casual labour is paid in cash against self-certified day counts.
   - Fix: Employee master file maintained by the external bookkeeper, with national ID/copy and photo, and changes approved by owner (new hire, rate change, termination); pay only to the employee's own account/wallet, no cash and no pay-through-supervisor; attendance by a method not controlled by the supervisor (biometric/phone check-in with geotag, or unannounced muster by the auditor against the payroll); casual labour needs signed day sheets countersigned by a second person and reviewed against tasks and output (e.g. hectares weeded); annual headcount-vs-payroll match; unclaimed wages report; statutory deductions evidenced.

## MINOR

13. [MINOR] Procurement is governed by "preferred suppliers", which is a kickback pipeline unless competed. Fix: three quotes above a threshold, annual re-tender of feed, drugs, seed and fuel, supplier-conflict declarations signed yearly, price benchmarking against an external index or a second farm's invoices, and owner sign-off on the preferred-supplier list.

14. [MINOR] Sales and marketing: the manager sets prices and chooses buyers. Fix: published minimum price list and discount authority limits; sales contracts above threshold owner-signed; credit sales require owner approval and ageing review; buyer remittance direct to bank; periodic mystery-buyer or price call-back checks; weigh-out reconciled to buyer's weigh-in.

15. [MINOR] Vet, agronomist and security retainers are control assets and also conflict risks: the vet who certifies deaths and sells drugs profits from both. Fix: separate prescribing from dispensing where possible; vet invoices go direct to bookkeeper and owner; security guards report to someone other than the manager (or at least a second reporting line), gate log reviewed weekly.

16. [MINOR] Fixed assets and fuel: no asset register, no tool/equipment check-out, no fuel log vs engine hours/distance. Fix: tagged asset register with photos and serials, annual physical verification, vehicle logbooks with GPS tracking, bulk fuel dip reconciliation, bowser lock, spare-parts store with issue slips, and no personal use without written policy.

17. [MINOR] Crops: input-use and yield fraud (fertiliser/agrochemical diverted and resold, harvest weights understated, post-harvest storage losses). Fix: application records tied to area x rate and reconciled to purchases; yield expectations per hectare with variance triggers; grain store counted at harvest and at each dispatch; weighbridge tickets retained.

18. [MINOR] Dashboard thresholds are described as "mortality spike", "disease signal" with no numbers. Fix: set numeric amber/red bands per enterprise, trend-based (rolling 4-week) as well as absolute, plus "no data received" alerts (silence is itself an exception, so a missing daily record must alert, not pass quietly).

19. [MINOR] Whistleblowing, disciplinary and ethics are mentioned only as "disciplinary/escalation ladder". Fix: written code of conduct signed by all staff, gift and conflict-of-interest register, anonymous channel, investigations run by someone not in the line, defined consequence for falsifying records (summary dismissal and prosecution policy applied consistently, including for a favoured long-serving employee).

20. [MINOR] Recruitment and vetting of the manager is not in the plan, though it is the single largest risk decision. Fix: reference checks with prior employers (and not only those the candidate nominates), criminal and credit/judgment checks as lawful locally, verification of qualifications, lifestyle red-flag monitoring (unexplained wealth, resisting leave, resisting delegation or documentation, defensiveness at audit), and a probation period with heightened checks. Family or tribal ties to staff and suppliers declared.

21. [MINOR] Phase 4 "hands-off" at month 6 is unearned; there are no exit criteria. Fix: a gate based on evidence (two consecutive clean unannounced audits, reconciliation variances within tolerance, independent cross-checks matching the dashboard, deputy tested through a real leave period) before the owner reduces oversight; and define what minimum oversight remains permanently.

22. [MINOR] Controls sit in a manual that the same team will write the day-to-day interpretation of; no review of the controls themselves. Fix: annual control self-assessment and independent review of the control set; any override of a control logged and reported to the owner; the risk register must include fraud risks with named owners.

23. [MINOR] Succession (Section 3.10) is listed but unspecified at the ownership level: what happens to the farm on the owner's death/incapacity, and for the manager's key-person knowledge. Fix: owner's will/trust/entity succession documented, named executor/trustee briefed, and a one-page "if I am gone" file in escrow with the representative; manager knowledge captured as living SOPs plus recorded walk-throughs, refreshed twice a year.

24. [MINOR] Records and evidence retention, privacy and surveillance legality (cameras on workers, biometric data) are unaddressed. Fix: confirm local employment/privacy law, post notices, define retention periods (financial 7 years or as local law requires; animal and drug records per regulatory minimum), keep off-site backups under owner control.
