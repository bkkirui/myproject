Word count: 4,413 (wc -w). Change log alone is 906 words (20%); Section 6 (species/poultry/medicines/crops) 578; Sections 5 about 980.

## (a) Were R1 MAJORs genuinely fixed?
Largely yes in structure: gates with exit criteria (R1-6), Gate 0 intake and baseline audit (R1-7), shared model first (R1-9), sign-off matrix (R1-9), staged autonomy on a full cycle (R1-5), time budget by activity with crisis reserve and logged metric (R1-3), manager recruitment from week 1 (R1-4), tiered audit (R1-10), stress-tested cash flow (R1-11), labour model (R1-12), tech last with GSM exception (R1-8; the justification is sound). Partly fixed, with defects below: R1-1 (overhead is capped but the cap is ill-defined and the hurdle is weak), R1-3 (the budget does not add up), R1-4/R1-5 (hire lead time versus gate timeline). Not re-raised: poultry, enterprise count, GSM alarms, 12-month minimum, the rejected "start with one" (reasoning accepted).

## Findings

1. [MAJOR] The memo does not tell the owner what he is approving, what it costs, or when he can stop.
   Problem: The title says "for owner approval", but there is no summary, no recommendation, no ask. The first 1,000 words are principles and an intake table. Nothing states: approve what (the whole programme, or only Gate 0?), the cash cost of getting to the G1 decision (baseline audit, lawyer, accountant, a consultant vet/stockman, bookkeeper set-up, recruitment search), the owner's hours to G1, or the expected elapsed time to L3 autonomy. 906 words of change log (a reply to reviewers, not to the owner) and about 800 words of species-level and threshold detail (S6, S7) sit inside the decision document, while Principle 3 itself says no SOPs before G1.
   Why it matters: A busy economist cannot approve or reject. The natural, correct decision is "fund Gate 0 only, with a cap, and decide at G1", but the document never frames it that way, so the owner must reverse-engineer the commitment. Sunk pre-G1 spend is also not in the kill triggers (they start after G1).
   Fix: Add a half-page front sheet: (i) recommendation: approve Gate 0 and G1 only; (ii) a capped pre-G1 budget (a range, to be quoted by named professionals) and owner hours; (iii) what G1 will decide and the three outcomes (proceed / restructure to lease or contract / exit); (iv) the earliest realistic date of L3 (see item 4); (v) the five owner actions needed this month (answer intake, name a representative candidate, engage the lawyer, authorise the audit, confirm hours). Move the change log to a separate file (keep one line pointing to it), and move S6, S7 and the S5 detail to an appendix labelled "G1/G2 design defaults, not for approval now". Target: body under about 2,000 words.

2. [MAJOR] The owner time budget is internally inconsistent and understated by roughly a factor of four.
   Problem: The components in S8 sum to routine 78–104 h/yr (1.5–2 h × 52), monthly 36, quarterly about 32 (4 × 1 day), crisis reserve 64–96 (8–12 days) and year-1 site presence about 120–160 (3–4 weeks): roughly 330–430 h/yr, i.e. 6.5–8.5 h/week averaged, in year 1. The headline "1.5–2 h/week" is only the routine line, and it is the number the owner will remember. It also excludes: Gate 0–2 effort (intake, G1 sign-off, lawyer, hiring interviews and offers, appointing the auditor and bookkeeper, bank mandates); and the approval queue. At L1 the owner approves all T2+ items, reviews weekly, and releases every non-whitelisted payment. Thresholds make that queue large: at an AOB of, say, $300k, T1 is $900 per item, so a single feed load, a vet visit with a procedure, or any repair is T2; contracts of 12 months or more are T3 (so annual feed or lease contracts fall to the owner); any budget-line overrun is T2. The escalation ladder gives the owner 30 minutes in daytime, which collides with teaching and meetings. The kill trigger "owner hours exceed budget by 50% for a quarter" cannot work because the budget is not a single number and a crisis reserve is built in.
   Why it matters: The time budget is the binding constraint of the plan (the plan says so by making it a gate metric and a kill trigger). If the owner approves believing 2 h/week and experiences 7, the plan fails by its own rule within the first year, and probably by dropping the controls that make absence safe.
   Fix: Restate one total: "year 1 about X h/yr (about Y h/week averaged), falling to Z at L3", by phase (G0–G2, pilot, L1, L2, L3). Add the approval queue explicitly: estimate item counts per week from the last 2–3 years of purchase records at Gate 0, and set T1/T2 limits so that expected T2 items fit about 30–45 min/week; use pre-approved annual supply agreements with price bands for feed/drugs/fuel. Define the kill trigger against the total (excluding declared crisis days). Replace the 30-minute daytime clock with "next scheduled slot, with the representative acting after 2 h" unless the owner confirms he can answer.

3. [MAJOR] The overhead cap is undefined and the control burden is not scaled to farm size.
   Problem: G1(c) caps "controls and oversight cost" at about 8–10% of gross revenue. It is not stated what is inside. Candidates: bookkeeper, 6–12 unannounced spot-counts a year, annual licensed-accountant review with confirmations, owner's representative or paid steward with a second mandate, third-party hotline, fidelity insurance, GSM alarms, consultant stockman for a year, legal and entity work, geotag/muster. Is the manager or deputy in or out? G1(b) separately tests "net margin after full management and control cost", so the cap and the margin test partly duplicate each other while leaving the key question open: what is the minimum control set that is still safe at, say, $150k, $500k and $1.5m revenue? S5 repeats "scaled to size" many times but gives no scale bands. R1's estimate was 15–30% for the whole overhead stack; a cap on only part of it at 8–10% could pass while total overhead is unaffordable, or fail every farm below about $500k.
   Why it matters: The control layer is the largest new cost the plan introduces, and its economics is the make-or-break at small/medium scale. Controls past the point where expected leakage prevented exceeds their cost are waste (a $20k steward to guard a $30k/yr theft exposure). The plan has no expected-loss logic: exposure by leakage channel versus cost of each control.
   Fix: (i) Define the cap: numerator = every line in S5 (list them), denominator = base-case gross revenue; separately report manager and deputy pay as "management cost". (ii) Provide a three-band minimum control set (small / medium / larger by revenue or head count), each with a unit price from Gate 0 quotes. At the small band, make the cheap high-yield controls mandatory (direct bank feeds, owner-held mandate, tag counts, dual signature on stock movements, quarterly unannounced count) and the rest optional. (iii) At G1 rank controls by estimated annual leakage prevented per cost and cut those below break-even, with the residual risk signed by the owner.

4. [MAJOR] The gate timeline is not achievable in the stated order, because the manager arrives after the gates that need him; the elapsed time to autonomy is never stated.
   Problem: Recruitment lead time is 3–6 months (S10); the offer waits for G1 (week 6); so a manager starts around month 5–8 at the earliest. But G2 (weeks 6–16) requires SOPs "completed from the sheet alone by a worker who did not write it", manager and worker field tests (S9), and a tabletop of "manager quits"; G3 and the L1→L2 step need him in post. Entity set-up and insurance need 2–3 months, which is also inside G2 and ahead of a safe hire. Who runs the farm and signs between week 0 and the manager's start is not stated (S10 says only "the incumbent manager" if there is one). Season clash: if G2 ends about week 16 (early Feb 2027) and G3 needs "in season", the pilot lands in the N. temperate birthing/planting peak (Mar–May), in which S10 forbids new launches and which is the worst moment for a new manager's first 8 weeks. G1 itself (three weeks, including five-scenario budgets for each enterprise, back-testing on 2–3 years of accounts that may not exist, and an options appraisal) is optimistic. Adding G3 (at least 8 weeks) plus G4 (at least 12 months, extended until a leave period has occurred), the earliest L3 is about 22–30 months from now; the memo never says this.
   Why it matters: The owner is likely to read "wk 0–16" as the project and plan his own sabbatical or grant calendar around it. Gate exit criteria that cannot be met in sequence will either be waived (undermining evidence-gated autonomy) or silently slip.
   Fix: Replace the gate table's "When" column with a single dated critical-path line for each season column, showing recruitment start, offer at G1, start date, G2 tests that need the manager moved to a "G2b (with manager, weeks of employment 1–8)", interim cover (consultant manager or the incumbent under a short contract) with named authority, and the earliest L3 date. State gate durations as earliest-possible, not committed. Put "pilot window must not start inside a birthing/planting/harvest peak unless the incumbent runs it" into G3.

5. [MAJOR] The G1 viability test accepts a farm that earns zero, and does not compare against the alternatives it requires.
   Problem: G1(b) passes if net margin after management and control cost is >= 0 in the base case. G1(e) requires an options appraisal "done", with no rule that the self-run option must beat lease, share-farming, contract grazing or sale. Break-even margin ignores the return on land and capital tied up and the owner's own time. A farm that yields 0 after all costs, but could be leased for a positive net rent with near-zero owner hours, "passes". Also, margin >= 0 in base only (stress cases need only a cash floor) means a plan with zero cushion is approved.
   Why it matters: This is the plan's single go/no-go decision rule. R1-1 and R1-13 asked for exactly this comparison; v2 added the checkbox, not the rule.
   Fix: Replace (b) with: base-case net margin after management and control cost exceeds the best alternative's net income (lease or contract) by a stated risk premium (default: at least the lease income plus 3–5% of invested capital, owner to set), and the stress case loses no more than the cumulative-loss limit signed in (f). If it fails, the default recommendation is the best-ranked alternative, with self-run as a reasoned exception.

6. [MINOR] Professional costs and the coordination of ten-plus external roles are not costed or owned.
   Problem: Vet, agronomist, lawyer, accountant, broker, baseline-audit consultant, bookkeeper, auditor, rotating spot-counter, representative, consultant stockman and hotline operator all appear, but nobody is named to brief and manage them, and S9 makes each a signatory. Agent-drafted SOPs have to be paid for as professional review time.
   Why it matters: In practice the owner becomes the project manager, and the time in item 2 is understated again.
   Fix: At G0, name one coordinator (a consultant farm manager or the representative) and give a fee range per role in the front sheet.

7. [MINOR] The control regime may be incompatible with the manager the plan wants to hire, and the incentive pool is small relative to what it must offset.
   Problem: Employee only, no signatory, no buyer price contact, 90-day notice, deferred bonus with clawback, geotag attendance, owner-appointed auditor reporting past him. A capable operator-type manager may decline, or price the distrust into the wage; the options list includes manager-operator with equity, but S5 is written only for an employee. A 50%-deferred bonus on a small farm may be a few thousand dollars, against a theft opportunity far larger.
   Fix: At G1, test wage expectations from 3–5 candidates against the control set before finalising it; give a variant of the charter for an equity/profit-share operator; size the bonus pool so it is credible against the exposure estimated in item 3.

8. [MINOR] The owner's representative is an unmanaged single point of trust.
   Problem: Principle 5 says anyone can be dishonest, yet the representative gets a second bank mandate, claims authority, and receives the "if I am gone" file. There is no vetting, cost, fidelity cover, or check on them (and a paid steward is a cost inside or outside the cap?).
   Fix: Same vetting and fidelity cover as the manager; the representative cannot approve payments to related parties; bank statements go to the owner too; include in the cap definition.

9. [MINOR] Gate numbers contradict each other.
   Problem: G3 requires stock within ±2% of independent count; S7 and S5 set the variance alert at 3–5% (amber/red). G3 requires "two unannounced audits clean" within 8 weeks, but S5 plans 6–12 spot-counts a year (about 1–2 expected in 8 weeks) and "clean" is not defined. The whitelist lets recurring supplier payments release on one approval, which is exactly the collusion channel; audit scope should say it targets whitelisted suppliers.
   Fix: Define "clean" (variance within ±2% and no unreported incident). Increase spot-count frequency during the pilot (cost it) or state G3 needs two. Add whitelist price benchmarking to the audit scope.

10. [MINOR] Readability and labelling.
    Problem: Letter collisions: G = gate, G = governance finding in the change log, L = autonomy level and livestock finding, T = tier, P = poultry finding; "S5", "WP6" and "AOB" are used without introduction in places. S3's staged-autonomy paragraph is one 170-word block. Section 10 needs a one-line "pick your column" instruction at the top of the memo, not only at S10. Principle 3 ("no SOPs until G1") is contradicted by the amount of SOP-level specification in S6.
    Fix: Rename autonomy levels A1–A3 and change-log codes; split the staged-autonomy paragraph into a three-row table; add a note to S6/S7 headed "design defaults to be calibrated at G1/G2".

11. [MINOR] The year-1 site presence was cut from R1's 4–8 weeks to 3–4 "to fit an academic's calendar" with no evidence supporting the new number.
    Problem: The rationale is convenience; no mechanism replaces the lost presence, apart from unannounced visits that begin after year 1.
    Fix: Keep 3–4 weeks as a default only if the front-loaded presence (G1 sign-off, manager's first 8 weeks, first stock count and first audit) is scheduled. Otherwise the representative must be present for those events and the plan says so.

## (b) Consistency summary
Consistent: gates versus kill triggers versus staged autonomy; authority tiers versus escalation ladder (owner 30 min, representative 2 h, emergency at 2 h); alerts versus silence rule; the poultry and biosecurity chain; change-log mapping (spot-checked E1, E3, E4, E5, E8, G2: accurate, including the stated modifications). Inconsistent: items 2, 4, 9 above.

## (c) Is the oversight burden sensible?
In direction, yes: the loss-leakage channels are the right ones, and the whitelist batch and direct bank feeds are cheap, high-yield controls. In size, unproven: the plan needs an expected-loss test per control (item 3), and the owner time cost of the approval workload is the hidden price (item 2). Proportionality by farm size is asserted, not designed.

## (d) Usability
Fails as a decision memo (item 1); strong as a design reference. A reader can find the Gate 0 questions, but cannot see the cost, the ask, or the date.
