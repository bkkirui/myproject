# R2 Governance / Internal-Control / Employment-Privacy Review of PLAN.md (v2)

## MAJOR

1. [MAJOR] The owner's representative is a new single point of capture, and the dual-mandate structure is unspecified.
   - Problem: Section 5 gives the representative review rights, a "second bank mandate", T2 approval power, the whitelist-batch approval, and escalation authority. Nothing says whether the mandate is "either to sign" or "both to sign", whether the representative may release alone, what cap applies, who vets or monitors the representative, or what stops a paid steward and the manager from colluding. A "trusted person" is likely to be a relative or friend of the owner, or a local who knows the manager.
   - Why it matters: R1-10 was fixed by adding a person with full payment power and no counter-control. If either-to-sign applies, the control set is only as strong as its weakest approver, and the owner is no longer in the loop for most payments. The representative also sits in the emergency fall-through (2 h silence), so the manager can time requests for when the owner is unreachable and the representative is the only approver.
   - Fix: (a) Representative may release alone only up to a low cap (default: T1 per item and a monthly total); anything above, any T3 item and any bank-detail change needs the owner, with the representative as a second signature when the owner is incapacitated (POA route). (b) The bank sends every release over a threshold, and every mandate change, to both mandate holders and the external accountant. (c) Representative signs the conflict declaration and code of conduct, is covered by fidelity cover, is vetted like the manager, and may not be related to the manager, deputy or any supplier. (d) The representative's own payments and approvals are reported in the quarterly cross-check to the owner. (e) If the representative is a paid steward, the contract is with the entity and has its own notice and independence terms (lawyer).

2. [MAJOR] The whitelist/weekly-batch relaxation opens a vendor-creation and rubber-stamp hole, and the preparer also reconciles.
   - Problem: Whitelisted "approved recurring suppliers within budget" pay on one batch approval. The plan does not say who can add a vendor to the whitelist or create a vendor record below the T2 "new supplier above threshold" line. Below that threshold the manager can introduce suppliers; once whitelisted, each pays up to the T1 per-supplier cap (1.5% AOB per 30 days) with no per-item owner review. Several shell vendors at that cap draw a material share of AOB. In addition, the external bookkeeper prepares payments, holds the employee master file and performs the monthly bank and supplier reconciliation, so one outside party prepares, edits master data and reconciles its own output. A batch of dozens of lines approved in minutes is not review.
   - Why it matters: This is the textbook trade-off between time budget and control; the whitelist turns the owner's single check into a formality, and the audit tier (6-12 spot counts) will rarely catch a plausible invoice from a real-looking vendor.
   - Fix: (a) All vendor creation and whitelist entry is T3 (owner), with business-registration or ID check, bank account in the vendor's own name, and a relationship declaration; the manager cannot request a vendor into the whitelist without a 3-quote record. (b) Whitelist entries carry a per-payee monthly cap and a fixed purpose; anything over cap, any new payee, any changed amount (+10% on prior period) or bank detail change drops out of the batch into per-item approval. (c) The batch approval screen is an exception report (new, changed, over cap, match failed, unmatched GRN) not a list; payroll is tied to the master file and muster headcount. (d) Reconciliation is performed by a person other than the payment preparer (e.g. the annual-audit accountant quarterly, or a second bookkeeper), and master-data change logs go to the owner directly. (e) The bank daily payment limit equals the largest plausible batch.

3. [MAJOR] Section 8 "paper tally sheets, weekly photo/SMS upload and a manager-compiled report" contradicts Section 5 data integrity.
   - Problem: Section 5 requires append-only logs with user, timestamp and photo/GPS, and says the manager cannot be the sole source of dashboard data. Section 8 starts with paper and a manager-compiled weekly report, which is precisely a self-reported narrative with no append-only property and edits before submission. The G3 silence alert depends on a "daily mortality form" with no mechanism specified while the plan also says tech is last.
   - Why it matters: During L1/pilot, when the evidence base for gating autonomy is built, the only data are the manager's. Photos can be reused (same carcass, different tag), gallery uploads back-dated, and tallies rewritten before the photo.
   - Fix: Define a minimum evidence layer at L1 that is not tech-heavy: unit leads (not only the manager) photograph the same-day paper sheet and the tagged carcass through an in-app or messaging capture that the manager cannot edit, to an owner-owned store with server-side timestamps. The tag number must be legible in the photo; capture from gallery is disallowed; duplicate-image and metadata checks at audit. Manager's compiled report is secondary and reconciled to the raw submissions. State that G3 data-completeness is measured on raw submissions, not on the compiled report.

4. [MAJOR] The emergency envelope can be declared by the person who spends it, with no aggregate cap and a conflicted fallback approver.
   - Problem: The emergency tier allows the manager up to 1% AOB per event for disease, water, power or welfare, with the vet or deputy as the fall-through if the owner and representative are silent for 2 h. No cap on number of events per quarter or year; no post-event review; no restriction on payee; the vet who approves spend may be the vendor; the deputy reports to the manager. "Silent for 2 h" is easy to arrange (night, weekend, owner on a flight).
   - Why it matters: A captured manager can stage emergencies (water, "disease") and route spend to a favoured payee, repeatedly, at 1% each, outside the owner's approval. This is a standard override channel.
   - Fix: (a) Aggregate emergency cap (default 3% AOB per year, 1.5% per quarter) after which every event needs the owner or representative. (b) The approver for fall-through must not be the payee; vet cannot authorise spend on their own invoices; deputy approval counts only when the owner and representative were both contacted by logged call/SMS. (c) Payment of emergency spend by existing whitelisted vendors or by the owner-released batch/direct bank transfer, not cash, with a photo and a short incident note. (d) Every emergency event reviewed by the owner or representative within 72 h and reported in the quarterly summary; a repeating emergency category triggers an audit check.

5. [MAJOR] There is no detect-to-response (fraud and misconduct) protocol; the plan stops at alerts, spot-counts and kill triggers.
   - Problem: Variance triggers (3-5%), failed spot-counts and whistle-blower reports raise alerts, but nothing says who decides, within what time, how evidence is preserved, whether the manager is suspended, when police and the insurer are informed, or how dismissal is handled lawfully. The only response ladder is the 72-hour manager-exit playbook (assumes a quitter), and the kill trigger needs "two failed audits". "Investigations by someone outside the line" is a single clause.
   - Why it matters: Late notice can void fidelity/crime cover (policies have notification and discovery-period conditions); evidence on phones and paper is lost in days; a hasty summary dismissal or suspension without process is an employment-law exposure that turns the victim into the defendant; informing the manager before securing records gives time to destroy them.
   - Fix: Add a one-page response protocol, signed off by the lawyer and insurer: trigger list; who leads (owner or representative, never the line); first 24 h actions (secure bank access, credentials, records, store and cameras; imaging of devices; interview protocol; suspension on pay per local law); insurer and, where required, authority notification deadlines; criteria for police referral; no-retaliation for the reporter; decision on dismissal after a documented hearing. Make a single failed spot-count or unexplained variance over red a mandatory protocol start, not a kill trigger.

## MINOR

6. [MINOR] Scaled controls have no non-waivable core. G1(c) caps controls at 8-10% of revenue, and the plan layers bookkeeper, auditor, representative, hotline, fidelity cover, consultant backstop, vet-of-record and alarm units. At small scale something must go, and the plan lets the manager-favouring items go first. Fix: list a core that is never dropped whatever the cost test says (owner/representative-held bank mandates, bank and supplier feeds to the owner, external bookkeeper, randomised spot-counts at a reduced frequency, annual accountant review) and state that if the core does not fit the cap, the answer is scale down or lease, not to drop the core.

7. [MINOR] Compulsory consecutive leave (10 working days) for manager and bookkeeper, and unit-lead rotation, appear only in the change log. They need to be in the Section 5 charter text and in the G4 criteria as stated ("a real manager leave period") with a defined review of the books during it.

8. [MINOR] Privacy and labour-law gating is applied only to cameras and biometrics. Other monitoring defaults are stated unconditionally: geotagged check-in and GPS on records (location data of employees), vehicle tracking, ID copies and photos held by an outside bookkeeper (cross-border or processor terms, retention, access), the anonymous hotline (its own personal-data and reporter-protection rules), background checks and lifestyle red-flag checks, and data stores owned by the owner personally rather than the employing entity (the controller is the employer/entity). Fix: extend the Section 9 privacy row to all of these; require a short data-protection assessment, staff notices in local language, consultation where required, processor agreements, retention schedule and access log, and make the entity the owner of data stores and accounts. Labour: the lawyer's list should also cover working-time and rest rules for night watch and 7-day cover, lawful deductions (bonus clawback, damage recovery), right-to-work and age checks for casual and seasonal labour and any labour contractor, and the position of unbanked workers under the "own account/wallet" rule.

9. [MINOR] Death-evidence and gate controls rely on parties the plan does not make independent. Carcass photos with legible tag and uniqueness checks (see item 3); gate records are listed as an independent feed "the manager cannot touch" yet no one is named who produces them (guards typically report to the manager). Fix: guards or gate log reporting line to the representative or security contractor; weekly log copy to the owner's store.

10. [MINOR] Small-staff segregation has no compensating control. "Receiver is a different person" fails on a farm of 3-6 people where the unit lead is also the requester and the only other person. Fix: state compensating controls (supplier delivery note signed by the driver, weighbridge or calibrated bag count photographed, owner or representative spot-check of deliveries, auditor sampling of GRNs).

11. [MINOR] Unannounced spot-counts conflict with the biosecurity design: rotating auditors visit multiple units. Fix: auditors follow the visitor protocol (clean to dirty, poultry last or separate visits, boots and clothing, standstill compliance) and the audit charter grants access to premises, records and staff interviews without manager consent.

12. [MINOR] Owner credential and impersonation risk. The control set rests on the owner's banking app and email. Fix: hardware or app MFA, a bank-side daily release limit, no approvals from messages or links, and a rule that payment instructions from "the owner" received by the bookkeeper are void unless in the banking system or confirmed by call-back.

VERDICT: 5 MAJOR remaining
