# R3 Adversarial Review: Economics / Management (PLAN.md v3 and appendices)

Reviewer lens: farm business economist and consultant, judging v3 as a decision memo for a busy senior academic economist. Format: numbered; Problem / Why / Fix. Word count checked: PLAN.md 2,144 words; Appendix A 1,390; Appendix B 1,560; CHANGELOG 1,810.

## (a) Were the R2 MAJORs genuinely fixed?

- **R2-E1 decision sheet: mostly fixed, one hole.** The sheet now says what is approved (G0+G1), when he can stop (four stop points), the outcomes and the dates, and it has five actions. It still gives no cost figure (item 3).
- **R2-E2 time budget: not fixed.** The table exists, but the headline total cannot be derived from it (item 1).
- **R2-E3 overhead cap: fixed.** B10 defines the numerator and denominator, reports management cost separately, gives three bands, a non-waivable core and an expected-loss test. Residual issues are MINOR (items 6, 7).
- **R2-E4 timeline: fixed in structure.** The path is dated and the manager arrives after G1. One date claim contradicts its own table (item 5).
- **R2-E5 hurdle: fixed in structure, with a real residual gap.** The hurdle now compares against the alternatives with a risk premium, but it leaves out the owner's time and the transition cost (item 4).

Word count: R2 asked for about 2,000 words or fewer. The file is 2,144, 7% over, and the CHANGELOG says so. It is acceptable. The front sheet alone is about 420 words and is readable on one page.

## (b) Findings

1. [MAJOR] The headline owner-time total ("~400-450 h in year 1, ~8 h/week") cannot be reproduced from the section 5 table that claims to reconcile it.
   Problem: The rows are G0-G1 20-30 h, G2/G2b 40-60 h, pilot 3-4 h/week plus ~70 h presence, A2 2-3 h/week. Take year 1 as Oct 2026 to Oct 2027. The critical path puts G1 in Dec-Jan, manager start Apr-Jun, G2b for 8 weeks, then at least 8 more weeks before the pilot. The pilot (A1) therefore occupies a few weeks at most of that year. Even a pilot running all 52 weeks at 4 h gives 25+60+208+70 = about 360 h, below the quoted range. A realistic path gives roughly 250-300 h. If "year 1" means the 12 months from manager start, the 8-week G2b gap, the 8-week wait before the pilot, and the weeks between G3 and A2 have no row. The 64 h crisis reserve is shown only at A3, although year 1 is when the manager is least proven. The sheet says the budget is "reconciled", but the total is neither derived nor labelled with its year.
   Why: Owner hours are the central cost the owner is signing up for, and the 150% kill trigger is pegged to these phase totals. A figure that cannot be reproduced undermines both. The error may run either way, because the missing rows could add hours as well as remove them.
   Fix: Add a month-by-month (or phase-by-weeks) hours schedule from Oct 2026 to the first A3 quarter, with a stated definition of "year 1". Include the interim period, a year-1 crisis reserve, and the representative's share. Derive the total from that schedule. Publish a range on the sheet with the year defined. Peg the kill trigger to the schedule.

2. [MAJOR] The plan never says plainly that the owner's original goal ("run without my involvement") has been replaced, and the sheet understates the commitment.
   Problem: The sheet states hours under the label "Honest owner time", but it does not say the goal is unattainable. Principle 1 says "management by exception, not absence". The steady state is about 5 h/week, which is about 6.5 working weeks a year at 40 h/week. It also includes 2-4 unannounced visits, quarterly alert-chain tests, owner-held mandates and permanent MFA approvals, and the owner can never fully disengage. The earliest A3 is Q3 2028 (plan Q1 2029), and it needs a stated commitment of about 2.5 years. The memo does not state the tension, and it does not say that the hours do not fall to zero.
   Why: The decision-maker should be able to see in one line that the plan trades his goal for a smaller time commitment, and what the cheapest alternative is. The lease or management-company route is the real answer to "I want no involvement", and it is mentioned only as a fallback if the budget is infeasible.
   Fix: Add one sentence to the sheet: "Your original goal (no involvement) is not achievable under self-run or managed operation. The floor is about 5 h/week and about 2.5 years to reach it. The only near-zero-hours options are lease or contract grazing, and G1 compares them honestly." Also state the minimum hours of the lease and management-company options, so the comparison is on the same basis.

3. [MAJOR] The sheet gives no cost figure, and the spend-cap rule is incoherent and probably too small.
   Problem: "The lesser of the quoted total and ~1.5% of last year's gross revenue" means the cap can never exceed the quote, so as written it is not a cap. If it means "1.5% unless the quote is lower", the arithmetic fails. The pre-G1 list is a vet-led baseline audit, lawyer, accountant, agronomist memo, interim lead (retention or contract manager), coordinator and manager search. For a farm grossing 500k, 1.5% is 7.5k. A lawyer for entity and contracts, an interim lead for 3 months, a search fee and a vet-led audit plausibly total 25-50k (5-10% of revenue). Stop point (3), "spend reaches cap", would then fire before G1 and send the owner back to re-approve. The owner also gets no figure for the steady-state overhead: B10 gives a 7-10% control cap, plus manager and deputy pay, plus the coordinator, none of which is on the sheet. R2-E1 asked for "what it costs"; this part is still open.
   Why: He cannot approve a cap he cannot judge, and the first stop point is likely to be a re-approval loop rather than a genuine stop. He also cannot see the order of magnitude of the programme he is deciding whether to start, so G1 becomes a sunk-cost decision.
   Fix: Fix the rule wording ("cap = X% of revenue, quotes above it return to you") and show an illustrative worked figure per scale band. Say which items sit inside the pre-G1 cap (move manager search and interim lead to post-G1, or price them separately). Add one line giving the steady-state overhead as a % of revenue (controls 7-10% plus management pay, with a note that a farm grossing under about 250k will likely fail the cap). The numbers can be defaults, labelled as such.

4. [MAJOR] The G1 hurdle omits the owner's time, the transition cost and the capital basis, so it can pass a self-run or managed farm that is worse than a lease.
   Problem: The hurdle compares "base-case net margin after management and control cost" with the best alternative's income plus a 3-5% risk premium "of invested capital". (a) The owner's 260+ h/yr (item 2) is priced at zero, while the lease alternative needs almost none. (b) "Invested capital" is undefined: land at market value, or only working capital and stock. (c) The comparison is steady-state, but the one-off costs (entity, legal, SOP writing, tools, interim lead, the consultant stockman, the pre-G1 spend, 12-24 months of low-productivity ramp-up) are set aside, even though the 24-month cash flow exists in the Farm Model. (d) The hurdle is net margin, but the cap in B10 is a percentage of revenue; with typical net margins of 5-15% of revenue (R1), a 7-10% control cost plus manager pay can consume the whole margin before the owner is paid.
   Why: These are the decision-relevant omissions for an economist. The G1 outcome ("proceed") could be driven by an unpriced owner input and an unamortised start-up cost.
   Fix: Add to criterion 1: an owner-time shadow price (his own figure per hour, default declared) charged to the self-run and managed options; amortise one-off set-up costs over a stated horizon (3-5 years); define invested capital (default: market value of land, stock and machinery, matching the sale-proceeds alternative); compare like with like on the lease side.

5. [MINOR] The manager start "~Apr-Jun 2027, in a trough" contradicts the plan's own table and rule.
   Problem: Section 9 puts Mar-May as a peak in both hemispheres (N calving/lambing and planting; S harvest and autumn breeding). The stated troughs are N Jun-Aug or Nov-Jan and S Jan-Feb or Jun-Jul. Only June fits, and "no manager start inside a peak" would rule out April and May. Section 3 also says an interim lead stays through any peak, so the contradiction is partly softened. A slip to Jul-Aug still works for N, but each slip pushes "earliest A3 Q3 2028" later.
   Fix: Put "June at earliest in either hemisphere, then next trough" on the sheet. Add a conditional sentence: "A3 needs 12 months from manager start; June start gives Q3 2028, otherwise add the slip."

6. [MINOR] Cap bands are anchored on revenue, not margin, and the lumpy core is hardest on the small band.
   Problem: A 7% cap on a farm with a 5-15% margin is a bar that is easy to pass. The bands give the smallest farm the lowest cap (7%) and the largest the highest (8-10%), though the core's fixed costs (bookkeeper, annual review, legal, fidelity, steward) are the most lumpy at small scale. "250k", "1m" are stated as a "currency-equivalent default" with no currency.
   Fix: State the cap also as a share of base-case gross margin (for example, controls <= a third of pre-management gross margin), name the reference currency, and add one sentence that the small band is expected to fail unless the owner's scale is above roughly 150-200k.

7. [MINOR] Pre-G1 hours and dates do not match: "20-30 h over ~8 weeks" against a plan-on G1 of 29 Jan 2027.
   Problem: 15 Oct to 17 Dec is about 9 weeks; to 29 Jan, about 15 weeks. The G1 pass requires measured inputs (soil, water, yields) that will not exist by December, so "conditional pass" is the likely G1 outcome; the plan acknowledges conditional passes but not that they are the base case.
   Fix: Say "~9-15 weeks" and note that a conditional G1 pass is the expected outcome for a first-year farm.

8. [MINOR] "Earliest autonomy level A3" is used on the sheet before it is defined.
   Fix: Add a short gloss: "A3 = quarterly owner review, manager runs routine operations."

9. [MINOR] Spot-count and visit counts do not align.
   Problem: Section 3 says "2-4 unannounced visits a year" at A3, while B6 sets spot-counts at 4+ (small band) or 6-12 a year. Section 5 budgets only the owner's 24-48 h of visits. It is not clear whether the owner or the auditor does the counts, or which of them the 24-48 h represents.
   Fix: One line saying who performs the spot-counts, and keep the owner's hours consistent with that.

10. [MINOR] Sections 6-9 carry design-level detail although Principle 1 says "nothing in the appendices is built until G1".
   Problem: The sheet is good, but the body (sections 6, 7, 8) is design reference, not decision content, and pushes the file to 2,144 words.
   Fix: Mark sections 6-9 "design defaults, for reference, not for approval now", as Appendix B already does.

## (c) Consistency check

- Gates, critical path and kill triggers are consistent, apart from item 5.
- Spend cap versus stop point (3): inconsistent in practice (item 3).
- Owner time: A3 arithmetic (routine 91 h + monthly 36 h + quarterly 32 h + visits 24-48 h + crisis 64 h = 247-271 h) agrees with "~260 h/yr, ~5 h/week". The year-1 total does not reproduce (item 1).
- Overhead cap bands in B10 agree with PLAN section 4 criterion 2 and with "core never dropped".
- G1 hurdle versus B10 cap: both must pass; no conflict, but the hurdle omits owner time (item 4).

## (d) Is the plan honest about the owner-time tension?

Partly. The hours are stated, and the plan says a lease or management company is favoured if the budget is infeasible. It does not say that the goal "run without my involvement" has been dropped, and it does not price the owner's time in the G1 comparison (items 2 and 4).

VERDICT: 4 MAJOR remaining
