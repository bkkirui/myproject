## MAJOR

1. [MAJOR] The B0.5 count schedule leaves a 5-9 month window with no count, in the period when the incumbent or interim lead runs the farm through a peak.
   - Problem: The opening count is about 22 Oct. The "6-8 week" count falls on 3-17 Dec. G1 is 17 Dec (plan 29 Jan), so counts 2 and 3 coincide (earliest case) or sit 6 weeks apart. The next count is the handover count at manager start: Jun-Jul 2027 (S), or Nov 2027 / covered Jun 2027 (N, interim lead through Oct). Fortnightly counts begin only in the pilot, after G2b, so not before late 2027. PLAN section 3 runs G2 for 18 weeks without any count. Appendix B6 sets a minimum of 4 a year but no start date, and PLAN says "controls until G2: B0". The B4 monthly signed stock reconciliation, B7 evidence layer and gate pass are also G2 or later. The window runs from Jan to Jun-Nov, so G1 to handover is 5-10 months.
   - Why it matters: R4 item 1 asked for recurring interim detection. As built, the stock, feed, fuel and drug channel is checked three times in the first 8-14 weeks and then not again until handover. The B0 sales rules and owner releases cover money out and recorded sales. They do not cover off-book sales or removal of live animals, grain, fuel or drugs. A loss in that window cannot be dated. The B0.1 variance test and the kill trigger (cumulative loss) then start from a stale baseline. The cost of the fix is small and the exposure is the farm's main asset.
   - Fix: Replace "again at G1" with a dated cadence. Keep count 2 at about 6-8 weeks, count 3 at G1, and add an unannounced count at least every 3 months until the B6 regime starts, plus the signed handover count. Also state the date the B6 minimum (4 a year) takes effect. Add a cheap monthly interim signal run by the coordinator: register roll-forward from births/deaths/sales photos against bank receipts.

## MINOR

2. [MINOR] The B0 "lawyer-cleared" gate collides with the PLAN dates.
   - Problem: B0 says count and access changes follow lawyer clearance on the same day. PLAN section 3 puts the Day-7 packs on 15-22 Oct and "lawyer clears incumbent handling and candidate notice" at G0 exit (~5 Nov). Either the Day-7 steps wait for 5 Nov (and "interim payment controls live by 29 Oct" slips), or they run before clearance. Neither the plan nor B0 states which, or what happens if the lawyer is not ready on day 7. The "not tipped off" aim is also weak: the count needs the incumbent to open gates and sign.
   - Fix: Define two clearances. A short Day-7 go (count, bank/statement changes on the owner's own accounts, credential inventory) is due before day 7. Full incumbent handling is due at G0 exit. If the short go is late, the owner-only steps (statement redirects, new-payee freeze, owner release above the float) proceed because they change nothing in the employee's terms. Role-changing steps wait. Add "refusal to sign the count is recorded by the counter and a witness".

3. [MINOR] Baseline findings have no route into B8 or the lawyer, and the exclusion test is loosely worded.
   - Problem: PLAN section 3 excludes the incumbent as interim lead on an "unexplained opening-count variance above 5%", a "related-party vendor" or a "ghost-payroll match". B8 triggers are a failed spot-count, a variance above red, a hotline report and a bank anomaly. A baseline finding is not listed, so the first-24-h secure step, insurer notice, hearing and dismissal rules do not formally apply. "Unexplained" is judged by the person under test. A phone or address match in a rural area (relatives, shared numbers) is a lead, not proof. A role change on a bare match is a constructive-dismissal risk.
   - Fix: One line in B0.3 and B8: a baseline finding above red or a confirmed match is a B8 trigger, led by the lawyer. "Explained" means documented by movement, sale or bank records and judged by the accountant and vet. A match requires independent confirmation before any role change. The role change preserves pay and is lawyer-worded.

4. [MINOR] No operational cover is named if the incumbent is excluded.
   - Problem: Contract-manager sourcing starts in week 1 with a 6-10 week lead time, so the earliest arrival is late Nov-late Dec. The exclusion decision is possible from early Nov. For 3-7 weeks nobody is named to run the farm. In the S hemisphere this is the lambing/calving peak. The B8 72-hour playbook is written for the manager, not the incumbent. The float and the A0.8 and A4 operator duties have no holder.
   - Fix: Name the bridge: the consultant stockman as acting lead with the unit lead, the float moved to them, the owner or representative on site for the first 1-2 weeks, and the 72-hour playbook applied. Price it in the "no incumbent" 1-3%.

5. [MINOR] "No disposal or sale without the owner's written OK" conflicts with welfare, biosecurity and perishable trade.
   - Problem: "Disposal" is undefined. Read literally it covers fallen stock and humane destruction, which A1/A3/B5 carve out of the cap and require without delay. It also covers daily egg and milk dispatch, routine culls and grain delivery in a harvest. A rule that cannot be followed will be ignored, which weakens the sales control.
   - Fix: Define it as sale, transfer, slaughter-for-sale and scrap of stock, produce in bulk, feed, machinery and fuel. Exclude fallen stock, welfare euthanasia and notifiable-disease measures (logged and reviewed in 72 h). Allow a standing owner-signed OK for named routine buyers within a price list, volume band and payment into the farm account. Add a monthly match of OK'd sales to bank receipts by the accountant, and tell buyers in writing to pay only into the farm account.

6. [MINOR] The float cannot be computed or lived with as written, and owner absence is not covered.
   - Problem: AOB (the base for 0.1% / 0.5% / 1% / 3%) is the annual operating budget, which does not exist until G1. Use trailing-12-month spend from the accounts and say so. At 0.1% per item the float is a few hundred units. Fuel, vet call-outs, spares and casual wages will exceed it, so most spend goes to the owner. The "30-45 min/week, >=90% inside T1" assumption in PLAN section 5 is for the manager's T1 (starting 0.3%/1.5%). In the interim the queue is larger, and every sale also needs an OK. The hours table (2.5 h/week G0 to G1) does not reflect that, and the 150% kill trigger could fire on it. If the owner is unreachable, feed and payroll stall. The interim representative is view-only, so the 48-h rule in B1/B2 has nobody to act. The emergency letter covers emergencies only.
   - Fix: Define AOB as trailing-12-month spend until G1. Set the interim float after a quick look at the last 12 months of purchase lines (the accountant's B0.5 calibration moved earlier). Re-estimate interim queue hours or raise the float for named recurring items (fuel, wages for casuals). Name the 14-day delegate on day 7 with bank-limited T2 powers (see item 10), and add a stalled-feed rule linked to the window memo's staged drought rows.

7. [MINOR] Payroll, new hires and new payees have gaps in the interim.
   - Problem: The payroll muster and the vendor/employee match are one-off baseline tests. A ghost employee added after the baseline is not a "new payee" under the freeze as worded, and no recurring muster is scheduled before G2. B0 does not say whether the B1 scheduled class (accountant-prepared schedule with a headcount and prior-period diff) applies now, so payroll is released by the owner with no discipline. The freeze has no exception route for legitimate new payees: the vet-of-record, agronomist, contract manager, harvest or lambing contractors, window-memo seed and fertiliser suppliers, the auditor. Vendor creation is T3, but B3's registry checks are not wired to the interim period.
   - Fix: Apply the scheduled-class rule to payroll from day 7. State that adding an employee counts as a new payee and needs owner approval with ID. Include payroll muster in each interim spot-count. Add one sentence: new payees are onboarded by the owner using the B3 vendor-creation checks done by the accountant, with a 5-working-day target.

8. [MINOR] Goods-received independence is weak in practice.
   - Problem: "Coordinator or unit lead" signs receipt. The coordinator is off-site, and the unit lead reports to the incumbent. The B3 small-staff compensating controls (driver-signed delivery note, bag count or weighbridge photo, representative spot-checks, auditor sampling) are not referenced in B0. The interim representative is view-only, so nobody visits.
   - Fix: Cross-refer B3 small-staff controls in B0.4. Require delivery-note photos to the coordinator, and a goods-received sample inside each interim spot-count.

9. [MINOR] The accountant concentrates roles in the interim, with no insurance condition, and role-map gaps allow it.
   - Problem: Under B0 the accountant counts stock, does registry lookups, co-releases payments with call-back, is the non-payee emergency approver, holds the payee log, receives all statements and (B0.5) does spot-counts. The PI/fidelity confirmation for the accountant is in B9, not a day-7 condition. The role map in B12 omits the accountant, the emergency approver, the co-releaser and the goods-received signer, so the "max three roles" test misses these. The existing bookkeeper (a preparer and possibly aligned with the incumbent) is not in the related-party tests. An unvetted interim representative gets view access to bank and payroll data before a conflict declaration or confidentiality undertaking.
   - Fix: Make PI/fidelity confirmation and engagement terms a precondition of co-release. Separate the roles of co-releaser and spot-counter where possible (auditor counts, accountant co-releases). Add the missing roles to B12. Include the existing bookkeeper and accountant in the B0.3 tests and the credential inventory. Require a signed conflict declaration and confidentiality undertaking before view rights.

10. [MINOR] Privacy, employment and incapacity wording to put in the lawyer brief.
    - Problem: (a) B0.3 matches employee bank, address and phone data against vendors and shares it with the accountant before staff notice or the B7 privacy row exists. (b) Revoking credentials and removing mandates on day 7 is a change in role terms. (c) B0.6 makes the personal POA the incapacity route until the entity exists and says the delegate has T2 powers only, but a POA is normally general and banks may not accept a limited one. B2 says the incapacity route is "not a personal power of attorney", which reads as a contradiction unless "after the entity exists" is added. B9 lists the POA without limits. Who holds the POA is not stated, and the representative may not be related to the manager or suppliers.
    - Fix: Add to the lawyer's day-7 brief: the lawful basis, processor terms and staff notice for the match; the role-change wording; a limited POA or bank-level delegate mandate with named holder and T2 cap. Reword B2 to "after the entity exists".

11. [MINOR] The scope and cost of interim counts are not stated.
    - Problem: B0.5 says "spot-count" but not what it covers (stock, feed, grain, fuel, drugs, payroll muster, sales vs bank, goods-received sample). It says "auditor or accountant" while the owner appoints the auditor only at G2 (B6), and the accountant is the control designer (R4-6 independence point). The decision sheet lists the opening count in the pre-G1 cost but not counts 2 and 3 (nor the extra quarterly counts from item 1). At the small band, where the cap is likely reached, the cost is unquantified.
    - Fix: Add one line defining interim count scope and the counter (auditor or a second individual under separate terms from the bookkeeper). Add count costs to the quoted lines beside lawyer and books review.
