**Stores claim.** "Stores last 6 months" means roughly 38-51 t of feed in stock. That is large, and complete mash keeps poorly in humid highland conditions (mould, aflatoxin, vitamin loss; typical best-before is weeks to a few months, UNVERIFIED). **Findings.**
- **Ruminants:** bulk forage supply 43-69 t DM against 43-61 t need. The farm can grow roughly 70-110% of bulk feed in a normal year, but only once stands mature (12-18 months; year 1 is about 40-60%). Jan-Feb needs hay/silage. Protein, minerals and dairy meal are still bought.
- **Poultry:** the flock is more than twice what the land can feed. Even 9.3 ha of maize (about 28 t) would cover about 30% of the ration and leave nothing for stock. Realistic: **5-15% of poultry feed** from range foraging, greens and sweet-potato vines/leaf meal. Bought feed stays the main cash item.
- **Duckweed/azolla:** my searches found no usable source (UNVERIFIED). From professional knowledge it could supply a few percent of duck ration from a small lined tank (not the pans: wild-bird and algae risk). It is a trial, not a plan.
- **Black soldier fly (BSF):** feasible as a pilot (larvae from kitchen and crop waste; protein offset of perhaps 10-20% for a small unit). Using poultry manure as substrate needs vet approval. Not a Gate 0 item. UNVERIFIED.
- **Sunflower** (suited to this altitude, UNVERIFIED) needs a press or miller for cake: 1 ha is trial scale only.

**Irrigating fodder from the pans.** Feasible physically only under reading A below. Legally **UNVERIFIED**: search found a 2009 guideline that below-ground pans under 10,000 m3 need no permit (old Act, current status unknown) and the Water Act 2016 / Water Resources Regulations 2021 (reg 23 "authorisation to construct works", then a permit; abstraction without authorisation is illegal, with reported fines up to KES 1m or 2 years). Livestock drinking is normally treated differently from irrigation. Ask the county WRA sub-office in writing before buying any pump or pipe.

## 2. Water budget

**Demand (L/day, UNVERIFIED norms):** poultry 500-840 (chickens 0.2-0.3 L, ducks 0.5-1.5); cattle 400-800; sheep and goats 240; cleaning, milking, staff 200-400. **Total drinking and hygiene 1.3-2.3 m3/day (40-70 m3/month, 480-840 m3/yr).**

**Fodder irrigation:** 2 ha Napier fully watered for 60 dry days is 3,600-4,800 m3. A "rescue" 25 mm/week on 0.5 ha is about 125 m3/week.

**Pan volume readings.** Usable = 85% of nominal (silt, dead storage). Losses assumed: open-water evaporation about 4 mm/day; seepage 1-10 mm/day if unlined (0 if intact liner). Surface area assumed 3,000 m2 per 8,000 m3 pan (depth about 3.5 m).

| Reading | Total / usable | Loss per day (evap + seep) | Days of cover: drinking only | + rescue irrigation 0.5 ha | + full 2 ha irrigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: 8 million L each | 16,000 / 13,600 m3 | 30-84 m3 | **160-425** | 130-270 | 87-133 |
| C: 800 m3 each (if a zero was added) | 1,600 / 1,360 m3 | 6-17 m3 | 70-165 | 37-52 | not feasible |
| B: 8 m3 each (tank, not a pan) | 16 / 14 m3 | n/a | **6-11 days** | none | none |

Reading B means a water emergency today. Reading A means drinking water is not the constraint, but losses (especially seepage) are, and refill depends on catchment: 9.3 ha at 1,300 mm is about 121,000 m3 of rain, so 10-20% runoff would refill 16,000 m3 only if the farm drains into the pans.

**Triggers (using the PLAN thresholds):**
- Pan level below 75% of measured usable: amber, stop all irrigation planning.
- Below 50%: red, drinking and hygiene only, start staged sales.
- Days of cover (usable / measured daily drop + demand) below 90: amber; below 45: red.
- 14 consecutive dry days in Nov-Dec: log level daily.
- Priority order: poultry and animal drinking, hygiene, nursery, then fodder.

## 4. The lorry

12 t is about 170 bags (a 1.4-1.9 month supply for 2,270 birds), so it could shuttle feed 6-8 times a year. Hay bales are volume-limited, so expect far less than 12 t of hay (UNVERIFIED). Composted litter (about 600 kg/m3, UNVERIFIED) loads 15-20 m3 per trip.

**Do not assume:**
- it is a tipper, 4WD or fitted for bulk; it can reach the farm on murram roads in wet weather, or carry its rated load on rural bridges;
- it has a valid NTSA inspection, insurance and goods-service licence, or a trained driver;
- it earns money (it is a capital item and the owner is >300 km away);
- it replaces miller delivery: millers often deliver bulk free above a volume (UNVERIFIED);
- one vehicle can carry feed, live birds and litter without washing and disinfecting between loads.

Controls: GPS tracker, trip log, fuel records, and a disinfect step on the gate.

## 5. Manure and litter for 2,270 birds

- **Volume:** previous rate (40-55 t per 1,000 birds) gives about 90-125 t/yr fresh, but free-ranging birds leave much on the range: expect 40-105 t collected (UNVERIFIED). Roughly 1.5-2.5 t nitrogen/yr.
- **Need:** Napier at 150-200 kg N/ha needs 300-400 kg N/yr for 2 ha. So litter far exceeds on-farm need. Apply 5-10 t/ha composted to Napier and crop land (3 ha = 15-30 t); surplus goes to sale or to neighbours as bagged compost.
- **Composting:** covered windrows on a sloped, bunded pad, turned, 90-120 days; leachate to a lined sump. Duck/goose litter is wet: mix with dry carbon (maize stover, Napier trash).
- **Rules (my recommendations, not legal findings):** no heaps, pads or carcass pits within 50 m of a pan and none upslope of one; no spreading within 30 m of a pan, on saturated ground, on slopes over about 12%, or 48 h before forecast heavy rain; incorporate quickly; keep a spreading log; never on sheep pasture or as cattle feed. Dead birds (30-50/month plausible) to carcass composting away from pans.

## 6. Measurements, first 8 weeks

| Week | Task |
|---|---|
| 1 | Count by breed with ages; feed stock count; GPS area map; staff gauges and rainfall gauge installed; contractor's excavation record or bill of quantities for each pan. |
| 1-2 | Pan volume: tape the top and bottom dimensions and depth grid; volume = h/3 x (A_top + A_bot + sqrt(A_top x A_bot)) for a frustum, or sum of grid cells; make a depth-to-volume table. |
| 1-2 | Soil sampling (4 units). Pan water for turbidity and coliforms. |
| 2-8 | Seepage and evaporation: record level daily at the same hour during dry days; evaporation from a floating marked bucket (level drop in bucket versus pond isolates seepage). |
| 2-8 | Feed use: weigh scoops issued per breed daily; compute bags/month per group. |
| 4-8 | Pasture yield: 1 m2 clip quadrats (10 per unit) before grazing, dried for DM. |
| 8 | Silt: sound bottom depth against as-built. Safety review: fence 1.2 m or more, ramp for livestock, warning signs, no drinking at the pan edge. |
