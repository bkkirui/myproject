# G0 People, tenure and governance (Borut, Bomet)

Prepared 2026-10-15. Farm HR/governance working notes, **not legal advice**: every legal point is for a Kenyan advocate (land and employment) to confirm. "UNVERIFIED" = only secondary sources seen; "DEFAULT" = my placeholder, not a sourced figure. Sources were accessed 2026-10-15 via web search summaries; I could not open the Gazette itself.

## 1. Week 1: find out what the land tenure really is

**Ask the owner for every paper (copies, photos) before any search:** title deed or green card, allotment letter, share/ballot certificate, sale agreement, receipts, survey/RIM map, any Land Control Board (LCB) consent, rates/rent receipts, names of seller and neighbours. "Until freehold title" suggests the owner may hold only a seller's agreement or an allotment, not a title.

**Searches (days 1-5):**
1. Parcel number from the papers (Borut parcels may be in an adjudication section; ask the area chief and the Bomet County Land Registrar/Adjudication office whether the Borut block is registered, still under adjudication, or a settlement scheme).
2. **Official search at the Land Registry** (Ardhisasa portal or manual Bomet registry). Reported fee KES 500-1,000, sources conflict, UNVERIFIED; reported that the registered owner may have to approve release on Ardhisasa, so if the owner is not the registered proprietor, the registry or advocate does it. Look for: proprietor name, acreage, charges, caveats, restrictions, lease. A "title" in a seller's name means the owner holds only a contract. Sources: [Ardhisasa guide, 2026](https://amccopropertiesltd.co.ke/how-to-conduct-land-search-using-ardhisasa); [nashthuo](https://nashthuo.com/how-to-do-a-land-search-in-kenya/) (both commercial, accessed 2026-10-15).
3. Rates/rent clearance and, for settlement-scheme land, Settlement Fund Trustees (SFT) account status and lease/allotment conditions (SFT can cancel for breach, per [Lamu case summary](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/keelc/2024/542/eng@2024-02-05), UNVERIFIED).
4. Check whether an LCB consent exists for how the owner got the land (Land Control Act s.6: sale, lease, mortgage, subdivision of agricultural land is void without it; application within six months of the agreement, extension only by the High Court; money paid under a void deal is recoverable). Source: [Strathmore summary](https://su-plus.strathmore.edu/handle/11071/9564), [Kitilit v Kibet](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/keca/2018/573). Ask the advocate whether an LCB consent is needed and whether the time has lapsed.
5. Physical check: the surveyor confirms boundaries on the ground; the neighbours and chief confirm who occupies and who claims.

| Status found | Lease | Sale | Loan/collateral | Insurance (property) | Succession | Effect on Gate 1 alternatives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Registered title in owner's name, clean search | Possible; LCB consent needed (lawyer to confirm for the term) | Possible with LCB consent | Charge possible | Insurable interest clear | Succession Act process on the title | All alternatives open |
| Title in seller's name; owner holds agreement | Owner cannot validly lease; sub-lease risky | Not owner's to sell; transfer first | None | Weak insurable interest | Contract right only | Only "self-run" on a licence; fix title first |
| Allotment/SFT scheme, conditions unmet | Usually restricted (UNVERIFIED) | Restricted | Rarely | Weak | Uncertain | Lease/sale likely blocked; lawyer to decide |
| Adjudication/group ranch share, no title | Unregistered interest; prospective | Same | No | Weak | Customary/Succession Act issues | Lease/sale parked; running the farm is possible, investment at risk |
| Dispute, caveat or charge found | Blocked | Blocked | Blocked | Hold | Contested | Pause fixed-asset spend; STOP-line to lawyer |

Rule: **no fixed investment (fencing, sheds, boreholes) beyond what the owner can afford to lose until the search is read by an advocate.**

## 2. Day-1 steps for the 3 staff

**Facts first (day 1-2):** full names, ID numbers, phone/M-Pesa numbers, home area, how long on the farm, current pay and what was promised, relatives of each other or the owner, and any who also keep their own animals nearby.

**Legal minimum (verify with advocate):**

| Item | Position | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Written contract | Employment of 3 months or more should be in writing with prescribed particulars (Employment Act 2007 s.9-10; section numbers vary by source, UNVERIFIED) | [Employment Act text](https://www.asi.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/1_EmploymentAct.pdf) |
| Probation | Commonly up to 6 months (UNVERIFIED); a court has found the exclusion of fair process during probation unconstitutional, so still give reasons and a hearing | [ELRC 2026](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/judgment/keelrc/2026/39/eng@2026-01-22) |
| Minimum wage (agriculture) | 2026 order (Legal Notices 95/96, deemed from 1 May 2026): unskilled KES 9,196.93/month (KES 385.24/day); stockman/herdsman/watchman daily 449.81, monthly shown as 20,621.15 which looks like a typo for about 10,621.15. **UNVERIFIED, read the Gazette** | [Grant Thornton wages guide 2026](https://www.grantthornton.co.ke/globalassets/1.-member-firms/kenya/insights/pdf/wages-guide-2026.pdf); [Kenya Law 2024 order](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2024/163) |
| Statutory deductions | NSSF 6% employee + 6% employer (lower/upper limits KES 9,000 / 108,000 from Feb 2026); SHIF 2.75% of gross (minimum KES 300 reported); Affordable Housing Levy 1.5% + 1.5%. All UNVERIFIED against NSSF/KRA/SHA | [PaySpace](https://payspace.com/blog/kenya-nssf-rates-update-2026); [Faidi HR](https://faidihr.com/blog/employee-benefits-statutory-contributions-in-kenya-2026-legal-guide) |
| Injury cover | WIBA: employer must insure against work injury (s.7; penalty reported up to KES 100,000 / 3 months) | [WIBA Act](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2007/13/eng@2007-11-09); [Sheriaplex s.7](https://www.sheriaplex.com/kenya-acts/5193-section-7-employer-to-be-insured) |
| Housing, leave, rest day, payslip | Required by Employment Act and agricultural wages order; details to be confirmed by the advocate | - |

Arrears risk: if staff were paid below the 2026 rate since 1 May 2026, arrears may be owed. Ask the advocate. Get KRA PINs, NSSF and SHA numbers for each worker in week 1.

**Role design (DEFAULT for 3 people; roles not persons until assessed):**

| Role | Core job | Never alone |
|---|---|---|
| Farm lead (supervisor) | Daily plan, morning count, photo report, calls owner, runs feed/drug keys with owner's rule | Cannot hold M-Pesa/bank, cannot be sole recorder, cannot sell |
| Livestock and poultry | Feeding, watering, checking sick animals, egg collection, cleaning | Does not carry drugs key alone |
| Fodder, crops and general | Pasture, fodder, fencing, water, night watch rota | Does not record counts for own area |

Rotation: swap the counter weekly so one person never counts what they care for.

**Plain-language job descriptions / task cards:** one A5 card per role, with pictures (cow, hen, bucket, sun for time of day, tick boxes), 6-8 tasks, each one line, in Kipsigis/Kiswahili as the workers prefer, explained orally by owner and read back. **Daily skeleton:** dawn (check all animals, count, water, feed), mid-morning (collect eggs, sick-animal check), afternoon (second feed, secure houses), dusk (count in, lock stores, photo of locked door and egg tray). **Weekly:** Monday weighing/feed stock count; Wednesday cleaning and drug store check; Friday egg and animal count with owner/counter; Saturday M-Pesa ledger read-out.

**Basic phone routine:** each evening the lead sends one WhatsApp group message: photo of the count board (chalk/whiteboard with date), photo of locked stores, deaths with ear-tag/photo, eggs trays. The owner (or a named person) writes a **phone ledger** (notebook or Notes app): date, item, amount, M-Pesa code. Every payment and sale has a code; M-Pesa statement is downloaded monthly and checked against the notebook.

**Assess who can lead (first 4 weeks):** keep it observable: arrives on time; counts match; reports bad news early; handles a sick-animal case calmly; treats co-workers fairly; reads/writes enough or can use voice notes; no unexplained money. Give each a small supervised task (run the Saturday count, order a feed bag with owner approval) and note results. Do not make the lead permanent before the 4-week review.

**Relationship checks:** ask directly who is related to whom, to suppliers, buyers, agrovets, the previous owner, or has their own animals/poultry; record it on a one-page conflict declaration signed by each worker and the owner. A relative is not banned but cannot be the only counter for their kin. Check references by a call to a prior employer or chief (with consent).

## 3. Right-sized controls (B0, B1-B10 for 3 people)

Plan figures (AOB percentages, auditors, managers, counsellors) assume a larger enterprise. For this farm they are **not** applied; use the following.

| Control (PLAN ref) | Non-waivable | Cheap replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Bank/M-Pesa (B1, B3, B0.2) | Owner holds the PIN and farm account; staff have no wallet access; owner pays wages; buyers pay the farm account/Paybill/till, never a worker's phone | Small petty-cash float, a cap the owner sets (DEFAULT none until owner decides); weekly receipt box |
| Opening count (B0.1) | Full count with a witness in week 1 (cattle/sheep/goat tag or mark, poultry by house and counted at night) | Independent person (see below) counts with owner; photos stored in owner's cloud |
| Stock register (B4) | One book + phone photos; each death photographed with tag/ear and witnessed, carcass logged | Weekly count compared with book |
| Drug and feed store (B4) | Locked; owner or lead holds key, never alone; drug use logged | Stock card taped on the door |
| Weekly count | Yes, every week | Different person counts each week |
| Receipts/sales (B4) | Written receipt for every sale; sale price cross-checked with M-Pesa | Photo of the receipt in the group |
| Authority tiers (B1) | Staff may spend nothing beyond the float; owner approves everything else | Emergency call-first rule for the vet: owner approves by call; money by M-Pesa only |
| Representative/auditor (B2, B6) | One trusted independent counter, unannounced visit at least quarterly (DEFAULT) | Not a formal audit firm |
| Leave and rotation | Rotate counter; annual leave | - |
| Cameras (B7) | If used, notice to staff and warning sign; keep footage short; locate away from private areas | Check ODPC rules with the advocate |

**Independent counter in Bomet (realistic):** (a) a trusted neighbour farmer not related to staff or a supplier; (b) an agrovet or the county veterinary/livestock officer on a paid visit (check the officer is allowed private work and is not also the supplier); (c) a retired extension officer or church/chief-recommended elder. The same person must not sell the farm inputs. Rotate every year or twice.

**Data and privacy:** the Data Protection Act applies to staff and CCTV data; the registration regulations reportedly exempt controllers with turnover under KES 5 million **and** fewer than 10 employees, with exceptions by sector (Bowmans, UNVERIFIED, [link](https://www.bowmanslaw.com/insights/data-protection/kenya-snapshot-analysis-of-the-data-protection-regulations-the-next-stage-in-data-protection-compliance-in-kenya/)). Keep staff ID copies locked and share nothing in public groups.

## 5. Risks specific to a first-time owner without records

| Risk | Likely form | First response |
|---|---|---|
| Theft | Eggs, birds, feed, small ruminants by staff or outsiders | Weekly count, locked stores, night watch rota, receipts |
| Rustling/stock theft | Cattle, sheep and goats at night or while grazing; law partly in a pending bill (Prevention of Livestock and Produce Theft Bill, not confirmed enacted) ([Parliament note](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/banditry%20kenya/32989/parliament-calls-for-views-on-proposed-anti-livestock-theft-bill)) | Ear-tag/brand every animal, photo ID book, secure night housing, report to chief and police |
| Disease | Mixed poultry (Newcastle, fowl typhoid), FMD, CCPP and PPR in small ruminants, ticks | Vet-of-record, vaccination calendar, quarantine new animals, isolate poultry species |
| Inputs fraud | Short feed bags, overpriced drugs, collusion with agrovet | Owner buys or watches delivery, weigh a sample bag, two quotes |
| Records | No baseline means no proof of loss | Week-1 count and photos |
| Land | Lost investment if title weak | Section 1 searches |
| Labour | Disputes, unpaid statutory deductions | Written contracts, pay slips |
| Distance | Owner far away | Daily photo, weekly call, monthly visit |

## 6. First 14 days (owner)

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Gather all land papers; list staff details; open a farm notebook and a farm-only M-Pesa/bank account |
| 2 | Visit the farm; do a first count with staff; photograph everything; tell staff the new rules (no cash, no sales without owner) |
| 3 | Instruct an advocate: land searches, contracts, entity choice |
| 4 | Official search request; check with chief and neighbours about boundaries |
| 5 | Meet the sub-county vet; agree vet-of-record; ask vaccination status |
| 6 | Hold role talk; hand out task cards; start daily WhatsApp reports |
| 7 | Independent opening count with a witness (neighbour/agrovet/livestock officer); lock stores |
| 8 | Register business name and KRA PIN; open employer registrations |
| 9 | Issue written contracts (advocate's template) and conflict declarations |
| 10 | WIBA quote; ask for livestock quotes; personal accident |
| 11 | Check pay vs minimum wage; fix any arrears plan |
| 12 | County permit application |
| 13 | Review first week of photos and counts; adjust roles |
| 14 | Read the search result with the advocate; decide Gate 1 path |

**Questions the owner must answer:** What papers exist for the land and in whose name? Who sold it to you and what did you pay? Acreage and boundaries? Are the animals on the farm now, whose are they, and are they tagged? Who are the 3 staff, how related, what pay was agreed, since when? Who is the nearest trusted person who can visit weekly? How far are you and how often can you visit? What cash and debt do you have? What markets and buyers? Is there water, power, phone signal? Would you lease or sell, and are you allowed to? What is the worst loss you can bear?
