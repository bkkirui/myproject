## 8. Land without title

Not legal advice; **a lawyer must confirm all of this before any step.**
- **Lease and sale may not be available at all** until the owner holds a registered proprietorship. An allotment letter, share certificate or adjudication-stage record is typically a contractual or equitable interest; a sale or lease of it may need the allocating body's consent, and in a Land Control Act area (Bomet is farming land) agricultural dealings usually need Land Control Board consent. Whether *this* interest can be leased, sold or charged, and for how long, is a lawyer question. A purchaser or tenant will also ask for a clean search. I could not verify the statutory text (Land Registration Act: faolex copy surfaced but provisions not read).
- **Credit:** banks and most lenders want a registered charge over title; without it, expect only chattel/livestock-backed or group/SACCO credit at small amounts (to confirm with lenders; assumption).
- **Insurance:** livestock and poultry cover is tied to the animals (identification, vet certificate), not land title, so it is likely obtainable; buildings cover on untitled land may be refused. To confirm with insurers.
- Effect on the model: ranking B excludes lease and sale; the `title_ok` switch on `Inputs` re-includes them once cleared. Until then the realistic choices are *run small, shrink, or wait*, not *lease or sell*.
- Also confirm the plot is the owner's and boundary/occupation is undisputed; a purchase price, deposit or instalments paid should be evidenced.

## 9. Decision statement

Plainly:
1. **Do not restructure into the PLAN's manager + deputy + full controls.** It is uneconomic in every case.
2. **Proceed only as a small owner-supervised enterprise with remote lightweight controls**, and only as a measured trial: the base benchmarks say it loses ~KES 0.23M a year before owner time, and the high case earns +0.78M. The honest label is a **conditional hold/measure**, not a pass. Take the cheap, non-waivable controls now (Day-7 packs, bank/M-Pesa to owner, bookkeeper, count), because they cost ~16k a month and protect KES ~1.6M of stock.
3. **At week 8, decide using measured values:** if annualised OMBM is at or above ~KES 0.5M, continue simplified; if near zero or negative, **shrink** (stop buying-in-feed layers first, cut to two staff plus casual, grow fodder) and re-run; if still short at week 16 (with title), compare **lease** against owner-operation; sale only with valuer and tax advice.
4. Cumulative loss limit: the G1 sign-off should set one (suggestion: KES 300k, about 10% of K) after which the owner must decide.
