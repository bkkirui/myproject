## 2. Controls for an absent owner (>300 km)

**Cash vault (key custody unknown: ask today).**

| Rule | Setting |
|---|---|
| Default | **No cash at the farm beyond a float.** Buyers pay by M-Pesa Till or bank; vault holds float, documents and tags only |
| Float cap | DEFAULT KES 10,000 total; per item KES 2,000; anything higher needs owner call and M-Pesa |
| Keys | Two different keys held by two different people (never relative + lead as the pair, never both staff on shift); owner holds a spare; re-key the lock now, because current key custody is unknown |
| Count | Daily at dusk: two staff count together, photograph the cash and the cash book page; weekly the owner gets a photo with the date on a board |
| Unavoidable cash sale | Serialised receipt book (duplicate), bank within 48 h, deposit slip photo to owner the same day |
| Banking proof | Bank slip or M-Pesa SMS forwarded to the owner; monthly statement received by the owner directly from the bank |
| Opening | Owner or a named non-staff person counts the vault contents once with a photographed, signed sheet (the opening cash is unknown) |
| Insurance | Ask a broker for cash-in-safe/burglary cover; terms and whether it applies to farm vaults UNVERIFIED (not searched successfully) |

**Bank and M-Pesa.**
- Farm account: owner is sole releaser; if the bank allows, owner plus one remote approver (accountant or spouse) for transfers above DEFAULT KES 20,000; staff get no cards or tokens. Dual-approval and alert features differ by bank; ask your bank in writing, UNVERIFIED (my search found no bank documentation).
- SMS/email alerts on every debit and credit to the owner and to one second person (the advocate or accountant), so no change can pass unseen.
- **M-Pesa Till for buyers.** Third-party guides report a KES 250,000 per-transaction and KES 500,000 daily limit and merchant fees capped near KES 200; Safaricom's own PDF shows KES 70,000 and 0.27%, so the figures conflict: confirm with Safaricom. Settlement to the bank can be set to sweep daily, with portal roles (operator/admin/checker/viewer) reported ([paybillke guide, accessed 2026-10-15](https://paybillke.com/guides/mpesa-limits-2026); [Safaricom till PDF, undated, older](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/images/Downloads/mpesa-business-till.pdf); [settlement and roles](https://paybillke.com/guides/mpesa-settlement-time-2026), all UNVERIFIED). Admin on the Till is the owner only; staff get "viewer" at most. A buyer's payment must show the Till name on the SMS the buyer shows the staff.
- Wages: owner pays on the 28th from the bank to each worker's own number or account; never in cash from the vault.
- Purchases: supplier paid by the owner after a delivery photo; no staff-held money except the float.

**Receipts and stock register.**
- Duplicate receipt book: number every receipt; the carbon stays in the book; photograph each page weekly.
- **Stock register (one book + a daily WhatsApp photo of a dated board):** opening, births/hatch, deaths, sales, slaughter, transfers, closing, for each of the **8 poultry groups** and each ruminant class. Evening counts by group at roost (poultry in/out of house). A group with variance above DEFAULT 1% for ruminants or 3% for poultry in a week is queried by call.
- **Mortality:** photo of every dead animal with date board and, for sheep, the tag; birds in batches photographed in a row; burn/bury witnessed by a second worker; weekly mortality total reported by the vet's log where the vet attended.
- **Egg/meat sales log:** date, group, trays/kg, buyer, price, receipt no., Till/M-Pesa code. Eggs: laying group count x lay rate gives an expected tray number; a 4-week trend line flags slow theft.
- **Lorry log book:** each trip: date, driver, odometer start/end, route, cargo and weight, who loaded, who received, signed by the receiver; fuel only against pump receipts with odometer photo; fuel use per 100 km tracked. No lorry hire or private use without the owner's written OK. **GPS tracker** (DEFAULT KES 6,000-12,000 device + KES 300-1,000/month, UNVERIFIED): worth it given 12-tonne cargo and fuel; owner receives the alert, not the driver. Key and papers held by a person other than the driver; odometer photo at each end-of-day.

**Weekly 15-minute owner routine (Sunday evening):** (1) 5 min: read the week's daily counts by group, mortality photos, and the vault photo; (2) 5 min: bank/M-Pesa statement against sales log and receipt pages; (3) 5 min: call the lead, then one other worker separately: "anything unusual, anything short?" Record the date and the three points in the farm notebook.

**Monthly routine (about 2 hours):** download bank and Till statements; match sales log to deposits and wages; lorry fuel per 100 km; feed used against bird numbers and the 6-month-store claim; vet visit log; one surprise-count result from the independent counter; staff one-to-one calls; decide the next month's cash float.

## 3. The relative-worker issue

- **Declaration** (signed by the relative, the other two, the owner): kinship, other animals the relative or family keep, any buyers/suppliers who are family.
- **Role separation:** the relative must not be the sole counter, sole vault-key holder, store keeper or lorry driver/fuel handler, and must not be paired only with another worker's kin. Sales and receipts: someone else records and a third person counts.
- **Constructive use:** a family member can be a trusted day-to-day messenger and carry the owner's message to the other two; share that position openly. Use the relative as a witness when the other two count, not as the counter in which his own role is involved. The relative may report problems to the owner outside the farm chain; show a way to report any worker's problem to the owner (phone, not WhatsApp group).
- **Family pressure risks:** requests for credit sales, free eggs/birds, loans, favoured hours or pay; staff may fear reporting a relative; "ethnic or family" buyers asking for discounts. Rule: any concession, discount or loan to anyone needs the owner's written OK; the relative gets the same discipline path as others (written warning, hearing). Say this to the relative in front of the others and in writing.
- Pay and treatment must match the other two, or the other two will hide losses to protect themselves.

## 4. Independent counter and surprise counts

| Option | Role | Realistic cost per visit (DEFAULT, UNVERIFIED) | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Neighbour farmer | Counts ruminants and poultry houses with a clicker and the tag register | KES 1,500-3,000 | Social ties; must not be related to staff, buyer or supplier |
| Agrovet | Feed, drug and expiry counts | KES 2,000-4,000 | Sells inputs: conflict; do not use for feed/drug audits of their own sales |
| County livestock/vet officer (private visit) | Health review, tag checks | KES 3,000-6,000 incl. transport | Needs permission to do private work; may be unavailable |

Design: **monthly surprise count** for the first 3 months, then every 6-8 weeks and at least quarterly (PLAN B0.5 maximum interval 4 months). The owner texts the counter the morning of the visit, not the staff. Counter counts first, compares to the book second; each count: one ruminant class and 2 poultry groups, store sample, lorry odometer vs log, vault cash. Two counters, rotated at least yearly. Annual cost at monthly cadence about KES 18,000-36,000; quarterly about KES 6,000-12,000. Compare with the exposure: 3 x 22,000 x 12 = KES 792,000/year wages alone, and an unknown stock value.

**Weekly vet: may rely on** clinical diagnosis, vaccination and treatment records, mortality cause where he attends, drug dispensing log. **May not rely on** for: counts, mortality certification when he sold the drugs, drug stock, authorisation of spend, or the opening count. If the vet sells drugs, the owner buys at least some drugs independently (two quotes) and compares invoices with the vet's treatment log. A vet who prescribes does not certify deaths for insurance (B6).
