## 1. Notifiable diseases and reporting route

**What was verified (S1):** The Act (Director of Veterinary Services administers; revised to LN 221/2023) defines notifiable disease by list plus Minister's gazette power. Entries seen include trypanosomiasis, heartwater, lumpy skin disease, pullorum, "fowl pest" (Newcastle/avian pest), paratuberculosis; the 2015 subsidiary schedule lists others (e.g. psittacosis/ornithosis, avian encephalomyelitis). Brucellosis was added by LN 68 of 2011. **The full current list was not seen: UNVERIFIED.** Ask the CDVS for the current schedule and gazette notices.

| Species | Likely notifiable / treat as reportable (confirm against list) |
|---|---|
| Cattle | FMD, LSD, anthrax, brucellosis, ECF/theileriosis, CBPP, RVF, trypanosomiasis, heartwater, black quarter (UNVERIFIED which are scheduled) |
| Sheep/goats | FMD, PPR, sheep and goat pox, CCPP, RVF, anthrax, brucellosis |
| Poultry | Newcastle ("fowl pest"), HPAI, pullorum/fowl typhoid, plus whatever the schedule adds |

**Route (UNVERIFIED contacts; get numbers on Day 1):** Ward/sub-county veterinary officer (Bomet has sub-counties incl. Bomet Central, Bomet East, Chepalungu, Konoin, Sotik) then Bomet CDVS (S5 names a Director of Veterinary Services, Gilbert Koech, at the time of that article; confirm current post-holder) then national DVS, Nairobi. Reporting is also to be made by the vet-of-record, but the holder should never wait.
- Act first: isolate sick animals, close the holding to movement in/out, no sale, no slaughter, leave carcasses unopened (anthrax), call the sub-county vet and your vet-of-record in parallel.
- Standstill/movement: the Rules (S2) require a permit to move cattle, pigs, sheep, goats within "restricted areas" scheduled under the Rules; inspectors/appointed issuers issue permits. Counties also issue ad hoc quarantines (S7: written permission from veterinary officers for any entry/exit). Whether Bomet is currently a restricted area or quarantined: **UNVERIFIED; ask CDVS.** Treat any movement of ruminants (including to market) as needing a vet movement permit until told otherwise.
- Slaughter/sale: sick or suspect animals must not be sold or slaughtered; slaughter only at inspected slaughterhouse or with vet/meat-inspector clearance (Meat Control Act; details UNVERIFIED).
- Compensation: no verified scheme; assume none. Insurance, if any, to be checked.

## 2. Risk profile, Bomet highland (all risk ratings are my qualitative judgment, UNVERIFIED locally)

Bomet is high-rainfall tea/dairy highland, a tick-borne disease and fasciola zone, with documented ECF vaccination campaigns (S5, 15,000 animals) and county anthrax/black quarter vaccination (S5). FMD (SAT1/SAT2) outbreaks were reported in nearby Rift Valley counties in 2026 (S6; neighbours like Nandi/Uasin Gishu named; Bomet not named; date reliability low).

| Disease | Relevance and what to do |
|---|---|
| ECF and other tick-borne | Likely the main cattle killer. Infection-and-treatment vaccine (ITM) is available through vets; ask CDVS whether the county programme still runs. Tick control by spray/dip with rotation of acaricide classes; resistance is widespread in Africa (S9), so vet-guided, not routine over-use. Cattle (10) only, but sheep/goats carry ticks too. |
| LSD | Vaccinate cattle annually (Neethling-type vaccine; check KEVEVAPI). Biting-insect spread. Notifiable. |
| FMD | Biggest market-closure risk. Vaccination is voluntary and often county-subsidised (S6); SAT types mean vaccine match matters (ask vet). Notifiable. |
| Brucellosis | Zoonosis; test purchased cattle/goats; no raw milk; abortion protocol. |
| Anthrax | County has run vaccination (S5); vaccinate annually if county advises; never open carcasses. |
| PPR | Sheep/goats; national campaign (S6), voluntary, KEVEVAPI vaccine; reported as long-lasting but get vet advice. |
| Sheep/goat pox, CCPP | Notifiable-type; vaccine availability UNVERIFIED. CCPP is chronic respiratory in goats. |
| RVF | Highland Bomet low-to-moderate; flood-linked (S8). Vaccinate only on vet advice. |
| Trypanosomiasis | Low in highland; vet to confirm. |
| Fasciolosis (liver fluke) | High in wet highland pasture and for sheep; strategic treatment with flukicide timed by vet; avoid wet grazing. |
| Haemonchus | Major threat to sheep/goats in wet seasons; FAMACHA scoring plus selective dosing; check for dewormer resistance (faecal egg count reduction test). |

**Poultry:** Newcastle (vaccinate on a schedule, kienyeji too), Gumboro/IBD, fowl pox (wing-web vaccine, mosquito-linked), fowl typhoid/pullorum (notifiable, test and cull), coccidiosis (litter control; ionophores kill ruminants, A4), HPAI. Kenya's current HPAI status: **UNVERIFIED** (no current report found; check WOAH WAHIS and DVS; past scares in 2006 and 2017 were import-related).
**Mixed-species poultry:** ducks and geese can carry and shed influenza (and Salmonella) with few signs; guinea fowl and kienyeji are hardy but Newcastle-susceptible; layers are the most vulnerable and valuable. Keep ducks/geese physically separate from layers and from open water used by wild birds; separate feed, tools and boots; guinea fowl are free-ranging and hard to confine, so decide at G1 whether to keep them.

## 3. Localised Day-7 safety pack (adapts Appendix A0)

1. **Vet-of-record.** Options: (a) private vet in Bomet town or Sotik (find via CDVS, Kenya Veterinary Association Rift Valley branch, local agrovet referrals; UNVERIFIED names), (b) county vet officers as the authority route (not a paid 24-h service), (c) KALRO Kericho/Veterinary Investigation lab for poultry necropsy (UNVERIFIED nearest lab). Verify any vet or paravet: search the Kenya Veterinary Board public register for the current year (S3; surgeons and paraprofessionals are separate registers; annual retention due by 31 December), ask for the practising licence and ID, and check the agrovet's premises licence with VMD (S4). Unlicensed sellers/diagnosticians are being closed (S4).
2. Suspicion card, owner pre-authorisation letter, euthanasia person, fridge/power/water check, interim treatment list: keep as written in A0. Euthanasia means: poultry cervical dislocation is feasible; large stock needs vet/licensed person (firearm licensing in Kenya is restrictive; UNVERIFIED).
3. **Vaccination skeleton** (confirm every product, dose and timing with vet; KEVEVAPI is the national producer; product list and prices UNVERIFIED; supply shortages and quality complaints have been reported, S6):

| Species | Skeleton |
|---|---|
| Cattle | FMD (campaign or ~6-monthly per vet), LSD annually, anthrax/black quarter annually (county), ECF ITM per vet, brucellosis (heifer calves, vet-only), tick programme |
| Sheep/goats | PPR (campaign), sheep/goat pox if available, clostridials/pulpy kidney per vet, anthrax where advised, CCPP per vet |
| Chickens, ducks, geese, guinea fowl | Newcastle (I-2 thermostable for kienyeji; schedule per vet), Gumboro for chicks, fowl pox, Marek for layer chicks if bought day-old; duck/goose vaccines are largely UNVERIFIED |

4. **Tick control:** vet picks acaricide class and rotation, spray race or hand-spraying schedule; record each treatment; withdrawal periods apply (milk/meat).
5. **Deworming:** FAMACHA/faecal egg counts plus selective treatment; fluke dosing timed by season; rotate classes.
6. **Quarantine of new stock:** default 30 days apart, test (brucellosis, vet-chosen), treat for ticks/worms, vaccinate; require a vet movement permit and origin check. Poultry: all-in/all-out; do not add birds into existing flocks.
7. **Poultry biosecurity:** separate houses per species and age; ducks/geese downstream of layers and not sharing ponds with them; footbaths; dedicated boots per house; layers visited first, others last; no visitors; covered feed; rodent control; one worker per zone if possible (3 workers: at least rota to avoid cross-species traffic).

## 4. Changes to generic PLAN thresholds for Kenyan smallholder reality

- **Floors:** with ~1,000 birds likely in many small flocks of 20-200 (breakdown unknown), the "3/6 birds per house per day" floors are too high for a 30-bird flock. Use percentage legs and a 7-day cumulative leg per flock, with a "2+ deaths with signs in one flock in a day" amber. Vet to set.
- **Kienyeji and free-range baselines** (predation, losses) are higher than layers/broilers; the vet should write species baselines before red thresholds apply.
- **Remove or soften** items assuming generators, fridge-backed vaccine storage, 24-h vet cover, lab turnaround, two-key registers and gate passes. Replace with: cool-box with ice packs for vaccine collection same day, mobile phone reporting, notebook register signed by two people.
- Vet response of 2 hours is unrealistic outside Bomet town; use phone triage within 2 hours and visit within 24 hours; sub-county officer as backup.
- Quarantine 30 days stays; ">=50-100 m poultry separation" may be hard on a small plot; if impossible, separate by fence/different corner and strict traffic rules.
- Movement permit and market closures (S6, S7) add delays: bake 1-2 weeks of lead time into sale plans.
- Cold chain "2-8 C" stays for vaccines, but power failures are routine in rural Kenya.

## 5. Medicine control and withdrawal

The Veterinary Medicines Directorate (VMD) regulates registration, distribution and prescription of veterinary medicines under the Veterinary Surgeons and Veterinary Paraprofessionals Act and 2015 regulations (S4; formerly the Pharmacy and Poisons Board). Buy only from VMD-licensed agrovets; require a prescription for POM-V drugs, especially antimicrobials; keep receipts and batch numbers. Withdrawal periods: use the product label and the vet's written advice. **No Kenyan statutory withdrawal table was found: UNVERIFIED.** The register, no-sale flag and egg/milk hold in A3 stay valid. Meat-export concern over residues (S4 press) means buyers may test.

## 6. Licences and permits (list only; flag for lawyer/county verification)

- Livestock movement permit (Animal Diseases Rules; sub-county vet) for ruminants.
- Slaughter: use licensed slaughterhouse; on-farm slaughter for sale generally not allowed (UNVERIFIED); meat inspection/transport certificates.
- Egg and poultry meat sale: county public health/food licence; egg grading/registration requirements UNVERIFIED; milk if dairy added.
- County single business permit; possible county livestock/poultry keeping licence or fee; agricultural produce cess.
- Poultry hatchery/breeding, if any, may need DVS approval (UNVERIFIED).
- NEMA environmental approval or audit (waste, manure, EIA thresholds UNVERIFIED).
- WRA water-use permit for borehole/river abstraction (S10, weak source; fees unverified).
- Land: title status and any land-control-board consent for lease/sale (not health, but gating).
- Firearm licence if euthanasia by firearm.
- Employment: NSSF/SHIF/KRA registrations for 3 workers; work injury insurance.
- Vaccinator/animal health worker registration of anyone treating animals (KVB, S3).

## 7. Questions for the owner

1. Are animals already on the farm? Vaccination, treatment and tick history; any deaths or abortions so far?
2. Exact count by species and age; how many separate flocks, and how housed/grazed? Are ducks/geese on a pond or stream?
3. Source of purchased stock and papers; planned new purchases?
4. Is there a dip, spray race or crush? Who sprays and how often? Is the farm in a tea/dairy area with a cooperative vet?
5. Water source (river, spring, borehole, rain) and power/signal at the site?
6. Do you sell milk, eggs, live animals or meat, and where? Do buyers ask for certificates?
7. Do you already know a vet or paravet and the local agrovet? Name them and we will verify.
8. How far do you live from the farm, and who is on site daily? Staff training in animal health?
9. Land title status (affects NEMA/WRA/county licences).
10. Budget for vaccines and vet visits per month.
