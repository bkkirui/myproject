## 4. Feed balance skeleton

**Method.** 1 TLU = 250 kg liveweight; dry matter intake about 2.5% of liveweight = 6.25 kg DM/TLU/day [UNVERIFIED, standard FAO/ILRI convention]. Conversion: cattle 1.0 (mature crossbred dairy cows may be 1.4-1.8), sheep and goats 0.1 each. Herd weights are unknown.

| Class | Head | TLU | DM need, kg/day | DM per year, t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cattle | 10 | 10-14 | 62-90 | 23-33 |
| Sheep | 50 | 5 | 31-45 | 11-16 |
| Goats | 10 | 1 | 6-9 | 2-3 |
| **Total ruminants** | | **16-20** | **100-145** | **37-53** |

Add 10-20% for lactation, lambing and wastage. Protein and energy: lactating cows need about 12-14% CP and more than 10 MJ ME/kg DM; Napier alone (about 8-10% CP, UNVERIFIED) is short, which is the job of Desmodium (a legume), kale and a purchased dairy meal. Minerals and salt are always bought.

**Land for forage self-sufficiency (rough).** Napier/Desmodium cut-and-carry can reach 15-25 t DM/ha/yr on-farm (research highland figures are higher, e.g. 36.8 t at Muguga [S6]; the Napier-Desmodium mix produced 45.9 t/ha in a central-highlands trial [S6]; on-farm 15-25 is my UNVERIFIED haircut). Grazed kikuyu gives perhaps 5-7 t usable DM/ha (UNVERIFIED).
- Cattle on Napier/Desmodium, cut-and-carry: **about 1.5-2.5 ha**.
- Sheep and goats on pasture: **about 2-3 ha** (plus 20% buffer).
- Total: **about 4-6 ha** of good forage, with Jan-Feb reserves (hay/silage) inside that. If sheep are kept on rough pasture only, 7-9 ha is realistic. 50 sheep on 3 ha is very tight.

**Is the herd viable?** It is a small herd and is realistic only if the farm has at least 5 ha of usable forage land. If total land is under 4 ha, the sheep number must come down, or feed must be bought. Total acreage is the first answer needed.

**Poultry feed demand.** Species mix is unknown. Intake: layers 115 g/bird/day in Kenyan farm flocks and 120-130 g from week 28 (KEBS table quoted by KALRO) [S8]; improved kienyeji, ducks, guinea fowl and geese: UNVERIFIED, assume 80-150 g.

| Scenario (1,000 birds, all adults) | Feed per day | Feed per month | 70 kg bags/month | per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low (90 g avg) | 90 kg | 2.7 t | about 39 | about 32 t |
| Mid (110 g) | 110 kg | 3.3 t | **about 47** | about 40 t |
| High (130 g, layers only) | 130 kg | 3.9 t | about 56 | about 47 t |

**Rough.** Chicks and growers eat less; 70 kg is the common bag size but 50 kg is also sold (UNVERIFIED). I found no 2026 price, so the agronomist must quote (a KALRO official puts feed at about 80% of poultry production cost [S8]). **Poultry feed is the biggest cash item by far** and is large next to the ruminants, whose feed is mostly grown.

**Can the farm grow its own poultry feed?** Not at this scale. A 40 t/year ration is about 55% maize (22 t) plus 20-25% protein cake (8-10 t), plus minerals. Highland smallholder maize yields of about 2-4 t/ha (UNVERIFIED) mean **6-11 ha of maize** and several more of sunflower or soya, on one crop a year (long rains). Realistic ceiling: 10-20% offset by growing greens, Desmodium leaf meal, kitchen and crop waste, and maybe sunflower, with ducks and geese grazing. Buying a balanced mash from two suppliers is the plan; revisit at G1 once flock mix and land are known. Ask whether 1,000 birds is actually sustainable, since feed cost per egg or bird sold is the viability test.

## 6. Manure, litter and chemical cross-flow rules (adapted to this farm)

1. **Ducks and geese on ponds: no.** Open water draws wild birds and raises avian influenza and salmonella risk, and conflicts with the plan's "no contact with surface water" biosecurity rule. Keep waterfowl in a separate unit with covered or nipple/trough water. Geese may graze Napier edges, kept off the chicken yard and off water used by chickens.
2. **Medicated poultry feed (ionophore coccidiostats such as monensin, salinomycin, lasalocid):** toxic to sheep, goats and cattle. Separate stores, scoops and labels; no spilled feed in ruminant areas; never feed poultry feed or litter to ruminants. This is higher risk here because the farm mixes species and staff roles are undefined. Observe egg withdrawal periods; no medicated feed to layers whose eggs are sold unless the label allows.
3. **Litter:** 1,000 birds yield roughly 40-55 t fresh litter/year (UNVERIFIED), which is a real fertiliser asset. Compost it (windrow, turned, 90-120 days before use on kale or other ground-contact crops, per PLAN A4). Apply to Napier (cut 3-4 weeks after spreading) and to the cropped plots; **never on sheep pasture** (copper, pathogens, ionophore residues) and never as cattle feed (botulism).
4. **Cattle and sheep manure** go to Napier and crops first; keep manure heaps and drainage away from the water source and the poultry zone (>=50 m).
5. **Chemicals:** acaricide, pesticide and dip waste never near water or poultry houses; register every product with a withdrawal interval; keep dip wash away from pasture until the interval ends.
