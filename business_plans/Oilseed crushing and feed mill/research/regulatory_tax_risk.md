# Regulatory, Tax and Policy-Risk Assessment: Oilseed Crushing + Animal/Fish-Feed Mill (Kenya)

*Prepared 19 October 2026 from a corporate-law and tax/policy-risk perspective. Reference plant (see [feedstock_supply.md](feedstock_supply.md)): a 20,000 t/yr seed crusher with an integrated extruded fish-feed and livestock-feed line in the Kisumu/Busia corridor, selling crude or refined oil into the domestic market and using cake in-house. Direct page fetches failed with DNS or proxy errors, including for the EAC Gazette PDFs, KRA, PwC and Bowmans. All findings therefore come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages. Each figure carries a source link and date. **[ESTIMATE]** marks my own figures and gives the reasoning. **[DATED]** marks sources from before 2025. **[CONFLICT]** marks points where sources disagree. **[UNVERIFIED – prior knowledge]** marks statements from my background knowledge that this session did not confirm. This is not a legal opinion. Every rate below should be confirmed against the gazetted text before financial close.*

## 1. Customs, duties and levies on edible oils, oilseeds and feed ingredients

### 1.1 Edible oils: the protection the crusher relies on

- **CPO (HS 1511.10).** The EAC CET rate is 0%. Kenya applied a one-year "stay of application" at **10% from 1 Jul 2024** (EAC Gazette of 30 Jun 2024) ([Milling MEA, Jul 2024](https://millingmea.com/cooking-oil-prices-surge-following-new-import-duty/) [DATED]). The June 2025 gazette shows 1511.10.00 at CET 0% with a 10% stay for Kenya and Uganda ([EAC Gazette No. 19, 30 Jun 2025](https://www.tra.go.tz/images/uploads/pages/EAC_GAZETTE_Vol._AT_1_NO_._19_JUNE_2025_.pdf)).
- **The High Court struck down the 10% duty on 27 Nov 2025.** In COFEK's petition, Justice Bahati Mwamuye held the levy unconstitutional because there had been no public participation (Arts 10, 209, 210). He barred the Treasury and EAC CSs, the AG and the National Assembly from implementing the 30 Jun 2024 gazette notice, and directed that future EAC tax measures go through public consultation and parliamentary review ([Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/latest-kenya-times-news/win-for-kenyans-court-rules-on-10-crude-palm-oil-import-duty-adopted-via-eac/); [AllAfrica, 27 Nov 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202511270355.html)). In Jan 2026 PwC advised importers to claim refunds of duty paid in the 12 months to 30 Jun 2025 ([AllAfrica, 14 Jan 2026](https://allafrica.com/stories/202601140371.html); [Streamline](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/windfall-for-importers-as-court-scraps-illegal-palm-oil-tax)).
- **But the 30 Jun 2026 EAC Gazette (No. 16, effective 1 Jul 2026) lists CPO among Kenya's tariff "increases" for 2026/27** ([EAC Gazette No. 16](https://www.eac.int/documents?controller=download&task=download.file&file=a3c7fa5a-b765-48ed-befa-b6b65bf5b3cc&name=EAC+Vol.+AT+1+NO.+16+JUNE+2026.pdf.pdf&Itemid=297), PDF not retrievable; [FNJ Associates](https://fnjassociates.co.ke/a-guide-to-customs-import-duty-in-kenya/); [Wansom, 2 Oct 2026](https://www.wansom.ai/briefly/news/kenya-s-customs-squeeze-export-declarations-car-valuation-disputes-and-eac-tariff-moves-2026-10-02)). **[CONFLICT]** I found no report of an appeal against the November 2025 judgment, nor of whether the government re-did the stay through Parliament to cure the participation defect. **Judgement:** the CPO duty is legally contested and could be collected, refunded or suspended at any time. The model should not depend on it. Removing it would take away about KES 15k/t of oil-price protection (row 25 of [market_demand.md](market_demand.md)).
- **Refined oils** (refined soy oil, RBD palm olein, refined sunflower and corn oil) carry a Kenya stay of **25%**, or 25% / USD 500 per t in the 2023 gazette ([EY, Jul 2023](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2023-1308) [DATED]; 2025 gazette above). Edible oils have been on the EAC sensitive list since 2017 ([The EastAfrican](https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business/edible-oils-now-on-eac-list-of-sensitive-goods-1362544) [DATED]), so 35% is the ceiling. This 25% protects *refiners*. It does not protect a seed crusher selling crude oil to refiners.
- **Rules of origin.** Oil refined from imported crude is non-originating and pays duty crossing EAC borders. Oil crushed from EAC-grown seed should qualify for duty-free EAC/COMESA trade (see [market_demand.md](market_demand.md) §1).
- **Further charges on imported oil:** IDF 2.5% plus RDL 2.0% plus VAT 16% (PwC Jan 2026 via [Dawan](https://www.dawan.africa/news/pwc-urges-importers-to-seek-refunds-after-high-court-strikes-down-10percent-palm-oil-duty)). There is also the **AFA import levy of 4% of CIF on finished nut and oil-crop products** ([Business Daily, Apr 2025](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/industry/manufacturing/kagwe-tax-boost-for-edible-oil-firms-in-special-economic-zones-5007318)). Whether this 4% is enforced on bulk palm olein imports was not established. If it is, it adds roughly 4% to import parity for refined oil.
- **Enforcement leakage.** In 2024 refined palm oil was misdeclared as crude, with an alleged KES 62bn revenue loss ([Capital FM, Aug 2024](https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/business/2024/08/kenya-loses-sh62bn-as-imported-refined-palm-oil-disguised-as-crude-oil-enters-mombasa-port/) [DATED]). Undervalued or misclassified imports reduce the protection the tariff schedule seems to offer.
- **Political economy.** KAM says 13 edible-oil companies have a combined capacity of 2.18 Mt and the sector turns over more than KES 130bn ([KAM on X, Oct 2024](https://x.com/KAM_Kenya/status/1841742903820894410) [DATED]). These refiners import palm and lobby *against* the CPO duty ([People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/news/agency-calls-for-removal-of-10pc-tax-on-crude-palm-oil/amp); [Millingmea](https://millingmea.com/edible-oil-processors-urge-treasury-to-eliminate-import-duty-tax/)). A small seed crusher therefore sits on the losing side of the industry's main lobbying agenda.

### 1.2 Oilseeds, cake and feed ingredients

- Soybeans, sunflower seed and cottonseed carry **10% CET**, yellow maize 25%. EAC and COMESA origin is duty-free ([World Grain](https://world-grain.com/articles/20337-kenya-livestock-sector-facing-high-feed-prices) [DATED]).
- **Feed-ingredient waivers recur almost every year:**
  - 2021–22: duty-free quotas for 18 millers covering yellow maize, soy meal, soybeans, cottonseed cake, sunflower cake, sorghum and fishmeal, to 31 Oct 2022 ([Star, Dec 2021](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2021-12-14-treasury-waives-duty-on-imported-animal-feed) [DATED]; [Food Business Africa](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/?p=1850132) [DATED]).
  - 2023–24: yellow maize, SBM, protein concentrates and premixes ([CDH, Mar 2023](https://cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2023/Practice/Tax/tax-and-exchange-control-alert-30-march-2023-take-advantage-of-the-import-duty-waivers-to-reduce-high-food-prices.html) [DATED]). The PS refused an extension in Feb 2024 ([Star, Feb 2024](https://www.the-star.co.ke/counties/central/2024-02-07-ps-rules-out-extension-of-import-duty-waiver-on-animal-feed-raw-materials) [DATED]).
  - 2025: a 50% duty waiver on 5.5m bags of yellow maize for vetted feed millers ([Milling MEA](https://millingmea.com/?p=2031342); [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/govt-announces-50pc-duty-waiver-on-imported-yellow-maize/)).
  - Sep 2026: the government is set to gazette duty-free import of 360,000 t of yellow maize (prior notes, [GNA](https://gna.org.gh/2026/09/kenya-mulls-duty-free-maize-imports-amid-shortage/)).
  - The July 2025 EAC Gazette lists firm-specific remission quotas for Kenyan manufacturers valid to 30 Jun 2026 ([EAC Gazette No. 25, Jul 2025](https://www.rra.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/EAC_Vol._AT_1_NO._25_JULY_2025.pdf)).
- **Implication for an integrated plant:** waivers *lower* the feed line's input cost and *depress* the market value of the crusher's cake. The **duty remission scheme** (EAC CMA Fifth Schedule) is open to a licensed feed manufacturer. **Apply for a remission quota on premixes, fishmeal and any SBM top-up from year 1.** [ESTIMATE] This saves 10% of the CIF value of imported protein. A 20,000 t feed line importing about 3,000 t of SBM/fishmeal at about KES 90k/t would save about KES 27m a year.
- **GMO restriction (see §4.3):** GM SBM, which is most world supply, currently cannot be imported. That is why non-GM SBM sells at a premium in Kenya.

### 1.3 IDF and RDL

- The base case uses **IDF 2.5% and RDL 2.0%**, which is what PwC applied to CPO in Jan 2026 ([Dawan/PwC](https://www.dawan.africa/news/pwc-urges-importers-to-seek-refunds-after-high-court-strikes-down-10percent-palm-oil-duty)). The statute's 2026 consolidation is on [Kenya Law](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/2016/29/eng@2026-01-01/source) but was not readable.
- **[CONFLICT]** on history:
  - IDF was cut from 3.5% to 2.5% in 2023 ([Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/101778-govt-restores-import-declaration-tax-finance-bill-after-losing-ksh10-billion-fy20232024) [DATED]).
  - RDL was 1.5% in 2024 ([Nation](https://www.nation.africa/kenya/business/lower-rates-imports-cut-sh8bn-from-railway-levy-4729872) [DATED]), with 2.5% proposed ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/import-costs-to-jump-on-railway-levy-increase-to-2-5pc-4810522) [DATED]).
  - A reduced IDF of 1.5% for manufacturers' raw materials and intermediates exists or existed ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/importers-kra-in-dispute-over-import-charges-2277554), date unclear). **[UNVERIFIED – prior knowledge]** IDF and RDL exemptions may be available to manufacturers on raw materials and to SEZ enterprises. Confirm with KRA.
- **Capital goods.** Most process machinery falls in the CET 0% band [UNVERIFIED – prior knowledge] but still pays IDF and RDL, about 4.5% of CIF. On USD 6–10m of imported plant that is roughly **KES 35–60m**, which belongs in capex. The EY Finance Act 2026 PDF refers to a VAT exemption on capital goods for manufacturing investments of at least KES 2bn ([EY FA2026](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-ke/documents/ey-kenya-finance-act-2026.pdf)). Otherwise 16% VAT on machinery is paid and later credited, which costs working capital.

### 1.4 Export and Investment Promotion Levy and excise

- The EIPL applies to selected ceramics (3%), iron and steel (17.5%) and some paper/board (10%). EAC-origin goods are exempt ([GTAI](https://www.gtai.de/de/trade/kenia/zoll/finanzgesetz-kenia-1917564)). This may hit **imported paper sacks and cartons** for feed and oil packaging, so EAC-sourced packaging is preferable.
- The Finance Bill 2024 proposed a **25% excise on vegetable oils**, which was withdrawn with the bill ([People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/news/edible-oil-firms-why-youll-pay-more-for-bread/amp) [DATED]). There is no edible-oil excise in force. It remains a tail risk.

## 2. Direct taxes, incentives and effective tax rate

### 2.1 Corporate income tax and recent Finance Acts

- **CIT is 30%.** The Finance Act 2025 introduced:
  - 0.5% (resident) and 5% (non-resident) WHT on goods supplied to public entities;
  - a **5-year cap on loss carry-forward** (from 1 Jul 2025);
  - advance pricing agreements;
  - a 15% domestic minimum top-up tax for groups with turnover of EUR 750m or more, which is irrelevant here ([KPMG, Jul 2025](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/07/kenya-tax-changes-finance-act-2025.html); prior macro notes).
- The **Finance Act 2026** was assented on 23 Jun 2026. It is mostly base-broadening (WHT on scrap and card fees, sugar import levy, tax amnesty to 31 Dec 2026). **No measure targets edible oil or feed directly**, and the Bill's proposed VAT exemption for animal-feed inputs was **dropped** ([EY](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1445-kenya-enacts-finance-act-2026); [MA Law Africa](https://malawafrica.com/kenya-finance-act-2026-a-comprehensive-legal-analysis-of-the-new-tax-reforms/); [kn.co.ke](https://kn.co.ke/finance-act-2026-kenya-key-tax-changes-and-business-implications/)).
- **[UNVERIFIED – prior knowledge]** The 1% minimum tax on turnover was repealed by the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2024. Confirm, because loss-making start-up years would otherwise bear about 1% of turnover.
- **eTIMS.** Input VAT and expense deductions are allowed only against valid eTIMS invoices ([biasharaguide](https://biasharaguide.co.ke/articles/significant-economic-presence-tax-kenya/)). This bites on **purchases from smallholder seed aggregators**: informal suppliers cannot issue eTIMS invoices, so seed purchases risk being disallowed as a deduction unless self-billing or reverse-invoicing on eTIMS is used. [ESTIMATE] Exposure if unmanaged: 30% × seed cost on the share bought informally. **This is a must-fix compliance item.**

### 2.2 Investment deduction (capital allowances)

- Manufacturing buildings and machinery get **50% in year 1, then 25% a year on the residue** ([PwC summary reviewed Dec 2025](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kenya/corporate/deductions); [CDH FB2025](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/export/sites/cdh/news/publications/2025/Practice/Tax-Exchange-Control/Downloads/Kenya-Finance-Bill-2025.pdf)).
- The rate is **100%** where:
  - (a) cumulative investment over the previous 3 years outside Nairobi and Mombasa counties is at least KES 2bn; or
  - (b) at least KES 250m is invested outside those counties in the year; or
  - (c) the investment is in an SEZ ([KPMG FA2021](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmg/ke/pdf/tax/2021/KPMG%20Analysis%20of%20the%20Finance%20Act%202021%20-%20Final.pdf) [DATED]). Kisumu and Busia qualify geographically.
- The Finance Bill 2025 sought to repeal the 100% tiers ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-introduces-finance-bill-2025)). A post-enactment summary says this "was not adopted" ([Bowmans FA2025 search extract](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/kenya-the-finance-act-2025/)).
- **[CONFLICT]** A secondary summary gives a KES 1bn non-Nairobi/Mombasa tier ([naicents](https://naicents.com/tax/it-4-investment-deductions-and-capital-allowances)). **Action:** obtain a tax opinion on whether a KES 1.5–3bn plant qualifies for 100%. The DCF in §2.6 shows the gain from 100% over 50/25/25 is small when early taxable income is low.
- **Interaction with the 5-year loss cap.** A year-1 deduction of 100% on a slowly ramping plant creates a large loss that must be used within 5 years. In my slow-ramp case (§2.6), about KES 0.2bn of losses would expire on a KES 3bn plant, costing about 0.4 pp of IRR. **[ESTIMATE] Mitigants:** phase capex, claim only what can be absorbed (confirm whether the deduction can be disclaimed), or put the feed mill (earlier cash flow) and the crusher in one entity so profits and losses offset.

### 2.3 Interest, thin capitalisation and FX

- The 30%-of-EBITDA cap applies **only to foreign-debt interest**, with a 3-year carry-forward. FX losses for companies over the threshold carry forward 5 years ([EY FA2023](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/kenya-enacts-tax-changes-under-finance-act--2023) [DATED]; [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.co.ke/insights/interest-restriction-rule/)). **[CONFLICT]** Sources differ on whether the cap applies to all foreign debt or only related-party debt.
- **Implication:** USD loans from DFIs such as AgDevCo, Norfund or IFC are capped at 30% of EBITDA. In ramp-up years with low EBITDA, much of that interest is deferred and could be lost after 3 years. Local KES bank debt at about 14.4% is uncapped.
- Interest paid to non-residents bears 15% WHT, often grossed-up under DFI terms. That adds about 2.6 pp to the effective cost of a USD loan at 15% gross-up on a 15% coupon [ESTIMATE].

### 2.4 SEZ / EPZ route

- **SEZ benefits:**
  - CIT 10% for years 1–10, then the incentive ends under the BLA 2024 cap (assented 11 Dec 2024, in force 27 Dec 2024) ([Bowmans](https://bowmanslaw.com/insights/kenya-amendments-to-the-special-economic-zones-act-under-the-business-laws-amendment-act-2024/) [DATED]; [CDH, Feb 2025](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2025/Practice/Tax-Exchange-Control/tax-and-exchange-control-alert-6-february-Not-so-special-after-all-An-analysis-of-the-impact-of-the-Business-Laws-Amendment-Act-2024-on-taxation-of-Special-Economic-Zones));
  - dividends paid by an SEZ company are exempt from WHT ([PwC WHT](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kenya/corporate/withholding-taxes));
  - supplies *to* SEZ enterprises are zero-rated, and SEZ enterprises need not register for VAT ([PwC incentives](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/kenya/corporate/tax-credits-and-incentives), Dec 2025);
  - 100% investment deduction;
  - exemption from the AFA import levy on EAC-origin oil crops (since 17 Apr 2025).
- **Pending legislation.** The Business Laws (Amendment) Bill 2025 proposes a fixed 10-year SEZ licence and capital gains tax relief on transfers inside SEZs ([CDH, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.cliffedekkerhofmeyr.com/en/news/publications/2025/Practice/Tax-Exchange-Control/tax-and-exchange-control-alert-20-november-kenya-Tax-highlights-from-the-Business-Laws-Amendment-Bill-2025)). An SEZ (Amendment) Bill 2026 would extend SEZ status to petroleum ([Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/kenya-debates-the-sez-amendment-bill-2026-to-grant-oil-firms-tax-free-status/)). Neither is enacted.
- **Catch for a domestic-market plant.** EPZ sales into the EAC customs territory are capped at 20% of output and treated as imports, so duty and taxes apply (EAC Customs Union Protocol Art. 25(3); [TradeMark Africa](https://www.trademarkafrica.com/news/new-push-to-have-more-epz-products-sold-in-east-africa) [DATED]; [DHL guide](https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-ke/logistics-advice/import-export-advice/go-global-with-kenya-s-export-processing-zones)). SEZs were designed to allow domestic sales, but **I found no current source on the customs treatment of SEZ goods sold into Kenya**. [UNVERIFIED – prior knowledge] Such sales are treated as imports into the customs territory. Oil crushed from EAC-origin seed may still enter duty-free as originating goods, but this needs a ruling.
- **Judgement:** the SEZ route is worth about 8 pp of tax on profits for 10 years. It pays only if (i) the domestic-sale treatment is benign, or (ii) a material share of oil and feed is exported to Uganda, Rwanda, South Sudan or DRC. **Seek an advance ruling from the SEZ Authority and KRA before choosing the site.** Note the EAC precedent that SEZ incentives can be cut retrospectively: the 10-year cap applied to existing licensees.

### 2.6 Effective tax rate: illustrative DCF [ESTIMATE]

**Inputs.**
- Capex is KES 3.0bn at t=0, scaled for illustration; the ratios below are scale-invariant.
- EBITDA ramps KES 0.3, 0.6, 0.8 and 0.9bn, then holds flat for 15 years.

- The script is in the session scratchpad. Results are directional only.

| Regime | Pre-tax IRR | Post-tax IRR (0% / 5% dividend WHT) | Tax wedge on IRR | Nominal CIT over 15 yrs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No incentive (12.5% straight-line), 30% CIT | 22.4% | 18.7% / 17.7% | 17% / 21% | KES 2.85bn |
| 50/25/25 investment deduction, 30% CIT | 22.4% | 19.2% / 18.2% | 14% / 18% | 2.85bn |

**Takeaways:**
1. The statutory rate on distributed profit is **33.5%** for a resident individual and **40.5%** for a non-resident 15%-WHT investor.
2. On a project-IRR basis, accelerated deductions cut the tax wedge to about **14–18%**. A pre-tax IRR of about 45% becomes roughly 37–39% post-tax.
3. The choice between 100% and 50/25/25 hardly matters when early EBITDA is low. **Loss expiry under the 5-year cap matters more.**
4. SEZ status roughly halves the wedge, *if* domestic sales are not penalised.

### 3.2 Recommended sequence and critical path [ESTIMATE]

1. **Months 0–2.** Incorporate (holdco plus opco), register for eTIMS and get an investment certificate. Shortlist 2–3 sites: an industrial plot in Kisumu, Busia or Mumias, or an SEZ plot. Commission legal due diligence and a **tax opinion on investment deduction eligibility and SEZ domestic sales**. Open AFA dealer registration early so seed buying can start for trials.
2. **Months 1–6.** Secure the land: lease ≥33 years (99 if foreign equity is expected) and get LCB consent or change of user. **File the NEMA EIA full study** as soon as the site and layout are fixed. Apply for WRA permits and the power connection at the same time.
3. **Months 5–9.** County building approval, NCA registration, start of construction. Book KEPHIS permits for seed imports.
4. **Months 12–18.** Commissioning. Run DOSH, the effluent discharge and air licences, AFA manufacturer inspection, and **KEBS marks on trial batches** (budget 2–4 months, which delays the start of sales of each SKU). Register as a feed manufacturer.
5. **Ongoing.** Apply for EAC duty-remission quotas on feed premixes and fishmeal for each 1 July cycle; applications are due before the EAC budget in about April [UNVERIFIED]. Make annual renewals (AFA, KEBS, county). Monitor the Livestock Bill 2026 and the GMO appeal.

**Total time from site selection to licensed operation:** about **15–21 months**. Of this, regulatory steps sit on the critical path for about 6–9 months, mostly EIA and power. A Lake Victoria shoreline site adds KeFS, KMA and WRA riparian review, plus exposure to the Senate inquiry. **Do not site the plant on the shoreline.** Put it inland, 10–30 km from the cage clusters.

## 4. Sector-specific regulation and policy

### 4.1 VAT mechanics for an integrated crusher and feed mill

- **Oil sales (16% output VAT)** allow full recovery of input VAT on the crusher. Seed bought from smallholders carries no VAT [UNVERIFIED – prior knowledge: unprocessed oilseeds are exempt or out of scope]. The crusher therefore pays net VAT on its value-add, about 16% of (oil price − seed cost attributed to oil).
- **Cake sold to third-party feed millers** is zero-rated as an "input or raw material for the manufacture of animal feeds" ([Feed Business MEA, 26 May 2026](https://www.feedbusinessmea.com/2026/05/26/finance-bill-2026-how-proposed-tax-shifts-will-impact-feed-industry-value-chain/)). This creates a refund position on that share. **KRA refund delays are a working-capital risk** [ESTIMATE: 6–18 months historically].
- **Finished feed** is exempt or zero-rated (**[CONFLICT]**, row 18). If exempt, input VAT on overheads is irrecoverable and must be apportioned (row 19). If zero-rated, there is a refund position. Feed is sold to farmers who are mostly not VAT-registered, so a move to **16% standard-rating** would raise farm-gate feed prices about 16% and cut volumes. This was never proposed. P ≈ 5–10% by 2030 [ESTIMATE].
- **A recurring proposal to move feed inputs from zero-rated to "exempt upon CS recommendation"** appeared in the Finance Bills 2025 and 2026 and was dropped both times (row 17). If enacted, crushers selling cake would lose input-VAT recovery on the cake share, and every input would need ministerial sign-off, which adds friction. Exposure [ESTIMATE]: about 1–2% of cake revenue.
- **Tax litigation over feed classification is live.** In Jul 2025 the High Court ordered KRA to refund about USD 0.16m to Animix, finding no basis to treat its feed imports as general-rated ([Feed Business MEA](https://www.feedbusinessmea.com/2025/07/11/kenya-high-court-orders-kra-to-refund-us0-16m-to-animal-feed-firm/)). Obtain a **KRA private ruling** on the VAT classification of each product line.

### 4.2 Edible-oil quality rules

- Vitamin A fortification is mandatory (KS EAS 769). A KEBS certificate of compliance is a prerequisite for renewal ([ATNi, Jul 2025](https://accesstonutrition.org/app/uploads/2025/07/20250701_ATNi_EAMA_Kenya_Micronutrients_v2.pdf)).
- Crude oil sold to refiners needs AFA and KEBS conformity, but fortification is done by the refiner. **[ESTIMATE]** Selling *crude* oil to refiners (Bidco, Pwani, Kapa, Menengai) avoids consumer-brand regulation but puts the plant at the mercy of the refiners' oligopsony. The COMESA Competition Commission alleged KES 67.7bn of overcharging by refiners ([Khusoko, Dec 2024](https://khusoko.com/2024/12/11/oil-firms-overcharged-kenyans-by-ksh-67-7-billion/) [DATED]).

### 4.3 GMO feed ingredients: legal status, Oct 2026

- **Timeline:**
  - Cabinet lifted the 2012 GMO ban on 3 Oct 2022.
  - The ELC dismissed challenges in 2023 and the High Court dismissed further petitions in Nov 2024.
  - **On 7 Mar 2025 the Court of Appeal issued conservatory orders barring government from allowing GMO imports** pending the Kenya Peasants League appeal ([Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/blow-to-govt-as-court-of-appeal-bars-importation-of-gmos-n358801); [OFI Magazine](https://www.ofimagazine.com/news/kenyas-court-of-appeal-issues-injunction-on-genetically-engineered-products)).
  - The appeal was argued in Dec 2025. Judgment was expected on **24 Apr 2026 but has been delayed**, and the orders remain in force ([Biosafety Information Centre](https://biosafety-info.net/articles/policy-and-regulation/africa/court-of-appeal-halts-government-promotion-of-gmos-in-landmark-ruling/), single source; [Via Campesina, Dec 2025](https://viacampesina.org/en/2025/12/we-are-not-against-science-but-want-choices-that-protect-our-livelihoods-kpl-struggle-against-gmo-in-kenya/)).
  - In Jul 2026 feed millers described GMO imports as still suspended ([The Star, Jul 2026](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-07-17-feed-industry-calls-for-reforms-to-make-animal-food-affordable)).
- **Implications:**
  - (a) GM SBM from Argentina, Brazil or the US cannot legally be imported. Non-GM protein (EAC soy, Zambian/Malawian SBM, sunflower cake) earns a premium, which favours the crusher's cake.
  - (b) Whichever side loses at the Court of Appeal can go to the Supreme Court, so final resolution may take until 2027–28 [ESTIMATE].
  - (c) If GM imports open, cake prices fall toward GM SBM parity (about KES 67–72/kg vs about KES 95/kg non-GM, per [feedstock_supply.md](feedstock_supply.md)), but the feed line's input costs also fall. **An integrated plant is partly hedged.**
  - (d) **Never accept GM-contaminated seed or meal**, given the possibility of criminal liability under the Biosafety Act 2009 [UNVERIFIED – prior knowledge]. Test incoming soy (strip tests) and keep identity-preservation records.

### 4.4 Feed regulation in flux

- The **Livestock Bill 2026** (first reading 13 Oct 2026) would create a livestock-inputs regulator covering the manufacture, import, distribution and sale of feeds ([Animal Business MEA](https://www.animalbusinessmea.com/2026/10/15/kenya-introduces-livestock-bill-to-regulate-animal-feeds/); [Uasin Gishu News](https://www.uasingishunews.co.ke/livestock-sector-set-for-major-boost-as-new-bill-is-introduced-in-national-assembly/)). The 2024 version proposed fines of KES 500,000 and registration duties ([Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/103130-details-new-bill-proposing-5-new-learning-institutions-and-ksh500k-fine-livestock) [DATED]) and a livestock fund ([Sacco Review](https://saccoreview.co.ke/livestock-bill-proposes-new-fund-regulator-and-leather-body/)).
- **[ESTIMATE]** A new regulator usually brings a **levy on feed turnover**. Model 0–1% of feed revenue from 2028, P ≈ 40%.
- Stricter enforcement is net positive for a formal, tested mill. KEBS found only 1 of 17 sampled feed brands fully compliant ([Milling MEA](https://millingmea.com/kenya-bureau-of-standards-warns-of-non-compliant-animal-feeds-amid-increasing-production-costs/), date unclear).

### 4.5 Lake Victoria cage farming and the fish-feed market

- **Senate petition:**
  - Homa Bay hoteliers petitioned the Senate on 5 Aug 2026, through Senator Moses Kajwang', to pause new cage approvals in tourism areas pending zoning and to relocate cages blocking beaches and navigation routes ([SeafoodSource](https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/kenya-s-senate-petitioned-to-control-the-spread-of-cage-fish-farming-in-lake-victoria); [Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/business/hoteliers-protest-over-chaotic-cage-fish-farms-in-l-victoria-5553600)).
  - The Senate Agriculture Committee held hearings in Homa Bay on 2 Oct 2026 ([People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/news/lake-victoria-fish-cage-crisis-senate-launches-probe-over-pollution-blocked-beaches-and-tourism-losses)).
  - **No moratorium had been adopted** as of mid-Oct 2026.
- **Pollution narrative.** Fish kills in Oct 2026 were blamed on pollution ([KNA, 1 Oct 2026](https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/pollution-blamed-for-mass-fish-deaths-in-l-victoria/)). Cage waste is cited by petitioners.
- **Fees.** The 5% landed-value levy and the KES 50,000 licence are upheld but under appeal ([Dawan](https://www.dawan.africa/news/18-months-on-fish-levy-standoff-rattles-investor-confidence-in-kenyas-blue-economy)). The CS's taskforce compromise has not been implemented.
- **Carrying capacity.**
  - KMFRI estimates about 275,000 t/yr production capacity for riparian counties under best practice, and about 25,427 cages, against roughly 7,000 today ([WAS 2025](https://www.was.org/Meeting/Program/PaperDetail/166835); [NORA](https://nora.nerc.ac.uk/id/eprint/530395)).
  - The spatial plan delineates 291 km² (8% of the Kenyan lake) as inshore aquaculture zones.
  - **[ESTIMATE]** Physical headroom is roughly 3–10× current output of 28,100 t. Growth will be capped by social licence and permitting rather than biology. Zoning is more likely than a ban. It would slow approvals in Homa Bay tourism areas and push expansion to Siaya, Busia and Migori.
- **Demand-side protection.** Excise on imported fish (10% or KES 100k/t) cut Chinese tilapia imports from 4,228 t (2022) to 1,928 t (2023) ([EastAfrican](https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/business/kenya-slashes-chinese-tilapia-imports-by-half-4520278) [DATED]). This supports local tilapia prices and so feed demand.
- **Implication.** Do **not** make the business plan depend on owning cages. Sell feed to many farms, including Victory Farms' competitors and smallholder clusters, and diversify into poultry, dairy and pig feed. Victory Farms makes about 90% of its own feed (prior notes), so it is not the target customer.

### 4.6 Effluent and environmental standards

- Crushing and refining effluent carries oil and grease, high BOD/COD and soapstock. Discharge needs an EDL and must meet the Fourth Schedule limits for discharge to the environment, or the Fifth Schedule for discharge to sewer ([Kenya Law LN 120/2006](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2006/120/eng@2022-12-31) [DATED]; superseded by LN 177/2024, [Kenya Law](https://new.kenyalaw.org/akn/ke/act/ln/2024/177/eng%402025-03-24)). Six-monthly monitoring returns are required.
- Numeric limits were not retrieved. **[UNVERIFIED – prior knowledge]** The 2006 schedule set BOD 30 mg/L, COD 50 mg/L and oil and grease "nil" for discharge to the environment.
- **[ESTIMATE]** Budget an effluent plant (DAF plus biological treatment) at KES 40–80m for a 20,000 t/yr crusher. If a mechanical-press-only design (no hexane extraction) is chosen, it also cuts air-licence and fire-safety burdens.

## 5. Land

- Under Art. 65, non-citizens hold land only on leasehold of up to 99 years. Freehold held by foreigners converts to a 99-year lease. In Jan 2025 Lands CS Wahome directed foreigners to surrender freehold titles for conversion ([People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/news/state-bars-foreigners-from-holding-freehold-lands-title-deeds/amp); [Roedl](https://roedl.com/insights/foreign-ownership-land-kenya)).
- **[CONFLICT]** A company counts as a "citizen" only if *wholly* owned by Kenyan citizens, per most sources. Others refer to any non-citizen shareholder ([Chambers Real Estate 2025](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/view/826355218/571)).
- Agricultural land transfers to foreigners need a presidential exemption under the Land Control Act (US State Dept ICS 2026, prior notes).
- **Implication.** The founder is a Kenyan citizen, so the opco can hold freehold *until* a foreign DFI or PE investor takes equity. At that point leasehold rules apply. **Hold land in a separate, wholly Kenyan-owned PropCo leasing to the OpCo on a 33–49-year lease**, or lease from an SEZ developer. This also ring-fences land value from operating risk. [ESTIMATE] The cost is a modest stamp duty and lease-registration charge (stamp duty on leases is about 1–2% of rent capitalised [UNVERIFIED]).
- **Outgrower land.** Smallholder seed contracts involve no land transfer and do not trigger the Land Control Act. Nucleus-estate leases of agricultural land do require LCB consent.
