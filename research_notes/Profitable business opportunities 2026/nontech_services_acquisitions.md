## Q1. What do BizBuySell, IBBA/Pepperdine, Stanford GSB and SBA data say about deal volumes, multiples and returns in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Main Street deal volume is flat to falling in 2026, down about 10% YoY in BizBuySell's Q2 2026 data. Multiples are sticky, at about 2.6–2.7x SDE for the median Main Street business and about 4x EBITDA in the $2–5M band. SBA 7(a) volume fell by double digits in FY2026 after a record FY2025, as the SBA tightened underwriting in three rounds: SOP 50 10 8 in June 2025, a citizenship rule in March 2026 and an FY2027 credit box in October 2026. Search-fund returns remain strong on paper (33.9% IRR, 4.75x across 862 US/Canada funds), but they are heavily skewed to the top decile.

### Cited Findings

**BizBuySell (Main Street, typically less than $2M price)**
- Q2 2026: 2,117 businesses changed hands, down 10% QoQ and YoY, with $1.8B total enterprise value. The average cash-flow multiple rose 2% YoY to 2.7x and the revenue multiple held at about 0.7x. Median sale price was $349,250 (−1% YoY), median revenue $692,087 (−3%) and median cash flow $155,921 (−3%) — [Sundance FG summary of BizBuySell Q2 2026](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report); [BizBuySell Insight Report](https://www.bizbuysell.com/insight-report/)
- Services were the largest segment, at 40% of all Q2 2026 transactions, with a median sale price of about $350,000. Manufacturing's median price fell 10% to $704,500 — [Sundance FG](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)
- Restaurants: Q2 2026 transactions fell 12% YoY and the median price fell 12% to $205,000, yet the cash-flow multiple hit a record 2.41x — [BizBuySell restaurant analysis](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/article/restaurant-industry-analysis/)
- Another broker reported the average cash-flow multiple held at 2.65x between Q1 and Q2 2026. The difference from 2.7x is a rounding/basis issue — [My Florida Business Broker](https://www.myfloridabusinessbroker.com/what-we-know-about-business-sales-after-two-quarters-of-2026/)
- Brokers report "increased buyer selectivity in more vulnerable industries (e.g. more discretionary, lower margin)" — [Sundance FG](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)
- Full-year 2025: transactions were flat and sale prices rose about 2%. Q1–Q2 2025 dipped, then Q3 2025 jumped 8%. BizBuySell's headline framing was "Business Acquisitions Stabilize as Buyers Get Selective: AI and Cost Discipline Drive 2026 Playbook" — [BizBuySell Q4 2025 Insight Report](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/bizbuysell-2025-fourth-quarter-insight-report/)
- Secondary aggregators cite an all-sector average of about 2.58x SDE and a median price of about $340K from BizBuySell data — [Hyperresearch](https://hyperresearch.ai/guides/small-business-valuation-multiples). BizBuySell's industry-multiples dataset covers Q3 2021 to Q2 2026 — [BizBuySell industry multiples](https://www.bizbuysell.com/learning-center/industry-valuation-multiples/)

### Inferences
- The Main Street market is in a "price-stable, volume-down" regime. Sellers are not cutting multiples, but fewer deals close. That is consistent with tighter SBA credit and longer time-to-close (Pepperdine +46%).
- Size arbitrage persists, from about 2–2.7x SDE under $1M to about 4x EBITDA at $2–5M to 5.5–6.5x above $5M. Buying several small firms and assembling a $3–5M+ EBITDA platform can create multiple expansion on paper even with flat sector multiples. That is the core logic of the search/roll-up model.
- The Pepperdine "$5M inflection" (capital-scarce below $5M EBITDA) is where individual searchers and SBA buyers face the least institutional competition.
- Search-fund returns are a power-law. The median outcome is much closer to about 2.8x/27% IRR, or lower for funds that never acquire (about 42–52% fail to acquire).

## Q2. How have SBA 7(a) rules, seller notes and interest rates changed acquisition financing (2025–2026)?

### Takeaway
The SBA has tightened three times since June 2025. Together the changes make deals harder to finance: buyers need real cash equity, seller notes count toward equity only on full standby, green-card holders are excluded, and acquisitions over about $3M now need a QoE report. Credit is also expensive, at roughly Prime 6.75% + 2.25–3% on 7(a) loans.

### Cited Findings
- **SOP 50 10 8 (effective June 1, 2025):** it set a 10% minimum equity injection for startups and full changes of ownership. Seller debt counts toward the injection only if it is on full standby and no more than 50% of the required injection — [Whiteford Taylor & Preston](https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-sba-issues-sop-50-10-8-key-changes-impacting-sba-7a-lending); [ClearlyAcquired](https://www.clearlyacquired.com/blog/major-sop-50-10-8-sba-changes-effective-june-1-2025----what-they-mean-for-business-buyers)
- Full standby means no principal or interest payments for the entire life of the SBA loan (typically 10 years), replacing the old 24-month standby practice — [MyReins](https://www.myreins.com/blog/sba-changes-sop-50-10-8-update); [Regalis Capital](https://learn.regaliscapital.com/guides/seller-financing-seller-notes-new-sba-rules/)
- Sellers retaining any ownership (rollover) must personally guarantee the loan, which pushes deals toward stock sales — [MyReins](https://www.myreins.com/blog/sba-changes-sop-50-10-8-update)
- The 7(a) Small Loan ceiling was cut from $500K to $350K, and the collateral threshold dropped from $500K to $50K — [Whiteford](https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-sba-issues-sop-50-10-8-key-changes-impacting-sba-7a-lending)
- **Citizenship rule (effective March 1, 2026):** 7(a) and 504 are open only to businesses 100% owned by US citizens or nationals, so lawful permanent residents are now excluded. The rule is not retroactive — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/sba-re-tightens-its-eligibility-rules-hitting-noncitizens); [BRG](https://www.thinkbrg.com/insights/publications/sba-citizenship-eligibility-changes-implications-for-lenders-and-operational-considerations/)
- **FY2027 credit box (from October 1, 2026):** a second round of tightening. It requires a CPA quality-of-earnings report for acquisitions over $3M, stricter debt-service standards, a 50% cap on non-cash equity sources and trailing-earnings (historical) underwriting. These details come from republished and secondary summaries of an October 9, 2026 American Banker story — [Novo (republished American Banker)](https://www.novo.co/small-business-news/sba-starts-new-fiscal-year-with-even-tighter-credit-box); [eCommerce Paradise](https://ecommerceparadise.com/sba-7a-loans-fall-credit-box/)
- One vendor blog labels this "SOP 50 10 8.1" (October 1, 2026), with a 1.25x DSCR floor on historical cash flow for acquisitions. This is single-source and unconfirmed — [AcquisitionAce](https://www.acquisitionace.io/learn/sba-loans/sop-changes-2026)
- American Banker reports that the new eligibility rules are expected to push loans toward nonbank lenders — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/sbas-new-eligibility-rules-expected-to-funnel-loans-to-nonbanks)
- **Rates:** prime has been 6.75% since the Fed's December 11, 2025 cut. The Fed funds target was held at 3.50–3.75% in June 2026. The October 27–28, 2026 FOMC outcome was not yet known as of research date, and pre-meeting odds were roughly even. Typical 7(a) pricing is Prime + 2.25–3.0%, or about 9.5–11.75% — [Beancount guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/26/fed-rate-path-business-loan-prime-rate-guide); [PrimeRates.com forecast](https://primerates.com/prime-rate-forecast-2026/)

### Inferences
- A 10% cash equity requirement, with seller notes limited to full standby, roughly doubles the cash an SBA buyer must bring compared with 2021–24 practices. That is about $150–250K cash on a $1.5–2.5M deal, which reduces the buyer pool for sub-$5M businesses. Sellers in that band face fewer buyers, which may support buyer leverage on price and terms, consistent with the IBBA Q2 2026 comment.
- Debt service at about 10–11% on 10-year amortization absorbs most free cash flow at 3x SDE. A rough rule: at 3.0x SDE with 90% leverage, annual debt service is about 45–50% of SDE before the buyer's salary. Thin margins of safety favor recurring-revenue businesses.
- Seller notes not on standby, earnouts and non-SBA lenders (nonbanks, family offices, independent sponsors) are likely to fill the gap.

### Gaps
- The official SBA SOP text for the FY2027 changes was not read directly.
- Default/charge-off trends for 7(a) acquisition loans in 2025–26 were not found.
- Current SBA maximum allowable spreads were not verified against SBA.gov.

## Q3. Which industries are saturated by PE roll-ups and which remain under-penetrated? (with multiples)

### Takeaway
Vet, dental, HVAC/plumbing, pest control, insurance brokerage, car washes and (increasingly) accounting are heavily consolidated. In these sectors, platforms bid up quality multi-provider assets and discount small, owner-dependent ones. Commercial cleaning, laundromats, funeral homes (beyond SCI), and smaller trade niches remain lower-multiple and less institutionalized. Vet multiples peaked in 1H 2025 and have plateaued.

### Cited Findings
- HVAC, dental and vet are described as "mid-cycle," with 3–5 dominant platforms competing for add-ons. First add-ons in a new region get premium multiples, while later add-ons in saturated regions get lower ones. DSOs are in a "maturity wave" of consolidation among existing platforms rather than new platform creation — [CT Acquisitions roll-up guide](https://ctacquisitions.com/roll-up-strategy-guide-2026/); [CT Acquisitions PE platforms by sector (288 US roll-ups across 32 sectors)](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/private-equity-platforms-by-sector-2026/)
- **Veterinary:** Ackerman's weighted-average general-practice multiple was 12.4x in 2H 2025, down from the 1H 2025 peak of 12.5x (a plateau). Ackerman warns that lower recap multiples could pull down practice multiples and notes that no successful platform recaps followed the Mission Pet Health deal within 12 months — [Ackerman Group Q4 2025](https://ackerman-group.com/owner-education/market-trends/quarterly-update/veterinary-practice-sales-2025-q4-veterinary-industry-market-update/)
- Closed vet deals from 2024 to Q1 2026 settled about 15–20% below the 2020–22 peak on a multiple basis (broker marketing claim) — [CT Acquisitions vet multiples](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/veterinary-practice-ma-multiples-2026/). Mars and other consolidators pay full price for clean multi-doctor assets but compress offers on solo, owner-dependent or rural practices — [Adastra Equity](https://www.adastraequity.com/ebitda-multiples/veterinary-practice). Single-doctor practices trade at 2.5–4.5x SDE — [Axia Growth](https://axiagrowth.com/resources/industries/healthcare/veterinary-practices)
- The FTC has taken multiple actions against JAB requiring divestitures, a sign of antitrust scrutiny of vet roll-ups — [CT Acquisitions PE vet report](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/private-equity-veterinary-2026/)
- **HVAC:** bolt-ons trade at 5–7x EBITDA and sub-$1M SDE businesses at 3–5x SDE. A cited Blackstone–Champions deal (February 2026) at about 18.5x EBITDA looks like an outlier platform deal — [CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/which-industries-pe-buying-most-2026/); [CT Acquisitions SDE multiples](https://ctacquisitions.com/sellers-discretionary-earnings-multiplier-by-industry/); [Home Services M&A Multiples Report 2026](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/home-services-ma-multiples-report-2026/)
- **Dental:** bolt-ons at 5–9x EBITDA for practices with $1M+ EBITDA. Another page from the same site says 8–14x for quality DSO assets — [CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/roll-up-strategy-case-studies-2026/); [CT Acquisitions healthcare M&A](https://ctacquisitions.com/healthcare-ma-trends-2026/)
- **Pest control:** 5–10x. 6–8x applies with 80%+ recurring revenue, and other sources cite 7–10x at 70–80% recurring. PE-backed buyers are about 60% of pest-control transactions — [Breakwater M&A](https://www.breakwaterma.com/blog/pest-control-company-valuation-multiples-2026); [Praxis Rock](https://praxisrock.com/insights/ebitda-multiples-by-industry)
- **Insurance brokerage:** about 8–14x EBITDA. One source cites a 16.7x 2022–25 average, now "showing signs of moderation" or expected flat in 2026. About 50 PE-backed or public brokers bid for each agency — [Praxis Rock](https://praxisrock.com/insights/ebitda-multiples-by-industry); [CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/ebitda-multiples-by-industry-2026/)
- **Commercial cleaning/janitorial:** the lowest multiple segment, at 3.0–5.5x EBITDA (4.0x median). Thin margins and high competition drag on valuations, and 80%+ contract revenue earns a premium — [Breakwater M&A](https://www.breakwaterma.com/blog-test/blog/cleaning-janitorial-company-valuation-multiples-2026)
- **Car wash:** single sites trade at 4–8x capex-adjusted EBITDA, regional chains at 8–12x and PE express platforms at 11–18x — [WashIndex](https://washindex.com/car-wash-valuation-multiples). Lower middle market sites are at 4–7x, with premium express tunnels at 6.5–7x+ — [Acquidex Q1 2026](https://acquidex.com/intel/car-wash/atlas/q1-2026). PE roll-ups have driven tunnel multiples up 20–40% over five years, with 50+ active roll-ups and $750M+ deployed in 2024 alone **[2024 data]** — [DealSeam car wash PE tracker](https://dealseam.com/car-wash-pe-rollup-tracker-2026); [CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/how-to-buy-a-car-wash/)
- **Laundromats:** SBA buyers pay 2.5–3.5x SDE, while consolidators pay 4–5.5x SDE or 5–6x EBITDA — [CT Acquisitions laundromat valuation](https://ctacquisitions.com/laundromat-business-valuation/)
- **Funeral:** SCI holds about 16% US share. Other consolidators include Carriage Services, Park Lawn, Foundation Partners (Access Holdings) and StoneMor (Axar). No 2026 multiple data were found — [CT Acquisitions roll-up guide](https://ctacquisitions.com/roll-up-strategy-guide-2026/)
- **Accounting/CPA:** PE holds stakes in 11 of the 30 largest US firms and has put more than $50B into CPA firms over six years (citing KPMG Corporate Finance). PE-backed accounting transactions totaled 183 in 2025, vs. 76 in 2024 and 47 in 2023. PE targets firms with $2–50M revenue, while smaller firms go to individuals or roll-ups — [Jupid](https://jupid.com/blog/who-is-buying-accounting-firms-2026); [Adastra Equity](https://www.adastraequity.com/blog/private-equity-accounting-firm-consolidation). One IFAC study reportedly counts 177 direct PE investments in accounting firms from 2015 to 2025, which led to 875 roll-up acquisitions (secondary; not verified) — [Footnote](https://footnote.news/p/private-equity-bet-accounting-was-a-fortress-ai-is-lowering-the-walls)
- **Macro PE context:** Bain's 2026 report (secondary summary) puts 2025 global M&A-exit value up 47% to $717B and dry powder at $1.3T. Headline buyout value excluding add-ons was $904B (+44%). Bain says the recovery is concentrated among the largest investors — [iTiger summary](https://www.itiger.com/news/1106650035); [Bain podcast page](https://www.bain.com/es/insights/bain-and-companys-global-private-equity-report-2026-executive-summary-podcast/). Q2 2026 PE deal activity declined on geopolitical uncertainty and tighter financing, with 329 exits — [Aranca Global PE Factbook Q2 2026 (PitchBook data)](https://www.aranca.com/assets/docs/Global-Private-Equity-Factbook-Q2-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Saturated (hard to win as an individual or search buyer, and multiple-expansion arbitrage is mostly arbitraged away):** vet, dental, insurance brokerage, pest control, express car wash, and HVAC/plumbing in Sun Belt metros. The opportunity in these sectors is selling to platforms, or buying the small "orphan" practices (solo vet, sub-$1M dental, rural HVAC) that platforms discount at 2.5–5x SDE and professionalizing them.
- **Less penetrated or still fragmented (relative opportunity):** commercial cleaning and facilities maintenance (low multiples, contract revenue), small CPA/bookkeeping firms below PE's $2M revenue floor, laundromats, independent funeral homes, niche trades (garage doors, fencing, septic, backflow testing, fire and life-safety inspection, elevator, commercial roofing service), and B2B route-based services. *Note: niche-trade examples are the researcher's inference; no 2026 source quantified their penetration.*
- Vet's flat 12.4x and the stalled platform recaps suggest the top of the cycle in the most mature healthcare roll-ups. Entry at platform multiples now carries real multiple-compression risk at exit.

### Gaps
- No PitchBook add-on share of buyouts or sector-level PE deal counts for 2025–26 were obtained (paywalled).
- Market sizes and fragmentation (e.g., share of firms with fewer than 20 employees) by sector from Census SUSB/ABS were not retrieved.
- No reliable 2026 multiples for self-storage, funeral homes, physical therapy, home health, childcare, tutoring or franchise resales.
- Most multiples come from M&A-advisor marketing pages, not deal databases (GF Data, DealStats). Primary verification is needed.

## Q4. How are labor shortages, AI back-office adoption, interest rates and tariffs affecting profitability?

### Takeaway
In HVAC and other trades, tariffs and the R-454B/A2L refrigerant transition have pushed equipment and labor prices up. So far contractors are passing these through, which supports margins. Technician shortages cap growth more than they cap pricing. Care businesses (home health, childcare) face a squeeze from both stagnant Medicaid reimbursement and wage pressure. AI is being used as an explicit thesis in accounting roll-ups (Thrive/Current, Modus), and is otherwise mostly anecdotal in non-tech services.

### Cited Findings
- **Tariffs, HVAC:** manufacturers are passing tariff costs through via surcharges. Trane's CFO said pricing "has not faded," and Lennox forecast about 2.5% cost inflation in 2026 from tariff carryovers. These quotes come via HVAC industry blogs, not filings — [Paschal 2026 HVAC price increases](https://gopaschal.com/resources/2026-hvac-price-increases/); [Jobber HVAC trends](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/hvac/hvac-industry-trends/). One blog claims tariffs add 15–30% to equipment cost before it reaches the supply house (unverified) — [HVAC Know It All](https://hvacknowitall.com/blog/2026-hvac-market-correction-survival-guide-for-techs-and-contractors)
- **R-454B/A2L transition:** estimates say redesign raised manufacturing costs about 15–20% and homeowner prices about 8–10%. Another source says R-454B pricing spiked up to 42% vs. R-410A. A2L-certified contractors were billing labor 18–30% above pre-transition rates by mid-2026, and retooling costs about $5–10K per truck — [Leadnox](https://leadnox.io/blog-hvac-a2l-price-objection); [AC Direct](https://www.acdirect.com/blog/why-hvac-costs-more-than-5-years-ago/); [HVAC Pricing Guide](https://hvacpricingguide.com/why-are-hvac-prices-so-high-2026/)
- **Technician shortage:** a shortfall of about 110,000 HVAC technicians, with about 25,000 leaving per year (ServiceTitan analysis, cited secondhand). Other estimates range from about 50,000 to "100K+ unfilled positions," so the true size is uncertain — [Call The Local](https://callthelocal.com/guides/skilled-trades-shortage-homeowner-problem-2026); [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/academy/hvac/hvac-industry-trends/)
- One blog names PE consolidation of HVAC contractors as a factor raising margins — [HVAC Calculator Hub](https://hvaccalculatorhub.com/blog/why-hvac-prices-rising-2026)
- **Home and community-based care:** about 95% of providers report moderate or severe staffing shortages and 77% have turned away referrals. About 700,000 people are on Medicaid home-care waitlists (KFF) — [TCF Care Matters 2026](https://tcf.org/content/report/care-matters-a-2026-state-report-card-on-care-affordability-and-access/); [New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/207658/cuts-medicaid-home-health-care). Medicaid home-care wages have stagnated at about $11–12/hour — [Penn LDI](https://ldi.upenn.edu/our-work/research-updates/how-medicaid-cuts-could-force-millions-into-nursing-homes/)
- The 2025 federal law cuts federal Medicaid by about $911B over 10 years (other estimates say about $1T). At least 18 states have proposed or enacted HCBS cuts since, and ARPA wage-support funds have expired in most states — [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-caregiving-crisis-and-the-2026-vote/); [CareBravo (vendor)](https://carebravo.com/learn/home-care-industry-trends)
- **Childcare:** providers "operate on the slimmest of profit margins," early educators are paid poverty-level wages, and the report argues SNAP and Medicaid cuts will squeeze families — [TCF Care Matters 2026](https://tcf.org/content/report/care-matters-a-2026-state-report-card-on-care-affordability-and-access/)
- **AI in accounting:** Thrive Holdings is committing $1B (per Forbes, as relayed by secondary sources) to buy local accounting practices and "rewire them with AI." Its platform Current has about 50 practices, and OpenAI took a stake and lent a team to train tax agents. Another source cites a $500M two-year budget, so the figures conflict — [Jupid](https://jupid.com/blog/who-is-buying-accounting-firms-2026); [Footnote](https://footnote.news/p/private-equity-bet-accounting-was-a-fortress-ai-is-lowering-the-walls). Modus raised $85M (Lightspeed) to take stakes in top-200 CPA firms and deploy AI, with at least five new partner-firm investments planned for 2026 — [Crossing](https://crossing.one/blog/modus-ai-accounting-firm-85m-raise-small-firms-2026)
- Tax software, AI bookkeeping and offshoring are compressing margins on billable-hour compliance work. "A well-run AI roll-up becomes very hard for a twelve-person firm to compete with on price" (opinion) — [Jupid](https://jupid.com/blog/who-is-buying-accounting-firms-2026); [Nexairi](https://nexairi.com/article/Accounting/pe-ai-squeeze-small-cpa-firm-three-paths-2026/)
- BizBuySell framed 2026 buyer playbooks around "AI and cost discipline" — [BizBuySell Q4 2025](https://www.bizbuysell.com/news/bizbuysell-2025-fourth-quarter-insight-report/)
- **Rates:** see Q2. Pepperdine reports a median $1M bank loan rate of 8.2% and PE target IRRs of 20–25% — [Chinook Advisors](https://chinookadvisors.com/news/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-pepperdine-private-capital-markets-report/)

### Inferences
- In trades, regulated cost shocks like A2L and tariffs have been margin-neutral to positive for well-run contractors, because demand for replacement HVAC is non-discretionary and competitors face the same costs. The binding constraint is technician capacity. Acquirers who bring recruiting and training engines (apprenticeship, sign-on pipelines) gain an edge.
- Medicaid-dependent care (personal care, Medicaid home health, subsidized childcare) faces payer risk in 2026–28. Private-pay senior care (non-medical home care for affluent seniors) and employer-sponsored or private-pay childcare are safer exposures.
- AI levers most plausible for acquirers of non-tech services: AI receptionists/dispatch and call booking in home services, automated bookkeeping/AP/AR, tax-prep and audit workpaper automation in CPA firms, claims and renewals processing in insurance agencies, and scheduling/route optimization in cleaning and pest. Only accounting has well-capitalized, publicly reported AI-roll-up vehicles so far. *This list is the researcher's inference; no quantified 2026 margin-uplift data were found.*

### Gaps
- No primary BLS 2025/2026 OES or Employment Projections figures were retrieved for HVAC techs, plumbers, electricians or home-health aides. A BLS-based home-health-aide base-pay figure of $16.82/hour (2024) **[DATED]** appeared only in a secondary snippet.
- No quantified data on AI-driven margin improvement in acquired service businesses.
- No BizBuySell survey data on buyer or seller tariff sentiment for 2026 were found.

## Q5. What demographic trends create durable demand and supply of businesses for sale?

### Takeaway
On the supply side, more than half of US small-business owners are over 55, and McKinsey projects about 6M SMB ownership transitions by 2035 (more than 1M sellable, up to $5T of EV). Most owners lack succession plans, so many businesses close instead of transferring. On the demand side, aging drives care and health services, though labor and payer constraints apply.

### Cited Findings
- More than half of US small-business owners are over 55. About 6M SMBs face ownership transitions by 2035, and more than 1M are viable sale candidates, representing up to $5T in enterprise value — [McKinsey, The Great Ownership Transfer](https://www.mckinsey.com/institute-for-economic-mobility/our-insights/the-great-ownership-transfer-a-new-era-of-business-stewardship); [McKinsey chart](https://www.mckinsey.com/featured-insights/week-in-charts/boomers-and-the-business-baton)
- A Guidant Financial figure puts the share of small businesses run by owners over 55 at about 41% (a different measure from McKinsey's) — [FIU Business](https://business.fiu.edu/business-now/2025/fall-2025/retiring-boomer-business-owners-a-challenge-or-an-opportunity.html)
- Boomers are about 40% of small-business owners, and fewer than a third have a succession plan (vendor) — [Teamshares](https://www.teamshares.com/resources/silver-tsunami/). Exit Planning Institute data reportedly show about 50% of retiring owners lack a formal plan (secondary) — [AcquisitionAce statistics](https://www.acquisitionace.io/data/statistics)
- An older SBA estimate: about 10M boomer-owned businesses change hands from 2019 to 2029 **[DATED estimate]** — [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/small-business/boomer-small-business-owners-retiring-golden-opportunity-younger-americans)
- Exit outcomes (via secondary relay of McKinsey; treat with caution): 92% of small businesses end by closure, 5% by completed sale and 3% by transfer — [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/baby-boomers-start-retire-5t-120000171.html)
- Care demand outstrips supply. 77% of HCBS providers turn away referrals and about 700,000 people are on Medicaid home-care waitlists — [TCF](https://tcf.org/content/report/care-matters-a-2026-state-report-card-on-care-affordability-and-access/); [New Republic](https://newrepublic.com/article/207658/cuts-medicaid-home-health-care)
- The skilled-trades labor shortage is described as a "long-term demographic trend," and HVAC prices are not expected to fall — [AC Direct](https://www.acdirect.com/blog/why-hvac-costs-more-than-5-years-ago/)

### Inferences
- The "silver tsunami" is real in owner-age terms, but transferable supply is far smaller than the headline. Only about 1M of 6M are sellable per McKinsey, and closure is the dominant outcome. The buyer opportunity concentrates in businesses with transferable systems, recurring customers and second-layer management.
- Durable demand: aging housing stock and the non-deferrable replacement cycle in HVAC, roofing and plumbing; aging pet owners and humanization of pets in vet care; seniors' care needs in home care and senior living services; dual-income households in childcare, cleaning and lawn care. *Household-formation and dual-income stats were not retrieved; this is inference.*
- Funeral services have a predictable demand tailwind from boomer mortality. Cremation mix shift (lower revenue per call) is a known headwind, but no 2025–26 data were retrieved.

### Gaps
- No Census Annual Business Survey or BLS household and labor-force data were retrieved for owner age, household formation, dual-earner share or childcare demand.
- No NFDA cremation-rate or death-forecast data for 2025–26.
- No primary verification of Exit Planning Institute or Guidant figures.

## Q6. Opportunity-by-opportunity evaluation (10 opportunities) and international differences

### Takeaway
On a risk-adjusted basis for an individual or search-fund buyer in late 2026, the strongest opportunities are recurring-revenue B2B services below PE's radar (commercial cleaning and facilities maintenance, small CPA/bookkeeping firms with an AI-enabled operating model, fire/life-safety and inspection trades) and owner-retiring residential trades in secondary markets. Vet, dental, insurance brokerage, pest and express car wash are better as exits to PE than as entry points. Medicaid-dependent care and childcare carry payer and labor risk.

### Cited Findings (by opportunity; "n/f" = not found in this research pass)

1. **SBA-financed acquisition of a boomer-owned Main Street business (general ETA)**
   - Size/supply: about 6M transitions by 2035, more than 1M sellable — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/institute-for-economic-mobility/our-insights/the-great-ownership-transfer-a-new-era-of-business-stewardship)
   - Pricing: about 2.6–2.7x SDE average and about $350K median price (Q2 2026) — [Sundance FG/BizBuySell](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)
   - Financing: 10% equity, standby seller notes, about 9.5–11.75% rates — see Q2 sources
   - Risks: tighter SBA box, longer close times (+46%) — [Chinook/Pepperdine](https://chinookadvisors.com/news/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-pepperdine-private-capital-markets-report/)

2. **Traditional / self-funded search fund**

**International differences**
- **UK:** for disposals to an Employee Ownership Trust on or after November 26, 2025, only 50% of the gain is CGT-exempt (Finance Act 2026, s.35). Business Asset Disposal Relief is 18% up to the £1M lifetime limit in 2026/27. The effective EOT rate is about 12%, and relief clawback applies if conditions are breached within four years — [legislation.gov.uk Finance Act 2026 s.35](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2026/11/section/35/enacted); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/04/27/employee-ownership-trusts-a-different-business-model); [David Craddock](https://www.davidcraddock.com/employee-ownership-trusts-explained-tax-valuation-and-how-the-new-rules-work/)
- **Europe/international search:** Spain, the UK, Italy, France and Germany have the most European search funds. International launches outnumbered US/Canada launches in 2025, and international returns are 30% IRR / 2.8x — [IESE](https://www.iese.edu/insight/articles/search-funds-record-expansion/)
- **Canada:** Canadian search funds are included in the Stanford US/Canada study. No Canada-specific acquisition-financing data (e.g., BDC, CSBFP) were found (gap).

### Inferences
**Realistic returns (illustrative, researcher's own arithmetic, not sourced).** Assume a $1.5M SBA purchase of a business with $500K SDE at 3.0x, 10% equity ($150K), a 10-year loan at about 10.5% (about $165K/year debt service) and an owner salary replacement of about $120K. That leaves pre-tax free cash flow of about $200K in year 1, a cash-on-cash yield of more than 100% on equity. The equity is highly levered, so a 20–25% EBITDA decline can wipe out debt service coverage. Selling at 4–5x after growing EBITDA to $1M+ is the main source of upside.
