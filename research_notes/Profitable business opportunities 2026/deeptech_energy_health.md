# Profitable Business Opportunities in Deep Tech, Energy/Climate, and Health/Biotech-Adjacent Sectors (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-19. Method: about 20 web searches; the figures come from search-result summaries of the cited pages, and most primary PDFs were not opened in full. Items older than 2025 are flagged **[DATED]**. "Fact" means a reported or measured figure. "Forecast" means a projection. Q3 2026 earnings for GE Vernova, Eaton and Vertiv were **not yet released** at research time (GE Vernova reports Oct 28, 2026), so the latest company data is Q2 2026.

## Q1. Where are the structural supply-demand imbalances in 2026, and what concrete opportunities do they create?

### Takeaway
The tightest and best-documented imbalances are in grid-side electrical equipment and labor. Transformer lead times run 2-4 years, the interconnection queue holds about 2,060 GW and projects take 5+ years to connect, and the US needs about 130k more electricians. All of this sits against data-center electricity demand that the IEA expects to roughly double by 2030. In health, the clearest imbalance is home and community-based care labor (turnover above 80%, demand up 39% by 2037), made worse by Medicaid cuts and immigration enforcement. GLP-1 access widened sharply in 2026 through price cuts and Lilly's oral pill, which creates service-layer demand.

### Cited Findings

**Electricity demand from data centers (macro demand driver)**
- Forecast: the IEA (April 2026 "Key Questions on Energy and AI") Base Case has global data-centre electricity use rising from 485 TWh in 2025 to 945 TWh in 2030, with AI-specific demand tripling. Its 2035 range is 700 TWh (Headwinds) to 1,700 TWh (Lift-Off), with a Base Case of about 1,200 TWh. One outlet reports 950 TWh, a minor discrepancy. Secondary coverage only; the primary IEA PDF was not checked. — [Enlit/IEA coverage](https://www.enlit.world/library/ai-and-data-centre-electricity-use-continue-to-surge-iea-finds); [w.media](https://w.media/data-center-power-demand-will-double-by-2030-iea/)
- Fact: data-centre electricity demand grew 17% in 2025, against 3% growth in global power demand overall (IEA via secondary). — [Enlit/IEA coverage](https://www.enlit.world/library/ai-and-data-centre-electricity-use-continue-to-surge-iea-finds)
- The IEA names shortages of gas turbines, transformers and chips, plus grid-connection delays and permitting, as constraints that are slowing data-centre development. — [power-technology.com](https://www.power-technology.com/newsletters/iea-warns-ai-could-double-data-centre-power-use-by-2030)
- Forecast: BloombergNEF projects US data-center demand rising from about 40 GW to 106 GW by 2035 (via secondary). — [Enki.AI](https://enkiai.com/data-center/data-center-power-crisis-2026-the-grid-bottleneck/)

**Transformer and switchgear shortage**
- Fact (reported): lead times for high-capacity US transformers reach up to 4 years (PwC analysts). Wood Mackenzie data show generator step-up (GSU) transformer demand up 274% from 2019 to 2025. — [pv magazine USA, May 2026](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/)
- Wood Mackenzie's Q2 2025 survey found a 30% supply deficit for power transformers and a 10% deficit for distribution transformers. Prices are up about 80% over five years. — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/)
- Large power transformers take about 110-160 weeks from US and Western OEMs, and Tier-1 backlogs are 48-60 months, against 6-8 months before the shortage. New factory capacity mostly arrives in 2027-28 or later. Grain-oriented electrical steel is a binding input constraint. Vendor blog, so treat as indicative. — [GridReadiness](https://gridreadiness.com/blog/power-transformer-lead-times-ai-data-center-2026); [Build.inc](https://build.inc/insights/data-center-transformer-procurement-2026)
- A July 2026 report says less than half of about 12 GW of US data-center capacity announced for 2026 had broken ground, citing transformer, switchgear and grid-tie unavailability. Developers are buying factory production slots at a premium before they pick sites. Single-firm estimates; treat with caution. — [Data Centre Digest](https://datacentredigest.com/the-transformer-shortage-nobody-saw-coming-how-a-quiet-component-crisis-is-throttling-data-center-power-delivery/); [Build.inc](https://build.inc/insights/data-center-transformer-procurement-2026)

**Interconnection queues**
- Fact: LBNL "Queued Up 2026" (data to end-2025, published June 2026) counts more than 2,060 GW of active generation and storage in queues, down about 10% year over year because withdrawals were high. Gas rose 86% to 253 GW. Solar (773 GW), storage (749 GW) and wind (220 GW) all declined. 549 GW holds a draft or signed interconnection agreement but is not yet operating. Projects built in 2025 took more than 5 years from request to commercial operation. Only 13% of capacity that requested interconnection in 2000-2020 had reached operation by end-2025; about 75% withdrew. Large-load queues (data centers) are tracked separately and **not** included. — [LBNL Queued Up 2026](https://emp.lbl.gov/queued-2026-edition-characteristics-power-plants-seeking-transmission-interconnection-end-2025); [PDF](https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2026-06/queued_up_2026_edition.pdf)
- LBNL says it is too early to judge the effect of FERC Order 2023 reforms. — [LBNL](https://emp.lbl.gov/queues)

Indeed shows year-over-year wage growth of 9.9% for commercial electricians and 8.2% for apprentices. Specialists in medium voltage and liquid cooling reportedly earn $240k-300k. — [MetaIntro](https://www.metaintro.com/blog/ai-boom-data-center-construction-jobs-worker-shortage-2026); [Simpro](https://simprogroup.com/blog/electrical-industry-statistics-2026)

### Inferences — Opportunity profiles (8-12 concrete plays)

Each profile is my synthesis of the cited findings above. **CL** = capital-light service or integration play. **CH** = capital-heavy technology or manufacturing play. Margin comparables are covered in Q4. Where a market size is not cited, it is a gap.

**1. Transformer and switchgear remanufacturing, refurbishment, testing and procurement brokerage (CL to mid)**
- Problem: 2-4 year lead times for new units, a 30% power-transformer deficit and prices up about 80% (cited above).
- Customers: data-center developers, utilities, IPPs and industrial sites.
- Demand evidence: developers are buying production slots at a premium, and less than half of announced 2026 capacity has broken ground.
- Economics: repair, remanufacturing, field testing and condition monitoring sell scarce capacity in months rather than years. Pricing power should hold until new factories arrive in 2027-28+. Section 232 grid-equipment tariffs (15%, rising to 25% from January 2028) favor domestic refurbishment over imports.
- Capital intensity: moderate (shop, test equipment, certified technicians).
- Regulation: utility qualification and UL/IEEE standards.
- Time to revenue: under 12 months.
- Competition: OEM service arms (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy) and regional rebuild shops.
- Key risk: the shortage eases after 2028 as new plants ramp, so this window is time-limited.

**2. Distribution transformer and MV equipment manufacturing (CH)**
- Problem: a 10% distribution-transformer deficit, GOES steel constraints and tariff protection.
- Customers: utilities and data centers.
- Economics: incumbents earn very high margins (Eaton Electrical Americas 27.5% segment margin). Capex is large, and lead times on GOES and other inputs remain constrained.
- Time to revenue: 2-3 years.
- Risks: overbuild in 2028+, GOES supply, and tariff policy changes (the reduced grid-equipment rate ends January 1, 2028).

**3. Data-center and MV electrical contracting, commissioning, and workforce training/staffing (CL)**
- Problem: a 130k electrician gap, electrical work at 45-70% of data-center build cost, and premium wages.
- Customers: hyperscalers, colocation providers and GCs.
- Demand evidence: contractors are turning work away, and Microsoft's president reportedly called the electrician shortage the top constraint.
- Economics: labor-heavy, with margin coming from scarcity pricing. Training academies and certified commissioning have high gross margins.
- Capital intensity: low.
- Regulation: licensing and apprenticeship ratios.
- Time to revenue: immediate.
- Competition: large EPCs (Quanta, MYR Group, Rosendin) and IBEW contractors.
- Risks: wage inflation squeezing fixed-price contracts, and a data-center capex pause.

**4. Liquid-cooling integration, retrofit and O&M (CL to mid)**
- Problem: high-density AI racks need liquid cooling. Specialists in this area command $240-300k pay, which signals scarcity.
- Comparables: Vertiv adjusted operating margin of 22.6% in Q2 2026, full-year guidance of 23.8%, and 2026 sales of $14B (+37%).
- A capital-light angle is third-party CDU and secondary-loop installation, fluid management and maintenance contracts.
- Risks: OEMs bundling service, and standards churn.
- Market size for liquid-cooling services was not found (gap).

**5. Behind-the-meter bridge power and BESS for large loads; storage EPC and O&M (mid to CH)**
- Problem: 5+ year interconnection waits and grid-tie unavailability.
- Customers: data centers and industrials that need to energize before the grid connection arrives.
- Demand evidence: record Q1 and Q2 2026 storage installs, Wood Mackenzie naming co-location with large loads as a key driver, and gas in queues up 86%.
- Policy: storage keeps ITC/PTC eligibility through construction starts in 2033 (see Q2), unlike solar and wind.
- Capital intensity: project-level capital is heavy, but EPC and O&M service is lighter.
- Regulation: FEOC/"prohibited foreign entity" sourcing rules on Chinese cells; Section 232 and other tariffs.
- Time to revenue: 12-24 months.
- Risks: utility-scale MW declined 8% year over year in Q2 2026 as markets saturate; FEOC compliance; battery price swings.

**6. Interconnection, large-load and flexibility services (CL; software plus consulting)**
- Problem: 2,060 GW in queues, 13% historical completion and separate large-load queues.
- Offerings: queue studies, siting and power-availability analytics, and demand-response or flexible-load agreements that let data centers connect faster.
- Customers: developers and data centers.
- Economics: software and consulting margins.
- Capital intensity: low.
- Regulation: FERC Order 2023 and state large-load tariffs.
- Risk: utilities insourcing the work.
- Market size not found (gap).

**7. GLP-1 ecosystem services (CL)**
- Offerings: telehealth prescribing and longitudinal care, adherence, nutrition and muscle-preservation programs, employer and payer GLP-1 benefit management, and Medicare Part D navigation.
- Demand evidence: oral Foundayo launched through telehealth channels at $149-349 self-pay, and Medicare pays $245/month with a $50 copay.
- Economics: the drug margin goes to Lilly and Novo. Service businesses earn on subscriptions or PMPM fees, but falling cash prices compress compounding and cash-pay telehealth markups.
- Regulation: FDA action on compounding, state telehealth rules, and Medicare coverage timing (unsettled).
- Time to revenue: fast.
- Competition: Ro, Hims & Hers, LillyDirect and NovoCare (manufacturer DTC goes around intermediaries).
- Key risk: manufacturer direct-to-consumer channels squeeze intermediaries.

**8. Tech-enabled private-pay and Medicare Advantage home care / aging-in-place (CL)**
- Problem: HCBS demand up 39% by 2037, 80%+ turnover and immigration-driven labor flight.
- Customers: families (private pay), Medicare Advantage plans and long-term care insurers.
- Economics: margin depends on caregiver retention, so scheduling, retention technology and remote monitoring help.
- Regulation: state licensure, Medicaid rate risk (a $911B federal cut; ARPA funds end with 2026).
- Positioning: avoid Medicaid-dependent models and favor private pay or MA.
- Key risks: labor supply and Medicaid rate cuts.

**9. AI-enabled revenue cycle / denials management (CL; software plus services)**
- Problem: denial rates around 10-12% and about $19.7B a year spent on denial work.
- Customers: hospitals and physician groups.
- Economics: contingency or percent-of-collections pricing. Software gross margins are high, and managed services are lower.
- Regulation: HIPAA and payer prior-authorization rules.
- Time to revenue: fast.
- Competition: crowded (Ensemble, R1, Waystar, Epic native tools, many AI startups). Rock Health reports capital concentrating in AI-native leaders (see Q3).
- Risks: payers' own AI escalation, and Epic bundling.

**10. European defense and dual-use component supply, drones/C-UAS and resilience infrastructure (mid to CH)**
- Problem: NATO's 5% of GDP by 2035 target (3.5% core, 1.5% infrastructure, cyber and resilience), at least 20% of budgets on equipment and R&D, and the EU €150bn SAFE loans (described as STEP in one source).
- Funding gap: SMEs lack access to finance compared with the primes (Clifford Chance, August 2026).
- Economics: long procurement cycles but multi-year contracts.
- Regulation: export controls, security clearances, and EU-content rules.
- Time to revenue: 1-3 years.
- Risks: procurement favoring primes, and political reversal.

**11. Robotics/automation integration for reshoring manufacturing (CL integrator vs CH humanoid OEM)**
- Funding: $18.8B in 2026 by mid-year, above all of 2025 ($15B); a later secondary report says $23B+, including $8.6B for humanoids.
- Positioning: capital-heavy humanoid OEMs are crowded and richly valued. A capital-light system integrator or robots-as-a-service model serving reshored plants and labor-short warehouses avoids hardware capex.
- Tariffs: 50% on steel, aluminum and copper, 25% on derivatives, and a 15% temporary rate on grid equipment raise imported-input costs. That favors domestic automation, but it also raises the cost of building plants.
- Risks: integrator margins are thin, and reshoring capex may lag rhetoric.
- Manufacturing construction spend data was not found (gap).

**12. PFAS water treatment and testing (mid)**
- Opportunity: the PFOA/PFOS 4 ppt limits remain, so 6-10% of about 66,000 systems still need action. The deadline is now 2031, and scope is narrower.
- Offerings: testing labs, treatment-system design, media/GAC/IX replacement and O&M.
- Risks: slower demand ramp, litigation outcomes and federal rollback.
- Market size not found (gap).

**13. Ag-tech (contrarian, funding-drought entry)**
- See Q3. Capital is scarce, so valuations are cheaper.
- Upstream farm and biology raised about $9B (+7%) in 2025.
- Weak evidence; a speculative entry rather than a priority.

### Gaps
- No primary-source market sizes for transformer refurbishment, liquid-cooling services, interconnection consulting, PFAS treatment spending, or home-care tech.
- Diagnostics: I found no 2026-specific data on demand, funding or reimbursement (for example, LDT rule status or PAMA cuts). Not researched in depth.
- Europe-specific grid constraints (EU grid connection queues, ENTSO-E, EU Grid Action Plan) were not researched. The evidence here is US-heavy.
- Nursing (RN) shortage numbers for 2026 were not found.
- Whether Medicare GLP-1 obesity coverage actually began in mid-2026 is unconfirmed.

## Q2. How did 2025-2026 policy changes (OBBBA/IRA credit changes, tariffs, EU industrial policy, NATO defense spending) shift profitability?

### Takeaway
Policy changes in 2025-26 cut long-run subsidies for wind and solar, left storage (and nuclear, geothermal and hydro) largely intact, protected domestic metals and grid equipment through tariffs, cut Medicaid (a headwind for home care), lowered GLP-1 prices while widening access, and set a NATO 5% target that is driving European defense budgets. Profit is shifting toward storage, grid equipment, firm power, defense supply and cash-pay or MA health services, and away from wind and solar developers and Medicaid-dependent care.

### Cited Findings
- **OBBBA (H.R. 1, signed July 4, 2025), wind and solar:** projects must begin construction by July 4, 2026, or be placed in service by December 31, 2027, to claim 45Y/48E. — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/one-big-beautiful-bill-new-law-disrupts-clean-energy-investment); [Kirkland](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/08/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-brings-big-changes-to-green-energy-tax-credits); [Beveridge & Diamond](https://www.bdlaw.com/publications/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-is-very-ugly-for-wind-and-solar/)
- **Storage, nuclear, geothermal, hydro:** credits stay in place. Storage receives the full credit for construction starts through 2033, 75% in 2034 and 50% in 2035. One source wrongly says storage is cut off after 2025; this conflicts with the others and is likely an error. — [Pierce Atwood](https://www.pierceatwood.com/alerts/congress-phases-out-energy-tax-credits); [CENet Capital](https://www.cenetcapital.com/blog/obbba-clean-energy-tax-credits); [FTI Consulting](https://www.fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/h-r-1-energy-transition)
- **FEOC:** 45Y/48E credits are restricted for projects that receive "material assistance" from specified foreign entities after December 31, 2025. — [Williams Mullen](https://www.williamsmullen.com/insights/news/legal-news/one-big-beautiful-bill-amends-renewable-energy-tax-credits-summary-key); [Barclay Damon](https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/what-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-means-for-clean-energy-tax-credits)
- **Effect in the queue:** solar, storage and wind volumes fell and gas rose 86% in the LBNL end-2025 data. — [LBNL](https://emp.lbl.gov/queued-2026-edition-characteristics-power-plants-seeking-transmission-interconnection-end-2025)
- **Tariffs:** the April 2, 2026 Section 232 proclamation (effective April 6) applies tariffs to full product value: 50% for goods made almost entirely of steel, aluminum or copper and 25% for derivatives. Electrical grid equipment and some industrial equipment get a temporary 15% rate (10% if made with US metals), which reverts to 25% on January 1, 2028. A June 1, 2026 proclamation sets a 50% tariff on copper articles and 10% where 95% or more US-smelted copper is used. Conflict: a tax-forum reply says Section 232 likely does not cover assembled transformers; verify the HTS annexes. — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/united-states-modifies-steel-aluminum-and-copper-section-232-tariffs); [Shapiro](https://www.shapiro.com/alerts/new-regulations-affecting-section-232-take-effect-on-april-6/); [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/index.php/forum/issue?id=120903)
- **NATO/Europe:** in June 2025, allies committed to 5% of GDP by 2035 (3.5% core, 1.5% infrastructure and resilience), with at least 20% on equipment and R&D and audits in 2029. Rutte says Europe and Canada reached 4% in 2026, counting defense-related spending; this is not comparable to the 3.5% core figure. The EU has a €150bn defense instrument. — [WisdomTree](https://www.wisdomtree.com/at/insights/blog/the-key-takeaways-from-the-nato-summit); [LIGA](https://news.liga.net/en/amp/politics/news/europe-and-canada-have-reached-4-of-gdp-in-defense-spending-on-their-way-to-5-rutte); [Clifford Chance, Aug 2026](https://www.cliffordchance.com/briefings/2026/08/financing-european-defence--how-can-europe-address-the-fiscal-an.html)
- **Medicaid:** the cut is $911B over 10 years, home care is an optional benefit, and ARPA funds expire at end-2026. — [KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicaid/issue-brief/payment-rates-for-medicaid-home-care-ahead-of-the-2025-reconciliation-law/)
- **GLP-1 pricing deals and TrumpRx:** see Q1. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/06/trump-eli-lilly-novo-nordisk-deal-obesity-drug-prices.html)
- **PFAS:** compliance moves to 2031 and four PFAS lose their limits. — [White & Case](https://whitecase.com/insight-alert/epa-partially-rolls-back-pfas-drinking-water-rule)

### Inferences
- Utility-scale solar and wind developers face a cliff at the end-2027 placed-in-service deadline. That likely pulls installation work into 2026-27 and leaves a demand gap afterward. Installers should pivot toward storage, firm power and grid services.
- Storage is the clear policy winner in clean energy. FEOC rules raise the value of non-Chinese supply chains and compliance services, which is a capital-light compliance and traceability opportunity.
- The reduced grid-equipment tariff and its 2028 snapback push utilities and developers to lock in orders now. Domestic transformer, switchgear and refurbishment players gain pricing power.
- In home care, profit shifts toward private-pay and Medicare Advantage-funded models. Medicaid-reliant agencies face margin compression.
- In GLP-1, lower prices expand volume, but intermediaries lose markup. Value moves to care management and outcomes services.
- EU industrial policy (Clean Industrial Deal, Net-Zero Industry Act, AI gigafactories) was not researched (gap).

### Gaps
- Whether the 25D residential solar credit ended at end-2025 under OBBBA was not verified directly in this research. Several law-firm summaries in the results imply it, but the claim is not cited here.
- Treasury/IRS "beginning of construction" guidance (2025-26) and how many projects met the July 4, 2026 deadline: no data found.
- EU Clean Industrial Deal, CBAM implementation and EU grid package effects were not researched.
- The 2026 PFAS final rule and litigation status are unconfirmed.

## Q3. Which segments are attracting capital, and which are in a funding drought (cheap entry points)?

### Takeaway
Capital is flooding into AI-adjacent physical infrastructure (data-center power and cooling), robotics/embodied AI (a record $18.8-23B+ in 2026) and AI-native digital health, and it is concentrating in fewer, larger rounds. Deal counts are falling in climate tech (record-low count in H1 2026) and ag-tech (lowest US H1 pace in the PitchBook series, per a secondary source). European defense SMEs report a financing gap despite surging budgets. These are the likeliest sources of cheaper entry valuations.
