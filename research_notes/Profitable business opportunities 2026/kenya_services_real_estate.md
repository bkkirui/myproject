# Kenya: Profitable Services, Real Estate, Healthcare, Education, Energy, Consumer and Financial-Services Business Opportunities (as of October 2026)

Research date: 19 October 2026. Method: about 25 web searches/fetches. Note: direct fetches of cytonnreport.com and content.knightfrank.com failed (DNS errors), so Cytonn and Knight Frank figures below come from search-result extracts of those reports and from secondary press coverage. Where a figure has only a secondary or vendor source, this is flagged. Nothing older than 2025 is used as a current figure without a "DATED" flag.

## Q1. Macro and financing backdrop that affects all opportunities

### Takeaway
Credit is getting cheaper but is still expensive. The Central Bank Rate (CBR) has been held at 8.75% since February 2026, after 425 bps of cuts, while average bank lending rates were still about 14.7%. Private-sector credit growth is weak, which favours equity-funded or DFI-funded entrants and makes acquisition finance costly.

### Cited Findings
- On 10 Feb 2026 the MPC cut the CBR by 25 bps to 8.75% from 9.00% — [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/02/10/kenyas-central-bank-cuts-benchmark-rate-to-8-75/)
- 11 Aug 2026: the CBR was held at 8.75%, the third consecutive hold — [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/08/11/cbk-holds-central-bank-rate-8-75-percent-august-2026/)
- The June 2026 hold ended a cutting cycle of 10 consecutive reductions totalling 425 bps since August 2024 — [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/06/10/kenya-cbr-mpc-rate-hold-june-2026/); [Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/analyst-desk/kenya-markets/cbk-mpc-june-hold)
- Inflation rose to 6.7% in May 2026 from 5.6% in April, driven by fuel and energy costs. This is inside the 2.5–7.5% target band — [Khusoko/Serrari, same as above](https://serrarigroup.com/analyst-desk/kenya-markets/cbk-mpc-june-hold)
- Average commercial-bank lending rates fell to 14.7% in March 2026 from 17.2% in November 2024. KBA says high non-performing loans (NPLs) slow how fast cheaper rates reach borrowers — [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/04/09/kenyas-mpc-holds-the-central-bank-rate-at-8-75-percent/)
- NCBA projects private-sector credit growth of about 7% in 2026, against the MPC target of 10.6% — [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/04/09/kenyas-mpc-holds-the-central-bank-rate-at-8-75-percent/)
- Sources conflict on the CBK's 2026 GDP forecast: one says it was cut to 5.3% from 5.5%, another to 4.9% from 5.3% — [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/cbk-maintains-8-75pc-rate-cuts-2026-growth-forecast/)

### Inferences
- With bank debt at about 14–15% nominal and inflation at about 6–7%, the real cost of debt is about 8%. A leveraged acquisition or development needs unlevered returns well above 15% in KES to make sense. That pushes investors toward high-margin, cash-generative services or toward USD/DFI-funded structures.
- Money-market funds (MMFs) yield well above inflation (see Q8), so they set the opportunity-cost hurdle for any illiquid investment.

### Gaps
- I found no lending-rate figure after March 2026, and no full August 2026 MPC statement.

## Q2. Real estate: yields, vacancies, price trends and specific opportunities

### Takeaway
Retail (about 8.8% yield) and industrial/logistics (about 9.5% yield, with demand roughly 3x supply for Grade A) give the best income returns. Residential yields are modest (about 5.9–7.8% on average, 9–10% in Westlands and Kilimani). Satellite-town land appreciation has slowed sharply: half of HassConsult's satellite towns showed falling prices in Q2 2026. Student housing (Acorn) is the proven institutional REIT play. The Affordable Housing Programme (AHP) is legally secure after the Court of Appeal ruling of September 2026, but how funds are used and how offtake is financed are still unclear.

### Cited Findings
**Residential**
- Nairobi Metropolitan Area (NMA) average residential rental yield: 5.9% in Q1 2026, up from 5.5% in Q1 2025. Westlands averaged 9.8% and Kilimani 9.3% — [Cytonn Q1 2026 Markets Review](https://cytonnreport.com/research/cytonn-q12026-markets-2)
- Cytonn's 2026 Outlook cites Westlands at 9.3%, Gigiri 8.5% and Kilimani 8.2% against a "market average of 7.8%". This conflicts with the 5.9% average above, probably because the segment or period differs — [Cytonn 2026 Markets Outlook](https://cytonnreport.com/research/cytonn-2026-markets)
- A secondary source citing Cytonn H1 2026 puts NMA apartment yields at about 6.1% and detached homes at 4.9%. This is unverified — [Afriqahome](https://www.afriqahome.com/guides/kenya-real-estate-2026)

**Retail (mall oversupply)**
- NMA retail rental yield was 8.8% in FY2025, up from 8.4% in FY2024. Occupancy rose 3.3 points to 85.5%. Cytonn flags oversupply of about 3.6 mn sq ft of retail space in Nairobi — [Cytonn 2026 Markets Outlook](https://cytonnreport.com/research/cytonn-2026-markets)

**Industrial / warehousing / logistics parks**
- Knight Frank says demand for Grade A logistics space in Nairobi Metro exceeds supply by about 3:1 — [Knight Frank via Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/knight-frank-says-nairobi-lacks-adequate-supply-of-quality-logistics-space)
- Average industrial yield in Kenya is about 9.5% (H1 2026 dashboard). Uganda is about 13%, Zambia 12.5% and Tanzania/Egypt 10% — [Knight Frank via Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/africa-industrial-property-knight-frank-report); [Business Quest](https://www.businessquest.co.ke/knight-frankindustrial-property-market-offers-double-digit-yields/)
- Prime Nairobi industrial rents conflict between sources: about US$6/sq m/month (Knight Frank dashboard, Sep 2026) versus US$4.2 (Aug 2026 article, which attributes the low figure to the poor quality of existing stock) — [Knight Frank Africa Report 2026/27](https://www.knightfrank.com/content-assets/f9012b7bd3784465a422696389146099/africa-report-2026-27.pdf); [Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/africa-industrial-property-knight-frank-report)
- Supply pipeline (H1 2026): Africa Logistics Properties (ALP) completed the 129,000 sq ft Kivu Warehouses (phase 3 of ALP West, Tilisi). The Link at Tatu City announced its 107,500 sq ft phase 2 — [Knight Frank Kenya Market Update H1 2026](https://content.knightfrank.com/research/3135/documents/en/the-kenya-market-update-h1-2026-13009.pdf)

**Serviced apartments**

- 2024: average yield 7.3% (up from 6.8% in 2023), occupancy 72.2% — [Cytonn](https://cytonn.com/topicals/nairobi-metropolitan-area-33)
- DATED (2023 report): best nodes were Westlands at 10.2%, Limuru Road 8.2% and Kilimani 7.7% — [Cytonn](https://cytonn.com/media/article/westlands-limuru-road)
- Unverified secondary figure: 74.7% occupancy in 2025 — [Afriqahome](https://www.afriqahome.com/guides/rental-yield-nairobi-neighborhood-2026)

**Land banking in satellite towns (HassConsult Q2 2026)**
- Satellite-town land values rose about 1.4% quarter on quarter in Q2 2026, up from 0.5% in Q1. Ruiru gained 4.1% to KSh 42.2 mn/acre, Thika 3.8% to KSh 32.4 mn/acre and Ruaka 2.8% to KSh 115.7 mn/acre — [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/real-estate/hassconsult-index-ruiru-thika-and-ruaka-lead-fastest-growing-land-markets/); [Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/nairobi-land-hass-consult-index-q226)

- Annual (y/y) satellite-town figures conflict across secondary sources. Ruiru is given as +2.8%, +10.6% and +13.1% — [Afriqahome](https://www.afriqahome.com/guides/satellite-towns-roi-comparison-kenya-2026)
- Kenyan Wall Street's headline: "Nairobi Satellite Land Beats Stocks, Bonds in Returns" (Q2 2026) — [Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/nairobi-land-hass-consult-index-q226)

**REITs (Development and Investment REITs, and student housing)**
- Listed or quoted REITs include Acorn D-REIT, Acorn I-REIT, ILAM Fahari I-REIT and LapTrust Imara I-REIT. One market report says the Kenyan REIT market is nearing Sh25 bn, with new listings and USD-denominated instruments — [Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/kenyas-reit-market-nears-sh25-billion-as-new-listings-and-dollar-denominated-instruments-reshape-the-asset-class/)
- Acorn's student-housing REITs posted a Ksh 1.52 bn profit, with assets growing to Ksh 29.3 bn — [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/acorn-student-housing-reits-post-ksh152b-profit-as-assets-grow-to-ksh293b-n379975)
- Acorn I-REIT H1 2026 net profit was Sh285.2 mn. Total assets rose 21.7% after it acquired Qwetu Karen and Qejani Karen — [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/acorn-student-housing-i-reit-net-profit-rises-to-sh285-2mn/)
- Acorn D-REIT HY2026 profit fell to Kshs 29 mn from Kshs 205.0 mn in HY2025, even though rental income rose (Cytonn note; the picture differs from other coverage) — [Cytonn Acorn HY2026 note](https://cytonn.com/uploads/downloads/acorn-holdings-earnings-note-hy-2026-vf.pdf)
- Acorn's two REITs have about 20,000 student beds. Acorn got CMA approval in January 2026 for a Sh2.2 bn Build-to-Rent D-REIT — [Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/acorns-student-housing-reits-draw-renewed-investor-interest-as-i-reit-closes-at-ksh-23-24-amid-kenyas-expanding-property-fund-market/)

**Affordable Housing Programme and housing levy**
- On 25 Sep 2026 the Court of Appeal dismissed the appeals against the Affordable Housing Act, so the 1.5% levy stays in place — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.co.ke/story/affordable-housing-levy-survives-court-challenge-inside-kenyas-15percent-housing-contribution-2026092518250405583)
- The levy raised KSh 88.7 bn in its first year. Only 52.8% went to affordable-housing projects, according to the Controller of Budget, as reported by Cytonn — [Cytonn](https://www.cytonn.com/topicals/a-review-of)
- Collection figures conflict across secondary sources: about KSh 63 bn a year, up to KSh 97 bn projected for the current fiscal year, and "over KSh 170 bn" cumulative — [Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/kenya-housing-levy-faces-new-parliamentary-oversight-push/); [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/06/05/affordable-housing-levy-debt-financing/)
- KRA is reportedly targeting employers over more than Sh100 bn in unremitted levy deductions — [Mjengo Hub](https://mjengohub.co.ke/articles/news/kra-to-crack-down-on-employers-over-sh100bn-housing-levy-default)
- The Affordable Housing Fund is financed by levy, voluntary savers' deposits, appropriations and offtake financing from banks and SACCOs. The Board can contract private firms to build — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=5cc92baa-b7ff-45cb-ba11-d49a4b5e35ec)
- Advocacy-style source: the first batch of developers is projected to receive KES 85.6 bn from projects worth KES 285.5 bn, and it alleges the levy was securitised with TDB and Afreximbank. Treat as opinion — [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/06/05/affordable-housing-levy-debt-financing/)
- SACCOs lent Ksh 157.2 bn to land and housing in 2025, their largest lending category. That makes them a natural offtake or mortgage channel — [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/sacco-lending-nears-ksh1-trillion-as-property-sector-attracts-biggest-share/)

### Inferences
- **Best risk-adjusted real-estate plays are Grade A warehousing/logistics and purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA).** Logistics has structural undersupply (3:1), about 9.5% yields, USD-linked rents for quality stock and institutional exit routes (ALP is DFI-backed; there is a USD security on the NSE). PBSA has a proven listed exit (Acorn I-REIT buys stabilised assets from the D-REIT), which is a clear "develop-stabilise-sell into REIT" model.
- Retail malls: yields look fine at 8.8%, but the 3.6 mn sq ft overhang means new mall development is high-risk. Buying distressed malls below replacement cost could work for a contrarian investor.
- Land banking: Q2 2026 data (half of the towns falling, the rest up in low single digits) suggests the easy appreciation era is over except in infrastructure-led nodes (Ruiru, Thika, Ruaka). Carrying cost (no yield) plus a 14%+ cost of debt makes broad land banking unattractive. I found no 2026 HassConsult data for Konza, Machakos or Kajiado specifically.
- AHP: contractor/PPP margins depend on payment from the Affordable Housing Fund. The 52.8% utilisation and the levy-default issues point to counterparty and payment-delay risk similar to SHA's.

### Gaps
- I found no 2026 data on office yields or occupancy, Tatu City/Konza land prices, Grade A warehouse occupancy, student-housing yield percentages, or AHP unit offtake/sales data and PPP contract terms.
- The Knight Frank Kenya H1 2026 PDF could not be fetched (DNS error). Prime residential rents and price data from it are missing.

## Q3. Healthcare: SHA/SHIF transition, private hospitals, diagnostics, specialised care, pharmacy, medical tourism

### Takeaway
Demand is strong and specialist capacity (oncology, renal, cardiac) is attracting large foreign entrants (Apollo, Alameda, HCG). The main risk is the payer: SHA owes private facilities a reported Sh76 bn and county facilities Sh26.87 bn. Cash-pay and private-insurance-focused specialist centres, plus diagnostics, look safer than SHA-dependent general hospitals. Medical insurance premiums grew 22.3% in 2025.

### Cited Findings
- The Controller of Budget says county hospitals' unpaid SHA claims reached about Sh26.87 bn by 31 Mar 2026, up from Sh8.29 bn at end-December 2025 — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/health/373606/county-hospitals-under-strain-as-sha-debt-rises-to-sh2687-billion-cob-nyakango)
- RUPHA says more than 700 private and faith-based facilities are at risk of closure and are owed over Sh76 bn by SHA, on top of legacy NHIF arrears. Some have gone cash-only for SHIF patients — [Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/health/private-hospitals-face-closure-as-sha-owes-sh76b-in-unpaid-claims-5163100)
- RUPHA suspended SHA credit services in September 2025 — [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2025/09/22/rupha-halts-sha-in-private-hospitals/)
- SHA began an Sh11.1 bn disbursement for approved claims on 13 Mar 2026 — [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/realtime/2026-03-14-sha-starts-sh111bn-payout-to-health-providers)
- Only about 3.3 mn of about 19 mn enrolled were actively contributing (structural cash gap) — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/private-hosp-get-over-half-of-sh4-6bn-shif-payment-in-shift-4980262)
- The Auditor General flagged Sh889.6 mn owed by SHA to Moi Teaching and Referral Hospital — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/health/349309/sha-payment-delays-leave-moi-teaching-and-referral-hospital-grappling-with-sh8896-million-pending-bills)
- Alameda (Egypt) opened a Nairobi outpatient clinic (oncology, renal, cardiac, spine) and plans a hospital costing about Sh9.75 bn ($75 mn) within about two years — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/egyptian-firm-alameda-enters-kenya-in-expansion-drive-5414106)
- RVL Healthcare is building a Sh601.2 mn specialist cancer hospital in Nyari, Nairobi (radiotherapy, BMT, nuclear medicine) — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/healthcare-firm-lines-up-specialist-cancer-centre-in-nairobi-5491542)
- Apollo and Balmer Healthcare plan a Sh26 bn ($200 mn) specialist hospital in Eldoret with 20+ specialities — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/kenya-races-to-become-a-specialist-healthcare-hub-5501288)
- HCG is raising its stake in Cancer Care Kenya to over 90% with about KSh 100 mn extra investment. Cancer Care Kenya revenue is nearing KSh 1 bn — [Dawan Africa](https://www.dawan.africa/news/cancer-care-kenya-revenue-nears-ksh1-billion-as-demand-for-oncology-services-grows)
- Equity Bank has lent about Sh33 bn to healthcare over five years, including Sh11.5 bn for medical equipment. One batch included 30 dialysis units, 2 MRIs, 2 linear accelerators and a genetic sequencer — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/hospital-equipment-upgrades-open-business-for-banks-5564762)
- Public competition: the 2026/27 budget allocates Sh45.3 bn to referral hospitals for specialised care. A KSh 29 bn plan will build 13 new 300-bed Level 5 hospitals with imaging and dialysis — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/health/366498/treasury-allocates-sh3-billion-to-expand-cancer-treatment-and-emergency-care-services); [Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/kenya-sh29bn-plan-13-level-5-hospitals-2026/)
- Pharmacy: Goodlife Pharmacy was acquired by CFAO Healthcare (31 Jul 2025, per Tracxn). Earlier deals: Haltons by mPharma (2019, DATED) and Diani Beach Hospital by Ascent Rift Valley Fund (2022, DATED) — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/explore/healthcare-startups-in-kenya/__aDCgDppwKsYqLbq490wJXzJ8-F-UjQe8cieNh_pumRY)
- Equity Afya is opening franchise-owned community pharmacies and targets 1,000 outlets across East and Central Africa — [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/equity-afya-opens-new-pharmacy-in-nairobi-cbd-as-it-targets-1000-outlets/)
- Medical insurance premiums rose 22.3% to Sh93.20 bn in 2025, the largest general-insurance class — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/business/396230/kenyas-insurance-penetration-hits-decade-high-at-263-per-cent-premiums-rise-to-sh46658-billion)
- An Oxfam-linked report scrutinises DFI-financed private-equity hospitals in Kenya, including TPG's Evercare (reputational and regulatory risk) — [PESP](https://pestakeholder.org/news/private-equity-is-buying-up-hospitals-in-low-and-middle-income-countries-with-the-help-of-development-finance-institutions/)

### Inferences
- **Opportunity: specialist outpatient/day-case centres (dialysis, oncology infusion, imaging) in secondary cities.** Revenue should come mainly from private insurance and cash, with SHA as upside, not base load. Equipment can be bank-financed (Equity's track record). Competition is intensifying in Nairobi oncology (HCG, RVL, Alameda, Aga Khan), so secondary cities (Nakuru, Kisumu, Eldoret, Mombasa, Meru) may be less contested. Apollo in Eldoret is a counterexample.
- **Pharmacy roll-up:** consolidation is under way (CFAO/Goodlife, Equity Afya franchising). An investor could roll up independent pharmacies, though competition for sites and staff is rising.
- **SHA-heavy general hospitals are the riskiest:** a receivables build-up of Sh8 bn to Sh27 bn in one quarter (county facilities alone) shows severe working-capital risk.
- **Medical tourism:** I found no hard inbound data. The specialist-hub push (Apollo, Alameda) suggests capacity is being built to capture regional demand.

### Gaps
- I found no hospital unit economics (revenue per bed, EBITDA margins), dialysis session tariffs, SHA reimbursement rates, or pharmacy margins.
- I found no medical-tourism arrival or revenue statistics.
- There were no results on Nairobi Hospital, MP Shah, Lancet or Pathcare.

## Q4. Education: CBC/CBE transition, private schools, TVET, universities, education finance

### Takeaway
The CBE transition created the senior-school (Grade 10) cohort in January 2026, with uniform public boarding fees of about KSh 53,554. That anchors the price below which private schools must justify their premium. TVET is the fastest-growing segment: enrolment has more than doubled since 2022, and the 2026/27 budget gives TVET KSh 58.5 bn with a target of 2 mn learners by 2027. The legal status of the university funding model is unresolved.

### Cited Findings
- From January 2026, public senior-school boarding fees are uniform at Ksh 53,554 a year. The day-school contribution is about Ksh 9,374. Schools are now clustered C1–C4 — [Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/news/education/senior-school-fees-shocker-for-parents--5254604); [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/national/from-free-to-ksh53554-how-much-grade-10s-will-pay-under-c1-c4-senior-school-system/)
- Private senior-secondary fees are about KES 100,000–350,000 a year (secondary guide). Top international schools charge about KSh 2 mn to over KSh 4.5 mn a year (secondary listing, unverified) — [Schools in Kenya](https://schoolsinkenya.co.ke/school-fees-in-kenya-2026-the-complete-guide-for-parents/); [Business.co.ke](https://business.co.ke/most-expensive-private-schools-in-kenya-2026-location-fees-costs/)
- A well-equipped secondary school may cost KES 10–50 mn+ to set up. Private schools are about 15% of enrolment (secondary guide, unverified) — [Huduma Global](https://hudumaglobal.com/blog/set-up-private-school-education-center-kenya-registration-requirements)
- TVET: the 2026/27 budget allocates KSh 58.5 bn. Enrolment was 562,499 in 2022 and 825,484 in 2025 (Budget Statement) — [Education News](https://educationnews.co.ke/?p=517213)
- Enrolment series conflict: 297,000 (2022) to 718,000 (2025) in one official series. The TVET PS cites about 350,000 (2022) to 1,201,320 (May 2026). The target is 2 mn by 2027 — [Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/counties/article/2001556846/tvet-enrolment-surges-as-kenyan-youth-turn-to-technical-skills)
- The HELB (Amendment) Bill 2026 would enable state funding for every student placed in a TVET or university. I found no confirmation it has passed — [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/latest-kenya-times-news/govt-revises-university-fees-formula-for-all-students/)
- The university funding model (VSLF, 2023) was declared unconstitutional by the High Court in December 2024. The Court of Appeal stayed that decision on 26 Mar 2025 (DATED). I found no final 2026 ruling. A 31 Aug 2026 report says the government revised the university fees formula — [Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/110357-court-appeal-clears-way-rutos-university-funding-model); [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/latest-kenya-times-news/govt-revises-university-fees-formula-for-all-students/)
- SACCOs lent Ksh 125.5 bn for education in 2025, which shows strong school-fee credit demand — [The Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/sacco-lending-nears-ksh1-trillion-as-property-sector-attracts-biggest-share/)

### Inferences
- **Private TVET and skills institutes** (accredited by TVETA and CDACC) aligned to the 2 mn target and potential HELB capitation are attractive. They are asset-light compared with K-12 schools and, if HELB funding extends to private TVETs, government-funded. This is subject to confirming that the Bill passes and that private institutions qualify.
- **Private senior schools under CBE:** each school must offer specific pathways (STEM, Social Sciences, Arts and Sports) with labs and facilities, which raises capex. Mid-market private schools at KES 100–350k compete against public boarding at about KES 53.5k. The premium segment (international and IB/IGCSE) is the more defensible niche.
- **School-fee and education-finance lending:** SACCO education lending of Ksh 125.5 bn shows the demand. A specialist school-fee or school-infrastructure lender is a viable non-bank niche.

### Gaps
- I found no data on Nova Pioneer, Crawford, Braeburn or ADvTECH (Makini, Regis Runda) expansions, school EBITDA margins, or occupancy.
- There is no confirmed 2026 university funding-model court outcome, and I found no data on education-finance lenders (for example Elimu-type lenders, or Sasakazi-style lenders).

## Q5. Energy and water: C&I solar, net metering, tariffs, captive power, LPG, water

### Takeaway
LPG is a clear structural-growth market: consumption grew 14.7% to 475,943 t in FY to June 2026 and per-capita use is rising. C&I solar has payback of about 3–6 years against grid tariffs of about KSh 22–25/kWh (vendor figures). Net metering is restrictive, though: a 50% export credit, retroactive "dumping" charges, and size capped at 1 MW and at peak demand. That favours behind-the-meter, self-consumption and storage designs over export-reliant ones.

### Cited Findings
- An EPRA Gazette Notice (18 Sep 2026) defines unauthorised grid injection as "dumping", charged at the base tariff. It codifies a net-metering export credit of 50% of exported kWh, effective retroactively from 1 Jul 2025 — [pv magazine](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/22/kenya-backdates-solar-export-charges-by-over-a-year/); [Pulse](https://www.pulse.co.ke/story/epra-tightens-solar-export-rules-inside-kenyas-net-metering-system-2026092520081554519)
- C&I net-metering systems are capped at 1 MW and at the 12-month maximum demand, under the Net-Metering Regulations 2024 — [Pulse](https://www.pulse.co.ke/story/epra-tightens-solar-export-rules-inside-kenyas-net-metering-system-2026092520081554519)
- Kenya Power is tightening rules on illegal solar connections — [TechTrends KE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/30/illegal-solar-connections-kenya-power/)
- EPRA pass-through costs of Ksh 4.16/kWh apply on top of the base tariff — [Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/127216-epra-adjusts-electricity-charges-ksh416-kwh-pass-through-costs-apply)

### Inferences
- **LPG distribution and piped LPG for estates and institutions** is a high-growth, cash-generative opportunity with government policy support. Piped LPG in new apartment blocks fits with the residential and AHP pipeline. Regional depots and cylinder refilling, plus school/institutional conversion contracts, are concrete entry points.
- **C&I solar-as-a-service (leases or PPAs) for factories, hospitals, malls and warehouses:** the economics depend on self-consumption, not export, because exports earn only 50% credit and are capped. Battery storage improves self-consumption. USD-pricing pressure (the parliamentary 7-cent cap applies to IPPs, not captive) is a watch item.

## Q8. Financial services beyond tech: MMFs and asset management, SACCOs, insurance

### Takeaway
Asset management is the fastest-compounding segment: CIS AUM was KSh 851.7 bn in March 2026, up from KSh 756.3 bn in December 2025 (+12.6% in one quarter), and MMFs are about 59%. SACCOs are large and profitable (KSh 1.21 tn assets, net income +25% in 2025). Insurance premiums grew 16.5% to Sh466.6 bn in 2025, with penetration at a decade high but still only 2.63%.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Asset management or an MMF licence** (CMA fund manager plus CIS approval) is a scalable, low-capex business. AUM growth of about 12% a quarter is partly new-fund reporting, so organic growth is lower. Fee economics (typically about 2% management fee for MMFs, not verified here) on growing AUM give high operating leverage. Distribution (digital, diaspora) is the key competitive factor against Sanlam, CIC, Standard Investment Bank and Etica.
- **Insurance brokerage focused on medical and life:** medical +22% and life now the majority, while penetration is only 2.63%, which leaves room to grow. Brokerage is asset-light and commission-based. A broker carries no underwriting risk, unlike insurers, whose profits fell on investment income.
- **SACCO-linked services** (offtake mortgages, core-banking and IT, credit insurance) rather than owning a SACCO, since SACCOs are member-owned and cannot be acquired by outside investors in the usual way.
