## 1. What is the macro and cross-cutting operating context for real-economy investment in Kenya in late 2026?

### Takeaway
Kenya's macro backdrop is stable but cost-pressured: the shilling is flat near KES 130/USD, policy rate 8.75%, GDP rebounding (5.3% in Q1 2026) after a weak 2025 for agriculture and manufacturing, but 2026 brought a fuel/freight shock (Strait of Hormuz disruption), a maize shortfall and rising inflation (6.8% Sept 2026). Two big trade tailwinds landed in 2026: China zero tariffs on Kenyan farm exports (from 1 May 2026) and an AGOA extension to end-2028.

### Cited Findings
**Growth (FACT)**
- GDP grew 4.6% in 2025 (vs revised 4.7% in 2024); agriculture grew 2.8% (down from 4.3%) due to erratic rainfall; manufacturing grew 2.0% (from 3.0%) as food-related industries contracted — [Capital FM, Apr 2026](https://www.capitalfm.co.ke/business/2026/04/kenya-growth-slows-to-4-6pc-on-weak-agriculture-manufacturing/). KNBS's own overview reportedly gives agriculture at 3.1% — [KNBS 2026 Economic Survey page](https://www.knbs.or.ke/reports/2026-economic-survey/) (conflict: 2.8% vs 3.1%).
- Manufacturing share of GDP slipped to 7.1% in 2025 from 7.3% as operating costs bite — [The Star, May 2026](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/2026-05-01-manufacturing-contribution-to-gdp-slows-to-71-as-operating-costs-bite).
- 2025: horticulture output +13.8% while wheat, tea and sugarcane fell — [Kenyan Wall Street summary of Economic Survey 2026](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/economic-survey-2026-highlights). Mining & quarrying +14.9%, construction +6.8% — [Vellum](https://vellum.co.ke/kenyas-reality-check-key-takeaways-from-the-economic-survey-2026/).

**FX, rates, inflation (FACT)**

**Trade access (FACT unless noted)**

**County cess / multiple taxation (structural)**

Counties continue to legislate new cess (e.g., Mandera Cess Bill, March 2026) — [Mandera Assembly](https://www.manderaassembly.go.ke/storage/bills/March2026/r0FFSqoUnhMi2nQ1e0Vb.pdf).

### Inferences
- Diesel and freight volatility is the single biggest 2026 cost shock for exporters, truckers and processors; businesses with local inputs, short supply chains or captive energy are favoured.
- A stable shilling (~130) reduces FX translation risk relative to 2023, but USD-priced inputs (palm oil, soybean meal, fertiliser, fuel) remain exposed to global shocks.
- The China zero-tariff is a low-base opportunity (USD ~45m combined for tea/coffee/avocado/macadamia in 2025), so upside is in building China-specific channels (frozen avocado, kernels, specialty tea/coffee) rather than an immediate volume windfall.

## 2. Export horticulture and tree crops: market size, trends, unit economics and risks (avocado, macadamia, tea, coffee, flowers, vegetables, berries)

### Takeaway
Horticulture is Kenya's best-performing farm segment (output +13.8% in 2025; exports ~KES 144bn per AFA / ~USD 1.68bn per KNBS). Flowers are the anchor (KES 81.3bn, 2025). Avocado production is at record levels (727k t forecast for 2026) but exports *fell* in 2025 on quality and regulatory tightening; macadamia remains under a raw-nut export ban that favours processors; coffee earned record values in 2025 but prices fell ~28% into April 2026; tea volumes rose but earnings slipped amid a regulatory fight over auction-only sales. French bean/vegetable exports are collapsing on EU pesticide residue (MRL) failures.

### Inferences
- Avocado: the bottleneck is export-grade fruit, compliance and logistics, not orchard area. With farmgate ~USD 0.70/kg vs FOB ~USD 1.30–1.45/kg, the exporter/packhouse margin (before freight, packing, rejects) is meaningful; well-run, compliant packhouses with own orchards or tight outgrower control and China-cleared frozen/oil lines look better positioned than pure traders, who face licence revocation risk.
- Macadamia: while the raw-nut ban holds, cracking/processing capacity captures the spread between in-shell farmgate and kernel FOB. A rough check: at ~25–30% kernel recovery, KES 130–200/kg in-shell implies roughly KES 430–800 (USD 3.3–6.2) of raw material per kg kernel against conventional kernel prices below the organic EUR 23–25/kg quoted — margin depends heavily on recovery rates and grades (calculation is mine; recovery rate is an assumption, not sourced). Policy reversal (ban suspension) is the key risk to a processor's thesis.
- Flowers are the most proven export business (scale, rising volumes, EU anchor) but face freight-cost inflation in 2026; sea-freight via Naivasha ICD reefer could cut costs if it scales.
- French beans are a contrarian/turnaround play: the market is shrinking because of compliance failure, so a fully MRL-compliant, vertically integrated grower-exporter could take share, but the risk of shipment rejection is high.

## 3. Livestock and protein: dairy, feed, poultry, aquaculture, beef/leather and insect protein

### Takeaway
Dairy is the largest formal protein market (formal intake >1bn litres in 2025, +11.5%) dominated by Brookside and New KCC, but 2026 drought and feed costs reversed growth. Animal feed is the system's chokepoint: millers run at 50–60% capacity, soybean meal is largely imported, GMO feed imports remain legally stuck, and a 2026 maize deficit pushed prices up. Aquaculture (Lake Victoria cages) is the fastest-growing protein segment, with Victory Farms as a DFI-backed proof point. Meat exports to the Gulf are growing fast from a low base; leather is hollowed out.

## 8. Development-economics evidence on smallholder productivity and outgrower models

### Takeaway
Rigorous evidence shows bundled input-credit + training models raise smallholder maize yields ~26% and profits ~16% in western Kenya — relevant to designing outgrower supply for any processor — but gains did not persist after farmers left the programme.

### Cited Findings
- One Acre Fund RCT (NBER WP 26054, Deutschmann, Duru, Siegal, Tjernström; working paper): +26% maize production, +16% profits (preferred specification); bundle = group-liability input loans, training, insurance, market facilitation **[DATED 2019 study]** — [NBER](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26054/revisions/w26054.rev0.pdf); [AEA RCT registry](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/6675).
- Teso region analysis: ~34% maize yield increase, ~20% maize+bean profit increase; no persistent difference for former clients — [La Follette School](https://lafollette.wisc.edu/research/the-impact-of-one-acre-funds-small-farm-program/).
- 2009 Chwele RCT: internal M&E results differed substantially from RCT results — [One Acre Fund](https://www.oneacrefund.org/publications/chwele-2009-rct-summary).

### Inferences
- Processors relying on outgrowers (macadamia, avocado, oilseeds, dairy) should budget for continuous input credit and extension — benefits fade when support stops. Independent verification of yield claims matters.

### Gaps
- No J-PAL/IPA evidence specific to contract farming or export-horticulture outgrower schemes in Kenya found in this pass.

### Gaps
- Verified unit economics (capex per tonne, processing margins, payback periods) were largely unavailable from reliable 2025–26 Kenyan sources for every opportunity; this is the most significant gap.
- Key competitors in avocado (Kakuzi etc.), abattoirs, trucking and flowers were not researched in detail.
- Solar assembly, wheat, warehousing rents and Kenya Meat Commission status not covered.
