## Q1. What does Kenya's 2025-2026 startup funding data say: volumes, top deals, debt vs equity, and which sectors produce profitable companies?

### Takeaway
Kenya was Africa's #1 funding market in 2025, at about US$0.93-1.04B depending on the tracker. That figure is misleading as a signal of ecosystem breadth. About 82% went to five asset-heavy energy/PAYGo companies, mostly as debt and securitisations. Kenya then fell to #3 in H1 2026, with US$126M, its weakest half since early 2021. The companies that reach profit are asset-financing businesses with large receivables books (M-KOPA, Sun King, d.light), not marketplaces. Kenya's graveyard of 13 collapsed startups, which together raised about US$718M, is dominated by capital-heavy distribution models.

### Inferences
- "Kenya #1 in 2025" is mostly a debt and securitisation story for asset-backed PAYGo and energy firms. Investors should read Kenya as a market for **asset-backed receivables businesses**, which can access local bank debt via securitisation, and not as a venture-equity hotbed.
- The H1 2026 collapse to US$126M means equity valuations for Kenyan early-stage companies should be cheap relative to 2021-22. That suits a well-capitalised investor-founder who can fund through a drought.
- Profitable Kenyan tech businesses share three traits: (a) receivables that can be securitised, (b) M-Pesa-based daily or weekly collections, and (c) device lock or asset-recovery capability. Unprofitable ones held inventory and ran logistics networks at thin margins (Twiga, Copia, Wasoko, MarketForce).

## Q2. What are the fintech opportunities on M-Pesa and Safaricom rails, and what is the regulatory regime?

### Takeaway
M-Pesa is a KSh 183B-revenue (about US$1.4B) rail moving KSh 41.7T a year. Credit layered on it is growing fastest: Fuliza disbursements grew 49% to KSh 1.47T. The rail itself is Safaricom's to monetise, so the opportunity is in **credit, insurance and SME-finance products built on top of it**. Licensing is now accessible: 281 licensed digital credit providers by September 2026, and the VASP Act plus 2026 regulations. Compliance costs are rising, however, through data-protection fines, KESONIA-based pricing and capital requirements. Bank licences remain scarce, as Moniepoint's 2026 entry via acquisition of Sumac MFB shows.

### Cited Findings
**Safaricom / M-Pesa FY2026 (year to 31 March 2026, released 7 May 2026)**
- M-Pesa revenue was KSh 182.7B (+13.4%), 45.6% of Safaricom Kenya service revenue (44.2% in FY25). Consumer payments were about KSh 74.6B (+18.4%), business payments about KSh 56.7B (+16.5%) and withdrawals flat at about KSh 36.8B — [tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy26-mpesa-results-explained/); [Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/m-pesa-safaricom-revenue/); [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy2026-results-earnings-analysis/)
- Transaction volume was 46.4B (+25.1%) and value KES 41.68T (+8.9%) — [Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy26-results/); [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy2026-results-earnings-analysis/)
- Fuliza disbursements were KSh 1.47T (+49%). Financial-services revenue is reported as KSh 9.8B (+20.6%) or KSh 10.0B (+19.1%), so sources conflict. Fuliza is underwritten by NCBA and KCB — [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy2026-results-earnings-analysis/); [Sharp Daily](https://thesharpdaily.com/safaricom-fy2026-earnings-rise-on-strong-m-pesa-growth/)
- Group net income was about KSh 95.6-100B depending on definition, a record — [Abojani](https://www.abojani.com/safaricom-fy2026-historic-ksh-95-6-billion-profit/); [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy2026-results-earnings-analysis/)

**Digital credit licensing and lending rules**
- CBK licensed DCPs: 195 (Jan 2026), then 252 (July 2026; Citizen Digital says 256), then **281 (30 Sept 2026)** after 29 new approvals. By Aug 2026, licensed DCPs had issued 9,596,509 loans worth Ksh 165.1B — [Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/127462-cbk-approves-29-new-digital-lenders-licensed-providers-hit-281); [allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202601020028.html); [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/cbk-approves-25-more-digital-lenders-n386450); [CBK DCP Directory Apr 2026](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Directory-of-Digital-Credit-Providers-April-2026.pdf)
- Dec 2024 amendments to the CBK Act reportedly reclassified digital lenders as Non-Deposit-Taking Credit Providers, with interest rate caps, stricter data mandates and **KES 100M minimum capital**. ### Inferences
- **Most attractive fintech wedge: SME working-capital and invoice finance tied to M-Pesa till and paybill data plus KRA eTIMS invoices.** Evidence: Fuliza's +49% disbursement growth shows heavy demand for short-tenor credit. DFI and bank first-loss structures (IFC, AfDB) are now available to lever an equity base. The 281-DCP field is crowded in consumer credit but thin in B2B.
- Consumer digital lending is commoditised and reputationally risky (ODPC fines, CBK purges). Avoid it unless the founder already owns a distribution or data edge.
- A bank or MFB licence is now a scarce asset. Acquiring a small MFB, as Moniepoint did with Sumac, is a real entry path for a large-budget investor and enables deposits, which are cheaper funding.
- The VASP regime makes **KES or USD stablecoin rails for B2B cross-border payments** (Kenya-Uganda-Tanzania-DRC trade, China import payments) legally possible from H2 2026. This is first-mover, high-risk territory, and timing depends on actual CBK/CMA licence issuance.
- Remittance growth of +1.9% makes standalone remittances a poor new-entry bet. It works only as an acquisition channel for savings, investment or diaspora-mortgage products.
- Insurtech remains small: 2.6% penetration and US$66M raised over five years. Embedded distribution (Turaco model) is the only proven path. Exits are likely trade sales to insurers or pan-African insurtechs.

### Gaps
- Safaricom's FY26 annual report was not read directly. Figures for M-Pesa 30-day active users, merchant count and the M-Pesa/Fuliza fee structure were not retrieved.
- Kenyan pension or savings fintech data (e.g., Octagon, Ndovu, Hisa/Etica money market funds, CMA collective investment scheme AUM) was not researched.
- The exact current DCP interest-rate cap and NDTCP capital rules were not confirmed from primary text.
- No confirmed list of VASP licence applicants or awardees.
- No unit economics (CAC, loss rates, take rates) for Kenyan SME lenders found in public sources.

## Q3. What are the AI and digital services opportunities: data labelling/BPO, Swahili AI, govtech, cybersecurity?

### Takeaway
Kenya's AI data-services and BPO base is real but fragile. One client exit (Meta leaving Sama) removed 1,108 jobs in April 2026. Labour litigation (a US$1.6B claim) and a draft 2026 fair-pay framework are pushing up compliance costs. Demand for AI data services is growing (a projected 37% CAGR job family), but plain customer-experience BPO faces up to 50% automation risk. Govtech is large: eCitizen reportedly collected KSh 539B in a year with 22,000 services. Access depends on government procurement and is politically contested. Cybersecurity threat volumes are very large (11.1B detected events in FY2025/26), but no reliable market-size figure exists.

### Cited Findings
**AI data labelling / BPO**
- Meta ended Sama's contract after reports that Kenyan workers reviewed intimate Ray-Ban smart-glasses footage. Sama issued redundancy notices to **1,108** Nairobi staff on 16 April 2026, the largest single tech-outsourcing layoff in Kenya — [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/04/16/kenya-meta-facebook-sama-content-moderators/7c5345ee-39b1-11f1-90c4-9772c7fabc03_story.html); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-smart-glasses-sama-kenya-workers); [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2026/04/16/sama-layoffs-meta-contract-termination-kenya/)
- 185 former moderators are suing Sama and Meta for a reported US$1.6B. In 2026, the Kenyan Court of Appeal dismissed Meta's appeal, so Meta can be sued locally. No merits ruling has been found — [Wikipedia: Sama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sama_(company)); [Privacy International](https://privacyinternational.org/examples/5127/kenyan-content-moderators-sue-over-working-conditions)

### Inferences
- **Lesson from Twiga, Wasoko, MarketForce and Copia:** do not own inventory and trucks at thin margin while subsidising growth with equity. Twiga's asset-light pivot came too late. The surviving models monetise **credit and data** on top of others' logistics (Apollo inputs-on-credit, M-KOPA device credit). - **SHA creates two investable gaps.** (1) **Provider receivables / claims finance**: lend against SHA claims, plus software to cut rejections (Sh3.43B rejected in one county cycle). ## Q7. Shortlist: 8-12 concrete opportunities, evaluated (synthesis)

### Takeaway
Ranked on risk-adjusted return for an investor-founder with ample capital and high risk tolerance, the best opportunities are asset-backed or credit models on M-Pesa rails (SME and receivables finance, e-mobility leasing, provider and claims finance). Next come regulated-scarcity plays (buying a licensed MFB, VASP-licensed B2B stablecoin rails, carbon projects that already hold LoAs). Last are services businesses whose demand is clear but whose margins are threatened (higher-skill AI data services, cybersecurity MDR, mid-size data centres). Avoid B2B inventory e-commerce and carbon-dependent clean cooking without LoAs. Most unit economics below are **inferences**, not sourced data. Sourced facts are in Q1-Q6.

### Inferences
Each item has these fields: Problem | Customer | Market size | Demand evidence | Unit economics (inferred) | Capital | Regulation | Competition | Comparables | Exit | Key risks.

1. **SME invoice and working-capital finance on M-Pesa till plus eTIMS data (Kenya, then Uganda and Tanzania)**
   - Problem: MSMEs lack collateral-light working capital, and bank pricing under KESONIA+K excludes them.
   - Customer: merchants, distributors and suppliers to FMCG and large corporates.
   - Market size: no sourced SME credit-gap figure (gap). Proxies are Fuliza at KSh 1.47T a year and DCP loans of KSh 165B.
   - Demand evidence: Mogo, Easetrade, 4G Capital, AfDB/Family Bank facilities in 2026.
   - Unit economics (inferred): 30-90-day tenors, yields of about 2-5% a month gross, losses that must stay under about 5-8%, funded by a 4-6x debt-to-equity bank or DFI facility with first-loss cover.
   - Capital: US$5-15M equity plus US$20-50M debt.
   - Regulation: DCP or NDTCP licence (reported KES 100M capital), ODPC compliance.
   - Competition: Pezesha, 4G Capital, Mogo, Moniepoint (via Sumac), banks (KCB, Equity), Safaricom/Fuliza for business.
   - Comparables: M-KOPA's lending-led profit (success); Lipa Later's collapse (failure).
   - Exit: sale to a bank or pan-African fintech (the Moniepoint pattern).
   - Risks: credit cycle, Safaricom competition, regulatory rate caps.

2. **Acquire and digitise a licensed microfinance bank (deposit-funded digital lender)**
   - Problem: new deposit licences are frozen, so cheap funding is scarce.
   - Customer: SMEs and diaspora savers.
   - Demand evidence: Moniepoint paid for Sumac.
   - Capital: US$10-40M (inferred).
   - Regulation: CBK MFB Act and change-of-control approval.
   - Competition: Moniepoint/Sumac, Faulu, KCB/Equity digital.
   - Exit: trade sale to a regional bank or fintech.
   - Risks: legacy loan book quality, CBK approval timelines.

3. **E-mobility asset finance for under-served segments (matatus, e-cargo/trucks, fleet battery-as-a-service)**
   - Problem: diesel cost, plus operators cannot afford EV capex.
   - Customer: 300+ matatu SACCOs, logistics fleets.
   - Demand evidence: BasiGo's 100+ buses and 15M riders; Spiro's US$265M raised in 2026.
   - Unit economics (inferred): per-km lease priced below diesel per-km cost, with daily M-Pesa collections.
   - Capital: US$20-100M, mostly DFI debt (BII, DFC, Proparco precedent).
   - Regulation: EPRA charging licences, NTSA.
   - Competition: BasiGo, Roam, Spiro, Ampersand, ARC Ride, M-KOPA (EVs).
   - Exit: strategic (OEMs, energy majors), infra funds.
   - Risks: technology and battery residual value, FX on imported vehicles, grid reliability, price wars in 2-wheelers.

4. **SHA provider claims and revenue-cycle finance plus SaaS**
   - Problem: delayed and rejected SHA claims starve clinics of cash.
   - Customer: private and faith-based hospitals, pharmacies, county facilities.
   - Market size: SHA pays about Sh11-13B a month in claims; arrears reportedly about Sh76B (mid-2025).
   - Demand evidence: closures threatened, and the HAKIKA 2026-2029 re-contracting.
   - Unit economics (inferred): factoring fee of 2-4% a month on approved claims plus SaaS fees.
   - Capital: US$5-20M plus receivables facility.
   - Regulation: DCP licence, Health Act, ODPC (health data is sensitive).
   - Competition: banks' hospital lending, Ilara (equipment finance), M-TIBA-type platforms (not researched).
   - Exit: insurers, banks, pan-African healthtech.
   - Risk: sovereign payment risk, which needs a cap on SHA exposure.

5. **Higher-skill AI data services and evaluation (RLHF, domain-expert, Swahili/East African language data) with an ethical-labour premium**
   - Customer: global AI labs and enterprises.
   - Market size: no sourced figure (gap). The AI data-services job family is projected at 37% CAGR.
   - Demand evidence: Chinese and Western firms hiring Kenyans; Sama still labels for Meta.
   - Unit economics (inferred): bill at US$8-25/hr, pay US$3-6/hr plus mental-health and compliance costs.
   - Capital: US$1-5M.
   - Regulation: draft 2026 AI worker standards, labour law, ODPC.
   - Competition: Sama, CloudFactory, Scale/Remotasks-type platforms, Indian and Philippine BPOs.
   - Comparables: Sama's 1,108 layoffs (single-client risk).
   - Exit: acquisition by global data-services or BPO firms.
   - Risks: client concentration, litigation (Meta/Sama precedent of local jurisdiction), automation.

6. **Swahili voice-AI agents for financial services, SACCOs and health (applications, not foundation models)**
   - Demand evidence: Intella entering Kenya; AethexAI's US$3M pre-seed; UlizaLlama use case.
   - Capital: US$1-5M.
   - Competition: Intella, Lelapa AI, global LLMs adding Swahili.
   - Risks: foundation-model commoditisation, the "proof gap" on local models.
   - Exit: acquisition by telcos (Safaricom) or regional fintechs.

7. **Managed cybersecurity (SOC/MDR) and fraud analytics for banks, SACCOs, DCPs and hospitals**
   - Demand evidence: 11.12B threat events in FY25/26, healthcare and ISP targeting, INTERPOL mobile-money fraud warning, ODPC enforcement.
   - Market size: gap.
   - Capital: US$2-10M.
   - Competition: Serianu (not researched), Safaricom Business, global MSSPs.
   - Exit: global MSSPs or telcos.

8. **Mid-size colocation, edge, GPU-ready data centre with captive renewable PPA under open-access rules**
   - Market size: about 20 MW installed; 90-200 MW pipeline projections.
   - Demand evidence: Oracle region, Digital Realty NBO2, Airtel build, RMB financing for iXAfrica.
   - Capital: US$50-200M.
   - Regulation: EPRA open access (May 2026), Data Protection Act data-localisation pressures.
   - Competition: iXAfrica, ADC/Cassava, Digital Realty, Airtel, Safaricom.
   - Comparable failure or warning: Microsoft-G42 stall.
   - Exit: global DC platforms or infra funds.
   - Risks: power, overbuild ("is capacity outpacing demand?"), FX.

9. **VASP-licensed B2B cross-border stablecoin and FX settlement (Kenya-East Africa-China/UAE trade)**
   - Problem: importers face USD scarcity and slow correspondent banking.
   - Regulation: VASP Act 2025 plus July 2026 regulations; licensing not yet confirmed operating.
   - Capital: US$3-15M plus licence capital.
   - Competition: Yellow Card, Onafriq, banks (not researched in depth).
   - Risks: licensing delay, AML enforcement, CBK FX policy.
   - Exit: global crypto or payments firms.

10. **Authorised carbon-project developer and aggregator (cookstoves, e-mobility, agroforestry) holding LoAs under the 2026 rule book**
    - Scarcity value: 1.67 Mt a year export cap.
    - Comparables: BURN (four authorisations, CORSIA credits) as success; KOKO collapse as failure.
    - Economics: 25% state share plus US$4/credit levy plus 25% community share (reported) compress margins.
    - Risks: sovereign and policy risk (highest of any item).
    - Exit: sale to carbon majors or integration with clean-energy operators.

11. **C&I solar plus storage developer (PPA or lease) for factories, hotels, data centres**
    - Driver: commercial tariffs of about KSh 22-25/kWh all-in (unofficial), open access.
    - Capital: project finance, US$10-100M.
    - Competition: CrossBoundary, Sun King/PowerGen (C&I), many local EPCs (not researched).
    - Risks: unclear net-metering credit, tariff freeze reducing savings, FX.

12. **Distressed or roll-up acquisitions of failed-startup assets (e.g., Twiga's FMCG distributors and logistics, Copia assets)**
    - Thesis: buy customer networks at a discount and run them asset-light with credit layered on.
    - Risk: the same structural margin problems.
    - Evidence: Twiga in administration since Aug 2026, with an administrator appointed.

**Avoid, or only as a contrarian bet:** B2B inventory e-commerce (Twiga, Wasoko, MarketForce, Copia), consumer payday digital lending (crowded at 281 DCPs, ODPC risk), standalone remittances (+1.9% growth), greenfield hyperscale AI DCs (power guarantee problem), and carbon-only revenue models without LoAs.
