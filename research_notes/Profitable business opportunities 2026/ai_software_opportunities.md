## Which concrete opportunities (8-12) are worth starting or investing in, and how do they score?

### Takeaway
The best risk-adjusted openings in late 2026 are narrow, workflow-deep vertical AI in regulated, back-office-heavy niches, plus measurement and forecasting products that suit a quantitative founder. The evidence for vertical AI in general is strong. The evidence for these particular niches is weaker. Healthcare revenue-cycle and prior-authorization work, accounting-firm automation, specialty insurance operations, and preconstruction for specialty trades have demand that funding shows, but the scaled leaders are moving fast. Horizontal coding tools, copilots, and generic legal AI are already crowded by multi-billion-dollar leaders and the frontier labs.

## Which AI application categories show real revenue traction versus hype, and which verticals remain underserved?

### Takeaway
Real traction is concentrated in coding, copilots, and a handful of vertical leaders: legal, healthcare documentation and revenue-cycle work, and accounting. Each has $100M+ ARR players or 2–3x year-over-year growth. Consumer AI apps monetize well but retain poorly. In my sources, insurance operations, construction trades, government, and logistics have many seed and Series A rounds but no disclosed scaled ARR. That pattern suggests these verticals are still underserved.

### Cited Findings
- Enterprise generative AI spend was about $37B in 2025, up from $11.5B in 2024. Applications took about $19B of it:

  - Departmental: $7.3B, of which coding was $4.2B
  - Vertical: $3.5B
  — [No Jitter](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/menlo-ventures-estimates-19-billion-in-gen-ai-spend-during-2025); [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/enterprises-increase-ai-spending-to-37bn-6d854973)
- Menlo counts at least 10 AI products above $1B ARR and about 50 above $100M ARR — [No Jitter](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/menlo-ventures-estimates-19-billion-in-gen-ai-spend-during-2025)

### Inferences
- Vertical AI was only about $3.5B of 2025 spend, against about $19B for applications overall. Penetration of industry workflows is still low even though headline valuations are high.
- For a new entrant, the gap lies in sub-verticals beneath the leaders: particular specialties, lines of business, or trades.
- Consumer AI's retention gap makes consumer plays risky for a capital-efficient founder. B2B workflows embedded in systems of record retain better, though I found no quantified B2B AI NRR to confirm this.
- Logistics and government had almost no 2026 evidence in my searches. That reflects lower venture activity, which may mean less competition, but it also means less proof of demand.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced NRR or gross-retention figures for B2B vertical AI companies.
- Logistics AI got no dedicated search, so I have no evidence on it.

## Where are the durable moats given falling inference costs and frontier labs moving into applications?

### Takeaway
Labs are moving explicitly into healthcare and financial services. OpenAI and Anthropic launched healthcare products in January 2026, and Anthropic launched financial-services agents in May 2026. Thin wrappers are therefore exposed. Durable moats come from proprietary outcome data, deep integrations with systems of record and counterparties (payers, carriers, clearinghouses), regulatory and compliance positioning, and pricing tied to outcomes. Model quality is not a moat.

### Cited Findings
- OpenAI launched ChatGPT Health on January 7, 2026, and Anthropic launched Claude for Healthcare on January 8, 2026 — [Azumo](https://azumo.com/artificial-intelligence/intelligent-developer/ai-intelligence-briefing-january-15-2026)
- Anthropic unveiled financial-services agents on May 5, 2026 for front-office, middle-office, risk and compliance, and portfolio-management work. OpenAI's finance offering is described as "mostly horizontal; vertical via partners" — [andrew.ooo (low-credibility aggregator citing Bloomberg)](https://andrew.ooo/answers/anthropic-financial-services-agents-vs-openai-wall-street-may-2026/)
- An analysis by Nelson Advisors argues that the labs' capital lets them absorb losses while competing with healthcare IT vendors — [Healthcare.digital](https://www.healthcare.digital/single-post/post-ipo-with-billions-of-dollars-could-anthropic-and-openai-pose-a-real-threat-to-healthcare-techn)
- I found no direct evidence of a specific startup being displaced by a lab product — [same search set](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/software-and-technology/openai-and-anthropic-now-hold-80-of-ai-50-venture-funding-as-enterprise-ready-startups-multiply.md)
- Strategic incumbents are buying or investing rather than building:
  - R1 is acquiring Humata Health — [HLTH](https://hlth.com/insights/news/r1-to-acquire-humata-health-to-expand-ai-powered-prior-authorization)
  - Guidewire invested in Sixfold — [Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2026/01/29/deals-sixfold-raises-30m-in-series-b-round/)
  - Baker Tilly and Sikich partnered with Basis — [Landbase](https://www.landbase.com/blog/fastest-growing-accounting-tech-companies-startups)
- AI gross margins improved from about 41% (2024) to about 52% (2026) as inference costs fell, but remain well below SaaS. Bessemer sees about 50–60% as healthy for AI-native companies — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/have-ai-gross-margins-really-turned-the-corner/); [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/why-ai-gross-margins-are-so-much-lower-than-saas-and-what-that-means-for-your-business/)
- Possible consolidation in the eval and observability tooling layer: ClickHouse may have acquired Langfuse (weak source, unconfirmed) — [Simplify](https://simplify.jobs/c/Braintrust-AI)

### Inferences
- Moats rank roughly as follows, strongest first:
  1. Proprietary outcome and feedback data, such as denial outcomes, loss ratios or settlement values
  2. Counterparty integrations and network position
  3. Regulatory trust (HIPAA, SOC 2, audit trails, state insurance rules)
  4. Workflow lock-in inside a system of record
  5. Brand among a niche buyer community
- Model or prompt advantage is not a moat.
- Incumbent exits through acquisition (R1–Humata) show a credible path to a sale at $10–100M of ARR in regulated verticals, even without reaching "AI decacorn" scale.
- Falling inference costs help application companies' margins, but they also lower the cost for competitors to enter. Pricing per outcome (per approved authorization, per closed claim, per completed close) captures value better than per-seat pricing.

### Gaps
- I have no quantitative evidence on how much inference prices fell from 2025 to 2026.
- I found no sourced case of a lab product measurably taking share from a vertical startup.

## What do recent VC funding data say about valuations, round sizes, and where capital is crowded versus scarce?

### Takeaway
2026 is a record year for US venture by deal value, but it is extremely concentrated. AI took 82.7% of year-to-date deal value through Q3, driven by lab megadeals; OpenAI and Anthropic took about 80% of AI 50 funding. Seed valuations for AI companies carry a large premium over non-AI. Capital is crowded in foundation models, coding, and first-wave vertical leaders. It is relatively scarce for non-AI software and for unglamorous verticals such as government and specialty trades.

### Cited Findings
- **Q3 2026** (PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor, released around October 8, 2026, via SiliconANGLE; primary not verified):
  - US deal value was about $98.4B, down roughly 40% from the prior quarter
  - Nine-month US deal value reached $515.8B, about 44% above the previous annual record
  - AI's share was 82.7% year to date and 65.9% in Q3, declining each quarter

## Which opportunities suit a founder with deep quantitative and econometric expertise?

### Takeaway
The best fits are opportunities where the hard problem is statistical inference rather than language generation:
- Causal measurement of AI and other business ROI (Opportunity 6)
- Rigorous LLM and agent evaluation (Opportunity 5)
- LLM-extracted economic and alternative data with point-in-time integrity (Opportunity 7)
- Causal pricing and demand forecasting (Opportunity 8)
- Insurance risk and loss modelling inside underwriting agents (Opportunity 3)
- Policy analytics for government (Opportunity 12)

In these areas, labs are less likely to compete, and methodology plus proprietary data form the moat.

### Cited Findings
- Buyers report AI spending outpacing measurement of its impact (ChurnZero 2026, 580 leaders) — [SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/news/churnzero-study-shows-ai-spend-outpaces-impact-measurement-in-customer-success-149163d7-0a2c-4766-9ffb-f4621cb8f93c)
- 31% of investment firms use AI to optimize investment or trading strategies. Alternative-data spending is $2.8B and growing 17% a year — [Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/blog/state-of-the-alternative-data-market-2026)
- Investors value evaluation as infrastructure: Braintrust raised at an $800M valuation — [Axios Pro](https://www.axios.com/pro/enterprise-software-deals/2026/02/17/ai-observability-braintrust-80-million-800-million)
- Underwriting is being rebuilt as AI agents (Sixfold, Pace), and incumbents such as Guidewire are investing strategically — [Insurance Edge](https://insurance-edge.net/2026/01/29/deals-sixfold-raises-30m-in-series-b-round/)
- Usage-based-pricing SaaS companies show higher revenue per employee (about $291K). That makes pricing science a lever with measurable value (secondary) — [Mean CEO](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11209)

### Inferences
- **Advantage:** An econometrician's edge lies in identification. They can tell vendors and buyers whether an AI agent caused a KPI change, using difference-in-differences, synthetic control, switchback experiments and the like. Few agent vendors can credibly do this, and buyers facing $37B of AI spend will be asked to justify it.
- **Path to market:** Start service-led (fixed-fee impact studies) and productize the repeated pipeline. This is a classic bootstrappable route.
- **Data products (Opportunity 7):** These reward econometric discipline: nowcasting, deseasonalization, point-in-time history, avoiding look-ahead bias. LLMs now make extraction from unstructured sources cheap, so the scarce asset is a validated, backtested signal, not the extraction itself.
- **Insurance (Opportunity 3):** This is the vertical-AI play closest to a quant skillset, since underwriting is risk pricing. The risk is longer enterprise sales cycles.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced funding or revenue data for causal-inference, experimentation or pricing-optimization startups in 2025–2026.
- I found no data on demand from government buyers for policy analytics.

## What capital is needed to reach profitability, and is bootstrapping feasible?

### Takeaway
Bootstrapping is feasible for service-led data, measurement and niche vertical products, where AI tooling sharply cuts build cost. It is not feasible for categories where the leaders raise $50–120M rounds (RCM, accounting ERP, horizontal tooling). For those, a capital-efficient path is a narrow wedge followed by a seed round, or a strategic exit to an incumbent.

### Cited Findings
- SaaS Capital data (via a secondary blog) shows:
  - 83% of bootstrapped private SaaS firms are profitable or near breakeven, versus 52% of equity-backed peers
  - Bootstrapped firms with $5–10M ARR generate about $177K per employee, versus about $152K for equity-backed peers, and bootstrappers grow about 20% a year
  — [Mean CEO](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11209)
- A directory lists bootstrapped AI agent companies, all **unverified, self-reported**: InstaLILY AI at $8.6M of 2025 revenue with 78 staff, and Inceptix AI at $330K with 3 staff — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/instalily.ai)
- AI gross margins of about 52% mean each revenue dollar carries more cost than SaaS (75–85%). Inference averages 23% of revenue at scaling AI B2B companies. Bessemer reports that fast-growing "Supernova" startups can run at about 25% gross margin, some negative, early on — [SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/have-ai-gross-margins-really-turned-the-corner/); [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/why-ai-gross-margins-are-so-much-lower-than-saas-and-what-that-means-for-your-business/)
- If raising, AI seed medians are about $25M post-money with about 18% dilution (Carta, April 2026) — [Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-vc-round-benchmarks-seed-series-a-b-2026/)

### Inferences
- **Bootstrappable** (roughly $100K–$500K of personal capital and 0–3 hires to reach about $1M ARR): Opportunities 6, 7 and 8, and possibly Opportunity 9 in a single trade.
  - Lower gross margins (about 50–60%) mean a bootstrapped AI company needs higher prices or outcome-based pricing to fund growth from cash flow.
- **Seed-scale** (about $1–3M): Opportunities 1, 2, 3, 4 and 10, which need compliance work (SOC 2, HIPAA) and enterprise sales.
- **Expected profitability:** Bootstrapped companies typically reach profitability around $1–3M ARR. Seed-funded vertical AI companies typically need $5–20M of total capital before profitability. This is my estimate, not sourced.
- **Founder-investor angle:** Bootstrap the quant-fit business, and use the domain insight it generates to angel-invest in second-wave vertical AI rounds at seed. Avoid late-stage AI rounds priced at around $1B with gross margins near 50%.
