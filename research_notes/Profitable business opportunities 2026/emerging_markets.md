## Key Question 1: What do 2025–2026 VC/PE data say about funding volumes, and which sectors are generating revenue and profits rather than just growth?

### Takeaway
African tech funding recovered to about $4.1B in 2025 (Partech), up 25% YoY. A record 41% of that was debt, mostly from DFIs, going to asset-heavy, revenue-generating models: off-grid solar/PAYGo, e-mobility and fintech lending books. H1 2026 was flat to down on most trackers (about $1.36–1.44B), with deal counts falling sharply and capital concentrating in a few large rounds, notably Spiro's $215M. The firms with disclosed profits are asset-finance and payments businesses (M-KOPA, Moniepoint), not marketplace or e-commerce plays.

### Inferences
- The investable center of gravity has moved from asset-light "blitzscaling" (marketplaces, quick-commerce) to asset-backed, cash-generative models financed with DFI debt: PAYGo devices, e-mobility, solar and lending books. Revenue-generating companies now get capital, and pure-growth startups struggle, as the falling deal count with flat volume shows.
- M-KOPA's falling product gross margin (27% to 15.9%) alongside its thin net profit suggests PAYGo profits come from credit and financing spread and scale, not hardware margin. Its returns are therefore sensitive to the cost of funds and FX.
- Debt making up 41% of funding means newcomers need models that generate predictable receivables (lease, PAYGo, subscription) so they can raise debt. Pure software or marketplace models will face scarce equity.
- India ($10.5B) is roughly 2.5x Africa's total, and LatAm (about $4B) is roughly on par with Africa. Africa remains small per capita, which suggests less competition for capital-efficient operators but also thin follow-on funding.

## Key Question 2: What do recent failures and downsizings in African fintech, e-commerce and quick-commerce teach about unit economics?

### Takeaway
Closures in 2024–2025 were concentrated in fintech and in B2B e-commerce and logistics. The common causes were negative contribution margins hidden by subsidized growth, heavy fixed-cost in-house logistics and sales forces, governance failures, and dependence on cheap equity. The survivors restructured toward asset-light, commission-based or asset-backed models.

### Cited Findings
- At least 18 African startups shut down in 2025, up from 12 in 2024. Fintech saw the most closures, which the author attributes partly to it receiving the most funding. — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/insight/african-startups-shutdown-2025/); [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/african-startups-shut-down-2025/); [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2025/12/04/2025-african-startup-review-unpacking-key-trends-and-events-startup-shutdowns/)
- Disclosed pre-closure funding destroyed by 2025 shutdowns was about $52.2M (one tally). — [The Condia](https://thecondia.com/african-startups-shut-down-2025/) (search summary; methodology unclear)
- Okra, a Nigerian open-banking API company, shut down in 2025. Contributing factors cited were a co-founder departure, loss of execution speed and reliance on screen-scraping. Bento, a Nigerian payroll company, collapsed amid a "spiral of mistrust and forensic scrutiny", which was a governance failure rather than purely a funding shortfall. — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/12/30/transparency-vs-the-silent-exit-how-african-startups-handled-crisis-in-2025-and-what-we-learned/). **Conflict:** some lists treat Okra as "restructuring" and Edukoya as "in administration" rather than shut down.
- Commentary attributes closures to weak unit economics, fragile operating models, growth built on cheap capital, higher global rates, unreliable infrastructure and regulatory fragmentation. — [Techpoint Africa](https://techpoint.africa/insight/african-startups-shutdown-2025/); [WeeTracker](https://weetracker.com/2025/12/04/2025-african-startup-review-unpacking-key-trends-and-events-startup-shutdowns/)
- **[DATED 2022–23]** Twiga Foods (Kenya B2B food distribution): in November 2022 it cut 211 staff and eliminated its in-house sales team, converting reps to commission-only agents. In August 2023 it cut 283 staff (33% of 850), replaced leased in-house delivery trucks with per-use contractors and launched a logistics marketplace, claiming a 40% reduction in logistics costs. The CEO cited customers' declining purchasing power. — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/21/twiga-disbands-in-house-delivery/); [TechCrunch 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/11/16/kenyas-twiga-dismisses-in-house-sales-team-affecting-21-of-it-employees)
- **[DATED 2023]** Copia (Kenya rural B2C e-commerce) made its third round of layoffs and exited Uganda, blaming "the economic downturn and the constrained capital markets". MarketForce (B2B) also downsized. — [ImpactAlpha](https://impactalpha.com/?p=102236); [African Marketing Confederation](https://africanmarketingconfederation.org/turbulence-in-africas-e-commerce-sector-continues-with-more-layoffs/)
- Some 2025 "comebacks" and pivots of earlier-failed models occurred (logistics, fintech, e-commerce). — [Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2025/12/09/from-logistics-to-fintech-e-commerce-to-mushrooms-inside-africas-year-of-unlikely-comebacks/)
- Contrast with the survivors: M-KOPA reached profitability after 15 years, with revenue tied to consumer receivables. — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/07/m-kopa-turns-first-ever-profit-revenue-surges-66-416/)

### Inferences
- Lesson 1: in low-ticket FMCG and food distribution, gross margins are often single-digit. Owning trucks, warehouses and salaried sales reps turns the model into a fixed-cost trap when volumes or purchasing power fall. Variable-cost logistics (marketplace or contractors) and commission-based agents are more resilient.
- Lesson 2: B2B e-commerce that tried to add credit (BNPL to retailers) on top of thin trading margins was exposed to both credit losses and FX. The financing layer needs separate, matched funding.
- Lesson 3: governance and transparency are value-critical. Fraud or mistrust episodes (Bento; in 2024 [DATED], Dash and others) destroyed companies faster than market conditions did.
- Lesson 4: models that won have hard collateral or lockable assets (PAYGo devices, e-bikes, solar), recurring payments and DFI debt access.
- For quick-commerce specifically, I found no African 2025–26 data. India's quick-commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart) is the relevant comparator but was not researched here.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous cohort-level data (e.g., CAC/LTV or contribution margin) for failed African e-commerce firms. Lessons are inferred from company statements and press.
- I found no 2025–26 status update on Twiga, Copia or MarketForce beyond a low-reliability blog.
- Specific failure cases from India, SE Asia and LatAm for 2025–26 were not researched.

## Key Question 3: How do macro conditions (currency depreciation, interest rates, debt distress, US tariffs and aid cuts) affect opportunities?

### Takeaway
The macro picture improved in Kenya and Nigeria through early 2026, with rate cuts, stabilizing currencies and rising reserves. A 2026 Middle East conflict then pushed up fuel and fertilizer prices, and the IMF cut its 2026 SSA growth forecast to about 4.3% (April). The IMF calls the aid cuts structural rather than cyclical. AGOA lapsed in September 2025 but was renewed retroactively. This creates both risks (FX, demand) and openings (local manufacturing, substitution for aid-funded services, private health supply chains).

### Cited Findings
- IMF REO SSA, April 2026 ("Hard-Won Gains Under Pressure"): regional growth is expected to fall to 4.3% in 2026, with heterogeneity. The Middle East conflict raised fuel and fertilizer prices, hitting non-resource-rich and fragile oil importers. — [IMF REO April 2026](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/reo/ssa/issues/2026/04/16/regional-economic-outlook-for-sub-saharan-africa-april-2026); [IMF press briefing](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/04/16/sp041626-subsaharan-africa-press-briefing-abebe-selassie)
- The IMF has a chapter titled "Aid Cuts in Sub-Saharan Africa: This Time Is Different". The decline in aid appears structural, not cyclical. — [IMF press transcript](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2026/04/16/tr-04162026-press-briefing-transcript-african-department-spring-meetings-2026); [IOL Business Report](https://iol.co.za/business-report/2026-04-16-imf-flags-mounting-risks-as-africas-hard-won-gains-come-under-pressure/)
- The October 2026 IMF update reportedly raised the SSA 2026 forecast to 4.5%, and bilateral aid is said to fall a further 12% in 2026. These come from secondary reports and were not verified against the IMF primary source. — [NAN News](https://nannews.ng/imf-projects-4-5-growth-for-sub-saharan-africa-in-2026-2027/)
- 83% of USAID programmes were confirmed ended in March 2025, and the US planned to exit WHO in January 2026. — [Pharmaceutical Technology](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/?p=361037)
- Kenya: CBK cut its policy rate to 8.75% in February 2026, its 10th consecutive cut. Inflation was 4.4% in January 2026, within the 2.5–7.5% target. CBK held at 8.75% in April 2026, citing oil-price risk, and described the shilling as stable. — [Focus Economics](https://www.focus-economics.com/countries/kenya/news/monetary-policy/kenya-central-bank-meeting-10-02-2026-central-bank-of-kenya-decreases-rates-in-february/); [allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202602260017.html); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/kenya/interest-rate/news/540270)
- Nigeria: CBN cut 50bp to 26.5% in February 2026. Inflation was 15.10% in January 2026 (rebased CPI). FX reserves were $50.45B (16 February 2026, a 13-year high), and the naira strengthened. **Conflict:** an April 2026 report says Nigeria and South Africa hiked rates afterwards. Unverified. — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2502-53250-nigeria-cuts-benchmark-interest-rate-as-reserves-climb-to-13-year-high)
- AGOA lapsed in September 2025. A one-year extension signed in early February 2026 restored duty-free access retroactively from 30 September 2025 through 31 December 2026, keeping the third-country fabric provision. The US House passed a 3-year extension bill in January 2026. A Kenyan government news item (September 2026) reports a further extension to 31 December 2028. That is unverified against USTR, and sources conflict. — [Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/123625-trump-extends-agoa-through-december-2026-kenya-among-countries-reap-benefits); [allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202601150552.html); [Kenya News Agency (search summary)](https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/?p=181226); [Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/agoa-extension-a-boon-for-kenyan-exporters/)
- Textiles and apparel are 90%+ of Kenya's AGOA exports. After the renewal, Kenyan exports to the US hit a record monthly high in March 2026. — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/economy/kenya-exports-to-us-hit-record-high-after-trump-agoa-renewal--5481446); [The Standard](https://thestandard.ke/sports/amp/business/2001540064/relief-for-kenyas-sh95b-exports-as-trump-renews-agoa-for-one-year)

### Inferences
- FX risk: businesses with USD-denominated inputs (solar panels, batteries, phones, pharmaceuticals) and local-currency revenue are most exposed. Currencies were calmer in 2026 (stable KES, stronger NGN), which favors entry now. Hedging and local-currency DFI debt remain essential, as M-KOPA's margin compression shows.
- Rates: Kenya's easing cycle (policy rate 8.75%) lowers local cost of funds for SME and asset-finance lenders. Nigeria's 26.5% policy rate keeps local lending expensive, which favors fee and payments models over balance-sheet lending there.
- Aid cuts create private-sector openings in health commodity supply chains, diagnostics, pharmacies and clinics, and possibly in last-mile service delivery formerly aid-funded. They also reduce household purchasing power and DFI/grant co-funding in some sectors.
- AGOA's uncertainty (1-year versus 3-year horizon) deters long-dated apparel factory investment. Investors should model a no-AGOA downside and use AfCFTA or EU EPA markets as a hedge.
- The Middle East conflict's fuel-price shock improves the relative economics of e-motorcycles and solar versus petrol and diesel.

- No debt-distress list (IMF/WB DSA ratings for 2026) was retrieved. Ghana, Zambia and Ethiopia restructuring status is not covered.
- US reciprocal tariff rates applied to African, Asian and LatAm exporters in 2025–26 were not retrieved.
- The October 2026 IMF REO figure was not verified.

## Key Question 4: Which opportunities benefit from development-finance co-investment (IFC, BII, DFC, Proparco) or blended finance?

### Takeaway
DFIs are now the dominant capital source: 64% of African PE/VC fund commitments, plus most of the 2025 venture debt. Energy access (Mission 300), e-mobility, off-grid solar, agribusiness and SME credit, and health supply chains have the deepest DFI and blended-finance pipelines.

### Cited Findings
- DFIs contributed 64% of African private-capital fundraising commitments in 2025, and African investors 21%. — [AVCA](https://www.avca.africa/data-intelligence/research-publications/2025-african-private-capital-activity-report/)
- DFI-driven venture debt (BII, Proparco, IFC, Afreximbank, Symbiotics, Mirova) funded off-grid solar, e-mobility and fintech lending books in 2025. — [Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/african-startup-funding-debt-2025/)
- BII invested £1.07B in Africa in 2025. Its 2026–2031 strategy commits at least 25% of new investment to frontier markets. — [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/1bn-plus-invested-in-african-businesses-last-year-bii-2026-06-25); [BII](https://www.bii.co.uk/en/news-insight/insight/articles/what-biis-strategy-means-for-africa/)
- BII and ILX closed a $15M MSME and agribusiness loan to an East African financial institution (March 2026) and plan up to $500M of co-financed debt. BII committed $65M across Zambia and Zimbabwe (October 2026) with technical assistance. — [BII](https://www.bii.co.uk/en/news-insight/news/british-international-investment-and-ilx-complete-first-joint-investment/); [NextBillion](https://nextbillion.net/news/bii-commits-65-million-in-zambia-and-zimbabwe-to-strengthen-financial-markets-and-unlock-investment-in-frontier-economies/)
- IFC and UK FCDO signed a DRC agribusiness programme in 2026 (£2.3M UK contribution, targeting 300+ women-owned SMEs and 5,000+ farmers). — [IFC](https://www.ifc.org/en/pressroom/2026/united-kingdom-and-ifc-partner-to-expand-agribusiness-and-accelerate-job-creation-)
- **[DATED 2024]** BII and FMO's CASA Plus cites a $74B agribusiness finance gap in SSA. — [BII](https://www.bii.co.uk/en/news-insight/news/bii-and-fmo-join-forces-with-the-uk-government-to-boost-finance-for-agribusinesses/)
- Mission 300 (World Bank and AfDB, 300M people connected by 2030): about 44M people connected per a March 2026 Rockefeller fact sheet, over 50M per the Africa Energy Portal (July 2026), and 59M per World Bank President Banga (October 2026). The WB+AfDB combined figure is 64M. ### Inferences
- The best DFI co-investment fit is in models that generate receivables or contracted cash flows: PAYGo solar, productive-use appliance financing, mini-grids with results-based financing (RBF), e-mobility leases, SME and agri-lending, warehouse receipts, and cold-chain-as-a-service for high-value horticulture, dairy and fish.
- Mission 300's off-grid half (roughly 150M people by 2030 if targets hold) creates a policy-backed demand pipeline for mini-grid and SHS developers. Watch national compacts and RBF windows.
- DFI dependence (64% of commitments) is a risk too. DFI priorities, such as frontier-market and climate mandates, shape what gets funded, and commercial LP appetite remains thin.

- No IFC 2026 blended-finance window details (e.g., IDA PSW) were retrieved.
- No confirmed 2026 cold-chain DFI transaction was found.

## Key Question 5: Where do development-economics RCTs (J-PAL, IPA, etc.) identify proven, scalable business models?

### Takeaway
The evidence base cautions against undifferentiated microcredit as a "transformative" business. Six or seven RCTs show modest or no average income effects and low take-up. It supports demand-side injections having large local multipliers (cash transfers in Kenya, 2.5x) and shows that small, well-timed price or financing nudges (fertilizer discounts) raise adoption. For businesses, this argues for product-plus-financing bundles timed to cash flows and for targeting high-ability entrepreneurs, rather than generic small loans.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATED 2022]** GiveDirectly general-equilibrium RCT, Kenya (Egger, Haushofer, Miguel, Niehaus & Walker, Econometrica 2022): about $1,000 one-time transfers to 10,500+ households in 653 villages, equal to over 15% of local GDP. It found large positive spillovers to non-recipients and firms, minimal price inflation, and a local multiplier of about 2.5 (versions report 2.5–2.7). Expansion was demand-led. — [Econometrica](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2022/11/01/General-Equilibrium-Effects-of-Cash-Transfers-Experimental-Evidence-From-Kenya); [Oxford Economics](https://economics.web.ox.ac.uk/node/2936131)
- **[DATED 2015]** Six microcredit RCTs (AEJ: Applied 2015): none found significant impacts on borrowers' household income. Take-up of new credit offers was low, about 17–33%. Meager (2019, pooling 7 trials) found effects around 5–7% of control means, with a credible interval including zero. — [IPA](https://poverty-action.org/news/microcredit-does-not-live-promise-transforming-lives-poor-six-studies-show); [MIT paper](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Six%20Randomized%20Evaluations%20of%20Microcredit.pdf); [J-PAL Finance insights](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/es/policy-insights/finance?lang=en); [NHSJS 2026 review](https://nhsjs.com/2026/operational-metrics-welfare-outcomes-and-the-microcredit-gap-a-critical-comparative-review/)
- Agricultural technology adoption: take-up of improved seeds, fertilizer and microinsurance is low. A Kenya study found small, time-limited fertilizer discounts (offered at harvest, when farmers have cash) substantially raised use. ATAI (de Janvry and Sadoulet) attributes low adoption largely to a deficit in effective supply. — [J-PAL Agriculture](https://www.povertyactionlab.org/sector/agriculture?lang=pt-br); [ATAI](https://www.atai-research.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Sadoulet_Intro-ATAI-technology-adoption__IEA-presentation-2.pdf) **[DATED]**

### Inferences
- Business implication 1: rural demand responds strongly to liquidity, as the cash-transfer multiplier shows. The shrinking of aid-funded cash programs in 2025–26 reduces this demand in some areas, which matters for rural FMCG and distribution businesses.
- Business implication 2: "supply-deficit" findings in agri-inputs suggest a profitable niche for reliable, quality-assured input distribution and agro-dealer networks with harvest-timed payment (cf. the "nudge" fertilizer study). One Acre Fund and Apollo Agriculture are the known operator analogues; their 2025–26 financials were not researched.
- Business implication 3: microcredit as a standalone business is not a poverty-alleviation silver bullet, but it can still be profitable for lenders. Lender profitability and borrower impact are distinct questions. Collateralized asset finance (PAYGo) has a better commercial record than unsecured micro-loans.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve RCT evidence on PAYGo solar (e.g., Kenya/Rwanda SHS evaluations), electric mobility, private schools (e.g., Bridge International Academies RCT in Kenya, Kremer et al.), drug-shop and pharmacy accreditation, or index insurance. All are relevant but unverified in this session.
- Evidence on high-growth SME interventions (e.g., consulting RCTs and business-plan competitions such as Nigeria's YouWiN) was not retrieved.

## Key Question 6 (Objective): Evaluation of 8–12 concrete opportunities

### Takeaway
On 2025–26 evidence, the strongest risk-adjusted opportunities combine hard-asset collateral or recurring payments with DFI-financeable cash flows and macro tailwinds. In rough order of evidence strength: (1) PAYGo asset finance (solar, phones, appliances); (2) e-motorcycle battery-swap and leasing; (3) mini-grids and productive-use energy; (4) SME and merchant payments plus embedded credit; (5) pharmacy, diagnostics and health supply chains (aid-cut gap); (6) agri-input distribution and agro-processing; (7) cold chain for high-value perishables; (8) AGOA/AfCFTA-linked apparel and light manufacturing (policy-risky); (9) BPO and AI data services (legal and policy risk in Kenya); (10) asset-light B2B FMCG distribution (high-failure category; only with lean models). Market sizes and unit economics are often not publicly available. Specific gaps are listed below.

### Cited Findings (by opportunity)
**1. PAYGo asset finance (solar, smartphones, appliances): East Africa and Nigeria**
- Comparable: M-KOPA had about $600M revenue in 2025 (+45%), 10M cumulative customers and a thin net profit ($4.8M). Product gross margin fell to 15.9%. — [FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/m-kopa-marks-15-years-with-10-million-customers-and-600m-record-revenue-6c2c790c); [AU-Startups](https://au-startups.com/news/m-kopa-600m-revenue-10m-customers-pivot)
- Financing: cleantech and off-grid solar took about $627M of venture debt in 2025, from DFIs. — [Techloy](https://www.techloy.com/african-startup-funding-debt-2025/)
- Capital required: high (receivables financing). Exit: strategic or PE. M-KOPA's long path to profit (15 years) indicates patient capital is needed.

**2. Electric motorcycles (battery swap, lease-to-own): Kenya, Uganda, Rwanda, Nigeria, Ethiopia**
- Comparable: Spiro reports 100k–135k+ bikes and 2,500 swap stations, has raised about $365M+ in equity and debt in about 12 months, and has a near-$1B reported valuation. — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-industry/0206-56078-electric-mobility-firm-spiro-raises-215-million-for-continental-expansion); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/155076-spiro-lands-18m-more-from-africa-go-green-fund-doubling-debt-to-36m/)
