# Asset-backed cash flows clear Kenya's steep hurdle

As of October 2026, the Kenyan opportunities that credibly clear a risk-adjusted hurdle share one trait: they lend against, or process, something that can be collateralised or exported. Four stand out. The first is SME working-capital and invoice finance underwritten on M-Pesa till data and KRA eTIMS invoices. The second is a licensed microfinance bank, bought for its deposit franchise. The third is e-mobility asset finance outside the crowded two-wheeler segment. The fourth is hard-currency agro-processing that policy protects: macadamia kernels under the raw-nut export ban, avocado oil and frozen lines into China's zero-tariff window, and oilseed crushing for a feed market that is structurally short of protein. The binding comparator is not the T-bill. It is a **tax-free infrastructure bond paying 11.75–12.74%**, which still ran about 6 points above 6.8% inflation in September 2026. Against that benchmark, a passive property portfolio yielding 7–9.5% gross fails outright. An operating business needs roughly **25–30% expected KES IRR if it is established and cash-generative, and 35–40% if it is greenfield**, measured after weighting for the chance of failure rather than conditioned on survival. A second tier clears the bar only under specific structures: develop-to-REIT student housing and Grade A logistics, asset management, SHA claims finance, specialist outpatient care and LPG distribution. The evidence underneath is thinner than the market story. Kenya's "Africa's #1" 2025 funding total was 82% five asset-finance firms and mostly debt. Thirteen collapsed startups had raised about US$718m. The flagship survivor, M-KOPA, earned about US$4.8m net in 2025 with negative operating cash flow. Almost no unit economics in this report come from a source. Every IRR is my estimate, and every comparable is a survivor. The macro picture is calm on the surface: 5.1% growth, the shilling flat near KES 130/USD and the policy rate at 8.75%. Underneath, debt service absorbs about 70% of revenue, there is no IMF programme, the state owes suppliers KES 466bn, and an election falls in August 2027. Together these skew risk toward shilling depreciation and late government payment. That favours businesses that earn dollars or hold their own collateral, and it argues against anything the state pays for.

## A tax-free 12.5% sovereign yield sets the floor

Kenya's macro regime looks stable, but the state is short of cash. **Q2 2026 GDP grew 5.1%**, after 5.3% in Q1, with ICT up 7.2% and manufacturing up only 2.2% ([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/business/kenya-s-economy-grows-5-1pc-despite-iran-war-price-shocks-5635400)). **Headline inflation rose to 6.8% in September 2026**, the highest since January 2024, driven by transport (+15.6%) and food (+9.5%) as the Middle East oil shock passed through ([KNBS CPI](https://www.knbs.or.ke/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Kenya-Consumer-Price-Indices-and-Inflation-Rates-September-2026.pdf)). The MPC held the **Central Bank Rate at 8.75%** on 7 October ([CBK](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/2026/10/07/mpc-retains-the-cbr-at-8-75-percent-3/)). The shilling traded at **KES 129.81/USD** with **US$14.7bn of reserves (5.9 months of import cover)** ([Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/kenyan-shilling-holds-firm-as-forex-reserves-drop-by-221million/)). The fiscal side is the weak point. **Debt service consumed 70.1% of revenue** at June 2026, against a 10-year average of 53.6% ([Cytonn](https://cytonnreport.com/research/kenyas-public-debt)). Public debt passed KES 13tn ([Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/kenyas-public-debt-breaches-ksh-13-trillion-record/)). National pending bills to suppliers stood at **KES 465.87bn** ([Capital FM](https://capitalfm.co.ke/news/2026/06/kenya-pending-bills-sh465-87-billion-cob-march-2026)). The CBK governor says IMF funding "is not in any of our planning" ([Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/127880-cbk-governor-explains-why-kenyas-new-imf-deal-has-no-start-date)). The general election is on **10 August 2027** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Kenyan_general_election)).

The state's financing need is why risk-free returns are so high. The 364-day T-bill cleared at **9.04%** on 15 October ([Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/kenya-91-day-treasury-bill-draws-ksh-49-8bn-in-bids/)). The August infrastructure-bond reopening offered **coupons of 11.75%, 12.667% and 12.737%, free of withholding tax** ([Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/cbk-opens-ksh150bn-tax-free-infrastructure-bond-sale/)). Money-market funds yield about **9.3%** ([Rio Times](https://www.riotimesonline.com/kenya-money-market-fund-yields-9-3-percent-2026)). The average bank lending rate was **14.4%** in September, and the gross NPL ratio was **13.9%** ([Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/private-sector-credit-growth-accelerates-to-10-6pc-in-september/); [Business.co.ke](https://business.co.ke/private-sector-credit-growth-hits-10-6-as-npls-fall-to-13-9/)). The NSE all-share index is up about 29–32% year to date, with a **5.8% dividend yield** ([Cytonn Q3 review](https://cytonn.com/uploads/downloads/cytonn-q32026-markets-review-vf.pdf)). Adding 7–9% nominal earnings growth to that yield gives an expected KES equity return of about 13–15%. That is my inference, and it is only a modest premium over a tax-free sovereign after a 30% rally.

The research notes contain one correction that an economist should make. The Kenya notes estimate the USD sovereign spread at 400–550bp, assuming a US 10-year yield of 4–4.5%. The global notes show the 10-year at **5.24% on 9 October 2026**, after the Fed raised rates to 3.75–4.00% in September ([ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/fixed-income-content-hub/treasury-yields-snapshot-october-9-2026/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/16/fed-rate-decision-september-2026.html)). Kenya's Eurobonds yielded **8.48% (2034) and 9.89% (2039)** ([People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/business/cbk-kenya-eurobond-yields-rise-as-bond-market-faces-fresh-pressure)), so the actual spread is closer to **320–470bp**. That fits Moody's B3 upgrade in January 2026 ([CNBC Africa](https://www.cnbcafrica.com/2026/moodys-upgrades-kenyas-rating-to-b3-on-improved-buffers-that-ease-default-risk)). The narrower spread does not lower the Kenyan hurdle, because the US base rate is higher. Converting KES returns to USD needs an assumed path for the shilling, and none of the notes found a published forecast. I use a PPP-style drift of 3.5–4.5% a year, the Kenya–US inflation gap, with fatter tails around the election. On that basis the tax-free infrastructure bond is worth about 8–9% in expected USD terms, which brackets the Eurobond curve. That is the cross-check one would want.

The operating-business hurdle comes from a different source. US private-equity respondents in the 2026 Pepperdine survey want a median **20% IRR for companies with US$1–5m EBITDA**, with US Treasuries at about 5.2% ([Chinook Advisors](https://chinookadvisors.com/news/key-takeaways-from-the-2026-pepperdine-private-capital-markets-report/)). Kenya's USD risk-free equivalent is 3–4 points higher. A Kenyan established-SME hurdle is therefore about 23–24% in USD, or about 27–28% in KES. That is above the 20–25% KES the Kenya macro notes proposed, which I consider too low for a B-rated frontier market with a 14.4% cost of debt.

Two adjustments matter more than the headline numbers. The first is **survival weighting**. Business plans and comparables report returns conditional on survival. The hurdle has to be met in expectation. Suppose a venture has a 30% chance of total loss over five years. To deliver an expected 35% IRR, the survivors must earn about 45%. If the loss probability is 50%, survivors must earn 55% (my arithmetic: ((1+r)^5/(1−p))^(1/5)−1). The second concerns the investor's stated willingness to take any risk. Hall and Woodward show that the certainty-equivalent value of founding a VC-backed company is slightly negative at CRRA = 2, because the founder gets nothing in nearly three-quarters of outcomes. The penalty disappears only as risk aversion approaches zero ([NBER w14219](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14219)). Risk tolerance therefore removes the utility wedge. It does not remove the opportunity cost, and markets set that cost: the tax-free sovereign, plus the returns DFIs and private-equity co-investors demand. A risk-neutral investor still needs to beat the expected-return hurdle. The only difference is that such an investor can concentrate capital to do so.

## Survivors and secondary sources flatter most Kenyan theses

The provenance of the evidence deserves explicit discounting. Several primary sources could not be fetched directly in this research: CBK, KNBS, Cytonn, Knight Frank and most Kenyan tech outlets. Many figures therefore come from press summaries of those documents. Where two summaries exist, they often disagree. Three examples show the scale. AFA puts 2025 horticultural exports at **KES 143.78bn**, about US$1.1bn ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/counties/central/2026-06-02-flower-exports-bloom-to-over-sh143bn-amid-rising-demand)), while KNBS-based reporting gives **US$1.68bn** ([FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/africa/article/9841203/kenya-ramps-up-cold-chain-investment-as-post-harvest-food-losses-reach-us-578-million/)). M-KOPA's 2024 result appears as a KES 1.2bn profit in one source and a restated US$21.2m loss in another ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2025/10/07/m-kopa-turns-first-ever-profit-revenue-surges-66-416/); [AU-Startups](https://au-startups.com/news/m-kopa-600m-revenue-10m-customers-pivot)). Official series put 2022 TVET enrolment anywhere from 297,000 to 562,499 ([Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/counties/article/2001556846/tvet-enrolment-surges-as-kenyan-youth-turn-to-technical-skills); [Education News](https://educationnews.co.ke/?p=517213)). None of the twelve-plus opportunities in this report has disclosed, audited unit economics at the level an investor would underwrite: CAC, loss rates, margin per tonne, or payback. Capex-per-tonne and payback figures from vendors (C&I solar, for example) are marketing claims.

Selection bias is worse than measurement error. Kenya topped African tech funding in 2025 with **US$1.04bn** on Partech's count ([Partech](https://partechpartners.com/news/2025-partech-africa-tech-vc-report-african-tech-funding-rebounds-to-us41b-driven-by-record-debt-activity-and-disciplined-equity-growth)). But **d.light, Sun King, M-KOPA, BURN and PowerGen took about 82% of that total**, mostly as debt and securitisations ([EcoNews Kenya](https://econews.co.ke/2026/02/17/clean-energy-dominates-kenya-venture-capital-in-2025/)). In H1 2026 Kenya raised only **US$126m**, its weakest half since early 2021 ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/kenya-knocked-off-africa-s-startup-funding-top-spot-5546952)). In Q3 2026 Kenyan startups raised about US$86m, **over 92% of it debt** ([Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/african-ventures-raise-us-674million-in-q3/)). The failures are large in the same proportion. Thirteen Kenyan startups that collapsed in about five years had raised about **Sh93bn (≈US$718m)**, led by Twiga, Copia, Gro Intelligence and KOKO ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/151415-kenyan-startup-graveyard-13-collapsed-firms-burned-through-718m/)). That figure is capital raised, not confirmed losses, since some of it was debt that may have been recovered. **Twiga entered statutory administration on 17 August 2026** after raising about US$185m and running 12 years without a profit ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/09/14/kenyan-startup-twiga-foods-enters-administration-after-years-of-financial-pressure/)). The most celebrated survivor shows how thin the winning margin is. M-KOPA's filings show **US$363.4m of contract revenue in 2025, net profit of about US$4.8m and an operating cash outflow of US$69.0m** ([Launch Base Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/10/08/m-kopa-profit-loss/)). Product gross margin fell from 27.0% to 15.9% ([AU-Startups](https://au-startups.com/news/m-kopa-600m-revenue-10m-customers-pivot)). M-KOPA earns its money on the financing spread, not on hardware, and it took 15 years to reach profit.

The comparables cited throughout this report suffer from the same selection: Victory Farms, Acorn, Kenya Nut, BasiGo and Cancer Care Kenya. They are in the evidence because they raised money and were reported on. The press covers fundraises, not exits or IRRs, and no denominator of comparable failures exists for any sector. The base-rate data are dated and also need careful reading. The KNBS 2016 MSME survey found that **46% of businesses that closed did so in their first year** ([KIPPRA](https://kippra.or.ke/14355-2/)). That is a distribution of closures by age, not a first-year hazard rate. The 2023 FinAccess MSE tracking survey found **71.6% of firms still operating** ([KNBS NADA](https://statistics.knbs.or.ke/nada/index.php/catalog/129)). That figure comes from a panel with its own attrition, and both samples are dominated by micro-retailers, not capitalised formal ventures. They justify a large survival haircut, but they do not identify one.

The academic literature makes the same point from the other side. Private business equity has historically earned roughly public-equity returns despite extreme concentration, the "private equity premium puzzle" ([Moskowitz & Vissing-Jørgensen](https://www.nber.org/papers/w8876)). In one VC dataset about 55% of investments returned less than cost, and about 6% returned 5x or more and generated about 60% of returns ([Kerr, Nanda & Rhodes-Kropf](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.28.3.25)). Markups have risen only in the upper tail of firms; the median firm's markup did not move ([De Loecker, Eeckhout & Unger](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23687)). The returns therefore sit in firm-specific moats, not in sectors. Even the best-documented operator path shows this. Search funds report a **33.9% aggregate IRR**, falling to about 20% once the top decile is excluded, and roughly one searcher in three never acquires a company ([CapitalPad](https://capitalpad.com/search-fund-statistics/)). The founder-quality evidence also bears on the investor-founder's own role. The mean founder age among the fastest-growing 1-in-1,000 startups is 45, and prior experience in the same industry predicts success ([Azoulay et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/p/cen/cpaper/2018-03.html)). In Kenya, that argues for backing or hiring sector operators rather than learning agribusiness or credit underwriting on the job.

Kenyan RCT evidence sharpens two theses that appear later. Six microcredit RCTs found **no significant effect on borrowers' household income** and low take-up ([IPA](https://poverty-action.org/news/microcredit-does-not-live-promise-transforming-lives-poor-six-studies-show)). Lender profitability and borrower welfare are separate questions, but the gap between them is where political and regulatory backlash against consumer lenders begins. One Acre Fund's bundle of input credit and training raised western Kenyan maize output by **26% and profits by 16%** ([NBER w26054](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w26054/revisions/w26054.rev0.pdf)), but the gains did not persist after farmers left the programme ([La Follette School](https://lafollette.wisc.edu/research/the-impact-of-one-acre-funds-small-farm-program/)). Any processor that relies on outgrowers has to budget for permanent extension and credit, not a one-off subsidy.

## Lending against M-Pesa cash flows is the deepest profit pool

Kenya's largest private profit pool is the financial intermediation the state crowds out. M-Pesa earned **KSh 182.7bn in FY2026 (+13.4%)** on 46.4bn transactions ([tech-ish](https://tech-ish.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy26-mpesa-results-explained/)). **Fuliza overdraft disbursements grew 49% to KSh 1.47tn** ([Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/05/07/safaricom-fy2026-results-earnings-analysis/)), which shows very large demand for short-tenor credit. The payment rail belongs to Safaricom. The opening is in what can be underwritten on top of it. CBK had licensed **281 digital credit providers by 30 September 2026**, which had issued KSh 165.1bn of loans by August ([Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/127462-cbk-approves-29-new-digital-lenders-licensed-providers-hit-281)). Consumer lending is crowded. B2B working capital is thinner, and it now has a better information set. Under 2026 enforcement, input VAT and expense deductions are allowed only against valid eTIMS invoices ([biasharaguide, secondary](https://biasharaguide.co.ke/articles/significant-economic-presence-tax-kenya/)). That turns supplier invoices into verified, machine-readable collateral. Debt funding for receivables books is available locally: Sun King's **US$156m KES-denominated securitisation**, funded by Stanbic, KCB and Absa ([Megaproject](https://megaproject.com/news/oilandgas/156m-sun-king-securitization-to-deliver-solar-for-over-a-million-kenyans)), Mogo's KSh 800m from I&M and Ecobank ([Dawan Africa](https://www.dawan.africa/news/mogo-secures-ksh-800-million-from-iandm-bank-and-ecobank-to-boost-sme-lending)), and IFC first-loss guarantees behind 4G Capital's bank lines ([Business Tech Africa](https://www.businesstechafrica.co.za/article/african-smes-are-finding-another-way-to-borrow-money)). One single vendor source reports a **KES 100m minimum capital** for non-deposit-taking credit providers, which needs checking against the statute ([HelloDuty](https://helloduty.com/blogs/data-protection-act-kenya-all-you-need-to-know)).

Whether this clears the hurdle depends almost entirely on the spread, and the sensitivity is extreme. Here is an illustration with my own assumed parameters, none of them sourced. Take a book funded 80% by debt at 15%, with operating costs of 10% of assets and a 30% tax rate. At a 36% annual gross yield (3% a month) and 8% losses, it earns about **21% after-tax ROE**, just under the hurdle. Clearing 25–30% needs about 3.25% a month. At 48% yield it earns about 63%. At 36% yield with losses equal to the sector NPL ratio of 13.9%, it roughly breaks even. At a 24% yield it loses money. The business clears the bar only at monthly pricing levels that invite rate caps and ODPC scrutiny. M-KOPA's thin realised margin suggests the true spread is narrower than headline pricing implies. The defensible version is therefore B2B and invoice-backed. It sells to merchants and suppliers of large corporates, not payday consumers. It is funded in KES to avoid the currency mismatch that compressed M-KOPA. It uses DFI first-loss cover to lever equity four to six times.

A licence is a scarcer asset than a lending algorithm. Moniepoint entered Kenya in March 2026 by buying **78% of Sumac Microfinance Bank**, which gave it a deposit-taking licence while new licences remain frozen ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/03/26/nigerias-moniepoint-enters-kenya-with-78-stake-in-sumac-microfinance/)). For a large-budget investor, buying a small MFB and digitising it replaces 15% wholesale funding with retail deposits. That is the cheapest way to widen the spread in the arithmetic above. The main risks are the legacy loan book and the timing of CBK change-of-control approval. No published valuation benchmark for Kenyan MFBs was found.

E-mobility asset finance is the second credit play, and it is reinforced by the oil shock. EPRA raised diesel by a record KES 40.30 to **KES 206.84/litre** in April 2026 ([Africanews](https://africanews.com/2026/04/15/kenya-fuel-prices-surge-amid-global-oil-crisis-and-domestic-controversy)). EV registrations rose from **4,048 in 2023 to 28,754 in 2025** ([CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/06/04/calls-for-policy-consistency-continuity-as-kenyas-electric-mobility-sector-highlights-milestones/)). BasiGo has put 100+ e-buses on the road for 300+ SACCO operators on pay-as-you-drive terms ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/06/1145216/2026-climate-tech-companies-to-watch-basigo-fleet-electric-buses/)). Its CEO's claim of 5–10x better operator ROI than diesel is unaudited. Spiro's **US$215m equity raise** in June 2026 shows two-wheelers are being won by scale ([ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/spiro-raises-215-million-to-scale-e-mobility-platform-across-africa/)). A new entrant should therefore finance matatus, e-cargo and trucks, or sell charging and swapping as a service to OEMs, using DFI debt. The binding uncertainties are battery residual value, for which no Kenyan data exist, FX on imported vehicles, and the annual review of the e-motorcycle excise exemption in each Finance Bill.

The SHA payment crisis creates a financing gap, but it is sovereign credit risk dressed as healthtech. Unpaid SHA claims at county hospitals rose from **Sh8.29bn to Sh26.87bn** between December 2025 and March 2026 ([Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/health/373606/county-hospitals-under-strain-as-sha-debt-rises-to-sh2687-billion-cob-nyakango)). Private and faith-based facilities claimed Sh76bn owed in mid-2025 ([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/health/private-hospitals-face-closure-as-sha-owes-sh76b-in-unpaid-claims-5163100)). Only about 3.3m of roughly 19m enrolees were actively contributing ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/private-hosp-get-over-half-of-sh4-6bn-shif-payment-in-shift-4980262)). That is a structural funding gap, not an administrative backlog. The investable form is factoring of *approved* claims with a hard cap on SHA exposure, paired with revenue-cycle software for the 2026–2029 HAKIKA contracting cycle ([Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/health/hospitals-sha-contracts/)). Abroad, revenue-cycle AI is a proven category: Candid Health's ARR grew 190% ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/candid-health/funding-rounds/series-d-2026-07)), and R1's August 2026 acquisition of Humata Health shows incumbents buy rather than build ([HLTH](https://hlth.com/insights/news/r1-to-acquire-humata-health-to-expand-ai-powered-prior-authorization)). Kenya has no equivalent exit buyer yet.

Two regulatory options belong in a small, diversified sleeve rather than the core. Final VASP regulations covering stablecoin issuers were reportedly published on 22 July 2026. This rests on a single secondary source, and no licences are confirmed ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/ke/en/assets/pdf/legal-alert-virtual-assets-service-providers-2026.pdf)). That makes KES or USD stablecoin rails for Kenya–East Africa–China B2B trade legally conceivable but untested. Kenya's August 2026 carbon rule book caps international transfers at **10 MtCO2e to 2030** ([BusinessDay/AP](https://www.businessday.co.za/world/2026-08-04-kenya-unveils-carbon-market-rule-book-and-caps-overseas-sale-of-carbon-credits/)). Carbon revenue is therefore worth something only for projects that already hold authorisation. KOKO's collapse after the state refused its Letter of Authorisation is the counterfactual ([African Climate Wire](https://africanclimatewire.org/2026/02/carbon-markets-sovereign-risk-and-clean-cooking-what-the-koko-case-tells-africa/)).

### Digital services earn dollars but carry concentration risk

USD-revenue services are a natural hedge against the shilling, but they carry client-concentration risk. Sama issued **1,108 redundancy notices** in April 2026 after a single client, Meta, left ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/04/16/kenya-meta-facebook-sama-content-moderators/7c5345ee-39b1-11f1-90c4-9772c7fabc03_story.html)). Kenyan AI data workers reportedly earn **US$1.46–3.74/hour against US$21–27** for US moderators ([Outsource Accelerator](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/kenya-ai-data-workers/)). Draft fair-pay standards will narrow that gap ([ITWeb](https://itweb.africa/article/kenya-sets-standards-for-ai-workers/raYAyMorGzX7J38N)). The surviving margin is in expert evaluation and RLHF work and in East African language data, sold to diversified clients with compliance priced in. Hyperscale compute is not a private play. The **US$1bn Microsoft–G42 Olkaria project stalled** because Kenya could not guarantee about 1 GW against roughly 3 GW of national capacity ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-and-g42-data-center-in-kenya-stalled-due-to-lack-of-power-capacity/)). Mid-size colocation is real but contested, with Digital Realty's 6.2 MW NBO2 opening in September 2026 ([W.Media](https://w.media/digital-realty-expands-kenyan-campus-with-6-2-mw-data-center/)). Lessons from global AI also transfer. Vertical AI was only **US$3.5bn of about US$37bn** of 2025 enterprise generative-AI spend ([No Jitter/Menlo](https://www.nojitter.com/ai-automation/menlo-ventures-estimates-19-billion-in-gen-ai-spend-during-2025)). AI products run gross margins of about 52%, against 75–85% for SaaS ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/have-ai-gross-margins-really-turned-the-corner/)). The frontier labs entered healthcare in January 2026 ([Azumo](https://azumo.com/artificial-intelligence/intelligent-developer/ai-intelligence-briefing-january-15-2026)). Swahili voice agents for SACCOs and clinics are viable only as applications on top of others' models, with proprietary speech and outcome data as the moat. An economist-founder has a less obvious USD-earning option that is cheap to start from Nairobi: causal measurement of AI ROI, given that spend is outpacing measurement ([ChurnZero via SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/news/churnzero-study-shows-ai-spend-outpaces-impact-measurement-in-customer-success-149163d7-0a2c-4766-9ffb-f4621cb8f93c)), and LLM-extracted economic data for a **US$2.8bn alternative-data market growing 17%** ([Neudata](https://www.neudata.co/blog/state-of-the-alternative-data-market-2026)). Neither has sourced revenue comparables.

## Policy-made scarcities pay exporters in hard currency, until revoked

The best non-tech opportunities exploit gaps created by trade policy and by shortages, and they earn dollars. Kenya now holds preferential access to the EU (permanent), to the US through AGOA until **31 December 2028** ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/2026-09-05-trump-signs-agoa-extension-hands-kenya-exporters-lifeline-to-2028)), and to China under an Early Harvest zero-tariff deal running **1 May 2026 to 30 April 2028** ([HKTDC](https://research.hktdc.com/en/article/MjI2NDc3NDY5Mg)). Early results are steep but come off a tiny base: avocado-oil exports to China up 700% ([Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/kenyas-exports-to-china-surge-after-zero-tariff-deal-n391240)).

**Macadamia processing** has the clearest policy rent. AFA reinstated a ban on exporting raw nuts in July 2025 ([FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/africa/article/9752103/kenya-bans-export-and-transit-of-raw-macadamia-nuts/)), and export value reportedly rose **73% to US$66m** ([Food Business Africa](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/?p=2162491)). One trade newsletter quotes farmgate in-shell prices of **KES 130–200/kg against organic kernel FOB of EUR 23.5–25.25/kg** ([Commodity Board](https://commodity-board.com/kenyan-organic-macadamia-fob-prices-hold-firm-amid-policy-shift)). At an assumed 25–30% kernel recovery, raw material costs about US$3.3–6.2 per kg of kernel (my arithmetic). Conventional kernel sells for less than the organic quote, so the spread is wide but unmeasured. The thesis carries a live policy hazard. Growers with unsold stock asked AFA in 2026 to suspend the ban ([Food Business Africa](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/kenyas-macadamia-backlog-forces-review-of-export-ban-as-farmers-count-mounting-losses/)), and reports conflict on whether it was suspended. **Avocado** is short of compliant supply, not of fruit. Production is forecast at a record **727,000 t in 2026** ([Fructidor](https://fructidor.com/en/news/news-detail/d665ba8f-c044-4367-997f-3aa177519922)), while 2025 exports fell to **121,000 t (US$159m)** because of quality problems and stricter sea-shipment rules ([USDA FAS](https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/Report/DownloadReportByFileName?fileName=Avocado+_Nairobi_Kenya_KE2026-0007)). Grade-1 Hass at the farmgate costs KES 85–100/kg, against roughly US$1.44/kg FOB in 2024 ([FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/article/9872275/kenyan-avocado-growers-face-cultivation-and-export-grade-challenges)). Handling immature fruit now means **immediate revocation of the packhouse licence** ([FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/africa/article/9824427/kenya-enforces-stricter-avocado-export-rules-as-season-opens/)). That favours compliant packhouses with oil and frozen lines that absorb reject-grade fruit. It does not favour traders. **Cut flowers** are the most proven export: KES 81.3bn in 2025, with volumes up 27% ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/counties/central/2026-06-02-flower-exports-bloom-to-over-sh143bn-amid-rising-demand)). They are an acquisition play, not a greenfield one, now that 20-ft container rates have jumped from US$1,000–2,000 to over US$3,000–4,000 ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/industry/strait-of-hormuz-troubles-put-kenya-on-fresh-price-alert-5592482)).

**Feed and oilseeds** combine import substitution with a domestic shortage. Feed millers run at **50–60% of capacity**, soybean meal is largely imported, and the legal status of GMO feed imports remains unresolved ([The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/2026-07-17-feed-industry-calls-for-reforms-to-make-animal-food-affordable)). Edible-oil imports cost about **KES 100bn a year** ([People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/business/trade-cs-lee-kinyanjui-pushes-for-local-alternatives-to-slash-ksh100b-edible-oil-imports/amp)). A sunflower or canola crusher sells oil into the import gap and cake into the feed gap. Its natural customer base is fast-growing aquaculture: Lake Victoria cage output doubled from **13,700 t to 28,100 t** between 2021 and 2025 ([Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-agriculture/1703-53851-kenya-s-fish-farming-boom-draws-new-bet-from-victory-farms)). Victory Farms says in-house feed cut its feed costs by about 30% ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/victory-farms-raises-sh1-9bn-to-scale-tilapia-output-5417840)). Its KSh 750m filing for about 30,000 t of Migori capacity ([Food Business Africa](https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/?p=2157044)) implies roughly KES 25,000 of cage capex per tonne of annual capacity. That is my calculation, and it excludes the hatchery, feed mill and working capital. A Senate petition to restrict new cages ([SeafoodSource](https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/kenya-s-senate-petitioned-to-control-the-spread-of-cage-fish-farming-in-lake-victoria)) is the risk to the farm's social licence. Multi-user cold chain is the infrastructure underneath all of this, given an estimated **US$578m a year of post-harvest losses** and Cold Solutions' new US$19m raise. Its returns depend on utilisation, which no source measures ([FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/africa/article/9841203/kenya-ramps-up-cold-chain-investment-as-post-harvest-food-losses-reach-us-578-million/)).

Policy rents should be priced as options with a hazard rate, because Kenya revokes them often. In 2025–26 alone: the Treasury authorised 500,000 t of duty-free rice, and a court cut it to 250,000 t ([Nation](https://nation.africa/kenya/business/government-clears-way-for-importation-of-duty-free-rice-5522940)). The 15% corporate tax rate for large housing developers was abolished ([Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/kenya/tax/finance-act-2026-tax-alert-on-notable-changes)). Net-metering export credit was cut to 50% and backdated to July 2025 ([pv magazine](http://www.pv-magazine.com/2026/09/22/kenya-backdates-solar-export-charges-by-over-a-year/)). An opposition governor has vowed to "reclaim" leased sugar mills after 2027 ([People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/news/natembeya-vows-to-reclaim-nzoia-and-mumias-sugar-factories-in-2027/amp)). Suppose the annual reversal hazard is about 25%; that figure is illustrative, not estimated. The expected life of a rent is then about four years, so capex that depends on it should pay back in about three. That rule puts macadamia cracking and avocado oil lines, which are modular and redeployable, inside the bar. It puts sugar-mill leases outside it. It also excludes greenfield EPZ apparel, which depends on AGOA. Apparel exports were KES 58bn in 2025 ([BusinessToday](https://businesstoday.co.ke/agoa-extension-a-boon-for-kenyan-exporters/)), but a new factory cannot pay back before the 2028 AGOA cliff. Buying existing, compliant capacity is the only defensible entry.

## Property fails passively; services win on capital-light compounding

Kenyan property income cannot beat the tax-free sovereign. Nairobi residential yields averaged **5.9%** in Q1 2026, with Westlands at 9.8% ([Cytonn](https://cytonnreport.com/research/cytonn-q12026-markets-2)). Retail yields 8.8% on top of a **3.6m sq ft oversupply** ([Cytonn](https://cytonnreport.com/research/cytonn-2026-markets)). Prime offices have been stuck at **8.5%**, which Knight Frank calls "comparable to T-bills" ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/real-estate/nairobi-prime-office-rental-yield-stuck-on-higher-supply-5589442)). Nairobi land rose only 1.3% year on year ([Cytonn](https://www.cytonnreport.com/research/kenyas-real-estate-5)), and **7 of 14 satellite towns posted price falls** in Q2 2026 ([Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/list-of-hass-property/)). Gross total returns of 8–11% before vacancy, maintenance and tax sit below a 12.5% tax-free bond. Debt at 14.4% makes leverage negative. Foreigners can also hold only 99-year leaseholds ([US State Department](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/kenya)).

The exception is development for sale into an institutional exit, where the return is a margin, not a yield. Grade A logistics demand exceeds supply by about **3:1** ([Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/knight-frank-says-nairobi-lacks-adequate-supply-of-quality-logistics-space)), and industrial yields average about 9.5% ([Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/africa-industrial-property-knight-frank-report)). Purpose-built student housing has a proven buyer in its own sponsor: Acorn's REITs hold **KSh 29.3bn of assets** ([Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/acorn-student-housing-reits-post-ksh152b-profit-as-assets-grow-to-ksh293b-n379975)). The I-REIT grew total assets 21.7% in H1 2026 by acquiring stabilised Karen hostels ([Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/acorn-student-housing-i-reit-net-profit-rises-to-sh285-2mn/)). Acorn has CMA approval for a KSh 2.2bn build-to-rent D-REIT ([Serrari](https://serrarigroup.com/acorns-student-housing-reits-draw-renewed-investor-interest-as-i-reit-closes-at-ksh-23-24-amid-kenyas-expanding-property-fund-market/)). Here is an illustration with my own assumptions. Build at a 13% stabilised yield on cost and sell at a 9.5% cap rate, with 60% debt at 14.4% over a 2.5-year build. Equity earns about 22% a year, just short of the 25–30% hurdle. It clears only at a yield on cost near 14%, which gives about 30%. Every 100bp of exit-cap expansion removes about 13 points of development margin. At a 12% yield on cost, the equity IRR falls to about 14%. The Affordable Housing Programme survived its Court of Appeal challenge in September 2026 ([Pulse](https://www.pulse.co.ke/story/affordable-housing-levy-survives-court-challenge-inside-kenyas-15percent-housing-contribution-2026092518250405583)). But only **52.8% of first-year levy collections reached housing projects** ([Cytonn](https://www.cytonn.com/topicals/a-review-of)). Contractors are therefore taking SHA-style payer risk.

Financial services offer the best return on capital outside lending, because growth compounds on other people's money. Collective-investment-scheme AUM rose from **KSh 756.3bn to KSh 851.7bn in a single quarter** to March 2026 ([MMFs Kenya](https://mmfskenya.com/kenya-money-market-fund-industry-size-and-growth)). Part of that rise reflects new funds entering the reporting, so organic growth is lower. CMA is now seeking risk ratings for 320 money-market funds ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/cma-seeks-risk-ratings-of-320-money-market-funds-5630908)), which raises compliance costs and favours scale. Insurance premiums grew **16.5% to Sh466.6bn** in 2025. Medical premiums rose 22.3%, and penetration reached only 2.63% ([Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/business/396230/kenyas-insurance-penetration-hits-decade-high-at-263-per-cent-premiums-rise-to-sh46658-billion)). SACCO net income rose 25% ([Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/saccos-deposits-rise-11-12pc-to-sh832-74bn-as-assets-hit-sh1-21tn/)). Because SACCOs are member-owned, the investable angles are offtake mortgages, core-banking technology and credit insurance sold to SACCOs, not equity in them. Fee levels and broker commissions were not verified. Distribution, through digital and diaspora channels, decides who wins against Sanlam and Standard Investment Bank.

Several consumer-infrastructure niches clear the bar on volume growth, though none on sourced margins. **LPG consumption grew 14.7% to 475,943 t** in the year to June 2026 ([The Online Kenyan](https://www.theonlinekenyan.com/daily/2026-09-29/lpg-consumption-in-kenya-up-14-7pc-to-475-943-tonnes-on-zero-rating)). C&I solar has vendor-claimed paybacks of 2–6 years ([Ki Energy Tech](https://kienergytech.com/commercial-solar-installation-in-kenya-full-cost-roi-guide-2026/)), but backdated export rules and a 1 MW cap restrict it to self-consumption designs with storage. Specialist outpatient care (dialysis, oncology, imaging) shows strong demand. Cancer Care Kenya's revenue is nearing KSh 1bn ([Dawan Africa](https://www.dawan.africa/news/cancer-care-kenya-revenue-nears-ksh1-billion-as-demand-for-oncology-services-grows)), Equity Bank has financed about Sh11.5bn of medical equipment ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/hospital-equipment-upgrades-open-business-for-banks-5564762)), and Apollo plans a US$200m Eldoret hospital ([Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/health/kenya-races-to-become-a-specialist-healthcare-hub-5501288)). Apollo's plan is also a warning that secondary cities will not stay uncontested. The defensible payer mix is cash and private insurance, with SHA as upside. TVET enrolment is rising under a KSh 58.5bn budget ([Education News](https://educationnews.co.ke/?p=517213)), and pharmacy is consolidating: CFAO bought Goodlife ([Impact Investor](https://impact-investor.com/?p=29110)), and Equity Afya targets 1,000 franchised outlets ([Kenya Times](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/equity-afya-opens-new-pharmacy-in-nairobi-cbd-as-it-targets-1000-outlets/)). Both are plausible roll-up targets. Kenyan acquisition multiples are a genuine gap. The closest benchmarks are US: about **2.7x SDE** for Main Street deals ([Sundance FG/BizBuySell](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)) and about **4x EBITDA** for US$2–5m deals ([SumCap](https://sumcapadvisors.com/q1-2026-market-pulse-lower-middle-market-ma-trends/)). With a higher country discount and 14–15% debt, a Kenyan buy-and-build needs entry below those multiples and low leverage to clear 25–30%.

Several popular theses fail the hurdle on the same evidence. Satellite-town land banking, new malls and buy-to-let residential cannot beat a tax-free 12.5% sovereign. B2B inventory e-commerce repeats the Twiga pattern of owning trucks and stock at single-digit margins. Consumer payday lending competes in a field of 281 licensed lenders and is the obvious target of regulatory backlash. Clean cooking without a Letter of Authorisation depends on carbon revenue the state can deny, as KOKO showed. A greenfield hyperscale data centre cannot get a power guarantee. Sugar-mill leases face explicit threats of reversal after 2027. Greenfield EPZ apparel cannot pay back before the 2028 AGOA cliff. General hospitals dependent on SHA carry the claims backlog. Standalone remittances are a slow-growth corridor: inflows fell 1.3% over the 12 months to August 2026 ([Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/09/18/kenya-remittance-inflows-august-2026-record/)). VASP stablecoin rails, authorised carbon development, Swahili voice AI and mid-size colocation with a captive renewable PPA belong in an option sleeve. That means many small positions sized on the assumption that most will return nothing, consistent with the skewness evidence.

Portfolio construction follows from the macro risks. **Currency matching** is the first rule. M-KOPA's margin compression shows what USD hardware with KES revenue does. KES-funded receivables books (the Sun King model) and USD-earning exporters hedge each other. **Exposure to the state should be capped**, given KES 466bn of pending bills and the SHA backlog. **Capital should be staged around August 2027.** G4S ranks political instability as Kenya's top 2026 threat ([Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/kenya-faces-heightened-political-instability-civil-unrest-in-2026-report-n376124)), and the June 2027 Finance Bill is the pre-election protest flashpoint. Commitments to long-payback physical assets are better made after the result. Credit books and acquisitions can start now. Co-investors are plentiful in debt and scarce in equity. DFIs supplied **64% of African private-capital fund commitments in 2025** ([AVCA](https://www.avca.africa/data-intelligence/research-publications/2025-african-private-capital-activity-report/)), and Kenyan startup funding is more than 90% debt. That is the structural reason a well-capitalised equity investor can negotiate good entry terms.

## Conclusion

Kenya's high risk-free rate does most of the screening. When the sovereign pays 12.5% tax-free and absorbs 70% of revenue in debt service, private credit is rationed. The country's most profitable private "businesses" turn out to be credit spreads in other forms: PAYGo, e-mobility leases, invoice finance and deposit franchises. The surviving export processors are, in effect, short-dated options on trade and policy windows that cluster at the same horizon. AGOA expires in December 2028, the China Early Harvest deal in April 2028, and SEZ incentives are capped at ten years ([US State Department](https://www.state.gov/reports/2026-investment-climate-statements/kenya)). An investor who underwrites only to 2028 cash flows, and treats anything beyond as upside, is pricing Kenya's policy volatility rather than assuming it away.

The deeper implication is about evidence, not sectors. Kenya's published record consists of fundraising announcements and survivor profiles. It contains almost no disclosed loss rates, margins per tonne or acquisition multiples, so the expected returns of every opportunity here are identified only up to a large survival and selection haircut. The investor-founder's real edges are equity in an equity-starved market and the patience to hold through an election. Both pay off only if the first capital spent buys information: proprietary loan-performance data, measured processing yields, verified offtake prices. Producing that information before committing large sums is the highest-return investment available, and it is also what separates a disciplined frontier investor from the people whose money became the KES 93bn graveyard.
